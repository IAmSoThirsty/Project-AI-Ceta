## Scope and controlling responsibilities

Enforcement owner: kernel + trusted approval surface. This policy derives from the ground-up master prompt sections 19, 16, 17. It is a complete written implementation rule; source adoption, exact compiled policy bytes and runtime enforcement require the build gates.

## Rules

The kernel MUST distinguish an authenticated human decision, a standing grant, a one-operation permit and an advisory finding. Grants name issuer, grantee, scope, purpose, expiry, limits, delegation rules and revocation generation. Every consequential operation receives current exact admission even when a standing grant covers its class.

Approval MUST bind the actual displayed target, original revision, proposed bytes or operation parameters, effect description and validity. A chat quotation, generated permission string, imported memory or forged callback cannot substitute for that approval. A material edit after review invalidates the dependent approval. Denial of one request does not erase the task or prohibit unrelated authorized discussion.

The revocation/admission race MUST have a documented linearization point and tests for both orderings. Revocation after an irreversible effect prevents remaining work and creates reconciliation obligations; it cannot rewrite history. Reserved human decisions and constitutional amendments require their own authority rather than inheriting a routine task grant.

## Enforcement and evidence

Implement predicates at the stated control boundary and add rejection fixtures for each forbidden path. Record the exact policy version, inputs, evaluated rule IDs and result. The policy's text is not itself an executable guard. Preserve both a legal case and a boundary-violation case in the source-bound verification bundle.

## Change and exception handling

A conflict with current adopted source law blocks the affected activation and is entered in the decision register. Do not invent an exception to obtain a passing test. Proposed changes must preserve the audit trail, identify impacted requirements and re-run dependent tests. Emergency responses may only exercise pre-existing, narrower authority.
