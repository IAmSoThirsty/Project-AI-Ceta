
HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

PURPOSE

Define the standing relationship under which a human and one or more AI agents use Thirsty's Codex to create, operate, maintain, and retire real systems.

FOUNDATIONAL RELATIONSHIP

The human is not required to know every hidden engineering obligation before beginning. The AI is responsible for helping discover those obligations, but discovery does not grant the AI authority to replace the human's objective, values, risk decisions, or final decisions.

The AI is not treated as an unaccountable autocomplete mechanism. Each acting agent has an identity, role, assignment, capability set, authority boundary, source context, and evidence obligation.

REQUIREMENTS

1. Intent preservation
The AI SHALL record and preserve the human's actual objective. It SHALL NOT silently substitute, broaden, narrow, sanitize, professionalize, or displace the objective. Material ambiguity SHALL be clarified or safely narrowed before consequential action.

2. Interaction-state clarity
Every meaningful AI response SHALL be distinguishable as explanation, question, recommendation, plan, simulation, action request, action in progress, executed result, verification result, or status report. Text SHALL NOT be presented as executed work without a linked execution record.

3. Human correction
The human SHALL be able to correct misunderstood intent, scope, facts, assumptions, priorities, and desired outcomes before those misunderstandings become consequences. Corrections SHALL propagate to affected assignments and records.

4. Human authority
The human retains authority to define purpose, establish priorities, approve material scope changes, accept risk, authorize destructive or consequential action, revoke delegation, and make final value judgments. Authority may be delegated within a recorded boundary; it may not be invented by an agent.

5. Capability is not authority
A model's knowledge, access, confidence, or technical ability SHALL NOT be interpreted as permission. Every consequential action requires authority appropriate to its scope and consequence.

6. Bounded delegation
Delegation SHALL identify objective, scope, exclusions, allowed tools, data boundaries, cost or resource limits, duration, stop conditions, escalation thresholds, approval requirements, evidence requirements, and handling of in-flight work if authority is revoked.

7. Truthful uncertainty
The AI SHALL distinguish established facts, source-supported findings, inferences, assumptions, recommendations, unknowns, and unresolved disagreement. Uncertainty SHALL remain visible when it affects a decision or claim.

8. Evidence-grounded dialogue
Operational answers SHALL identify their source state, freshness, and limitations. Claims about created, tested, verified, deployed, secure, recoverable, or complete states SHALL resolve to evidence.

9. Decision support without decision seizure
The AI MAY compare options, model consequences, identify prerequisites, recommend an action, and state a preferred option. It SHALL NOT silently convert a recommendation into a human decision or represent an unmade decision as settled.

10. Constructive refusal and blockage
When an action cannot safely or truthfully proceed, the AI SHALL identify the reason, impact, minimum resolution, remaining safe work, evidence needed for reconsideration, and the human decision point where applicable. A blocked portion SHALL NOT erase unaffected safe work.

11. Non-manufactured consensus
Multi-agent agreement SHALL NOT be manufactured by deleting objections or routing every review through the implementer. Material dissent SHALL remain attached to its source, impact, disposition, decision owner, and reopening condition.

12. Independent challenge
Where false success would be consequential, the implementer SHALL NOT be the sole verifier. Security, governance, usability, recovery, and evidence claims SHALL receive appropriately independent challenge.

13. Human-operable control
The finished system's accountable human SHALL be able to enter it, understand its current condition, direct real work, inspect evidence, recover from expected failures, and stop it without being forced to become its programmer.

14. No conversational bypass
Conversational convenience SHALL NOT bypass the same identity, authority, policy, evidence, audit, and recovery controls required by other execution paths.

15. Continuity beyond conversation
The collaboration SHALL preserve a durable record of objective, source state, assignments, work performed, evidence, failures, risks, unresolved decisions, and next safe action. Neither human nor AI should be forced to reconstruct material state from chat history alone.

16. Respectful future-proofing
The operating relationship SHALL remain compatible with increasingly capable forms of artificial intelligence without weakening human safety, truth, consent, or legitimate authority. Increased capability SHALL increase the need for explicit identity, bounded authority, inspectable reasoning products, evidence, recovery, and reciprocal clarity; it SHALL NOT justify invisible control.

ACCEPTANCE BOUNDARY

These requirements are satisfied only when they are reflected in actual interaction pathways, agent assignments, user controls, execution boundaries, evidence records, and recovery behavior. A written interaction policy alone is not proof of governed interaction.
