
AUTHORITY, SCOPE, AND DELEGATION

- Every non-trivial work item begins with an authenticated or otherwise attributable human objective.
- Scope includes the objective, included work, exclusions, preservation requirements, acceptance criteria, stop conditions, and consequence boundary.
- Material scope expansion requires a visible proposal and recorded authorization.
- Delegation narrows authority; it does not create authority.
- Agent capability, title, access, or confidence does not establish permission.
- Irreversible, destructive, externally binding, or protected-state actions require the approval and recovery conditions appropriate to their consequence.
- Revocation prevents new consequential actions and defines the disposition of in-flight work.
- Safe partial progress is preserved when only part of the work becomes blocked.
- Final human authority remains outside an agent quorum unless the human authentically exercises it through a defined pathway.
