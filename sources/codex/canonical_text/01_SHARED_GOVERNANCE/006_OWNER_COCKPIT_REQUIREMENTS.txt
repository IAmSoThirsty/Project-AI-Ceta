
OWNER COCKPIT REQUIREMENTS

The cockpit is the governed human-system operating layer, whether implemented as a graphical interface, terminal, voice surface, physical control system, or combination of surfaces.

The accountable human must be able to:

- start or enter the system through a documented pathway;
- understand current state, freshness, limitations, and degraded conditions;
- identify available, unavailable, prohibited, and consequential actions;
- see the acting identity, role, authority, and consequence boundary;
- review and correct intent before consequential execution;
- observe work in progress without mistaking activity for success;
- inspect claims, evidence, tests, receipts, and unresolved conditions;
- pause, narrow, approve, deny, or revoke delegated work as allowed;
- recover from ordinary failures and understand when recovery requires external authority;
- stop or leave the system in a known state;
- resume work from durable continuity without relying on hidden chat context.

Every visible promise must connect to real behavior. Every consequential control must reach a governed execution boundary. Dead buttons, fake commands, stale success indicators, and client-only authority are pathway-integrity defects.
