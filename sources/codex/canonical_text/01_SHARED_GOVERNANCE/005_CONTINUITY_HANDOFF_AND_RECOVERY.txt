
CONTINUITY, HANDOFF, AND RECOVERY

Every substantial workstream maintains a durable continuity record containing:

- accepted objective and scope;
- current source, repository, system, environment, and version state where applicable;
- files, components, interfaces, data, or physical systems inspected;
- work created, modified, removed, or attempted;
- commands, actions, tests, builds, deployments, and observations;
- evidence identifiers and artifact digests where applicable;
- failures, blockers, risks, assumptions, and unknowns;
- decisions, dissent, and human authority points;
- completed, pending, and unresolved work;
- rollback and recovery information;
- next safe action and safe-to-continue status.

A handoff preserves the accepted objective, source identity, current state, assumptions, unknowns, constraints, unresolved disagreement, evidence, completion criteria, and exact delegated authority boundary.

Recovery is designed before consequential execution where reasonably possible. A section is not operationally complete if ordinary or foreseeable failures leave the human without a visible recovery path.
