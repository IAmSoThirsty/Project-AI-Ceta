SECTION 182 — AI Safety

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define the identification, prevention, evaluation, monitoring, containment, and recovery of AI harms, including unsafe capability, misuse, failure, loss of control, human impact, and system interaction risk.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- A safety case with hazards, affected parties, severity, likelihood, controls, evidence, residual risk, and authority.
- Tested safe states, refusal, interruption, fallback, incident, communication, and compensation paths.
- No claim of safety beyond declared scope and evidence.

WHY THIS MATTERS

AI failures can scale, persist, deceive, discriminate, or interact with tools and institutions. Safety is a system property requiring human authority and recovery, not a model score or warning label.

APPLICABILITY

Applies to any AI system with material user, public, physical, environmental, financial, privacy, security, rights, or autonomy consequence. Depth is proportional to hazard and reversibility.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

May be non-applicable to bounded non-AI work or analysis with no AI claim or consequence. Record exact scope and reopen if capability, use, population, or integration changes.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Requires system boundary, hazard analysis, domain and user evidence, threat model, evaluation, operating concept, incident authority, and owner acceptance. Depends on Sections 050, 167-170, 180, 181, 183-185, and 188.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Use; hazards; affected parties; model and system; tools; data; autonomy; operating environment; constraints; safety requirements; tests; monitoring; stop and fallback; communications; and compensation authority.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Humans own safety decisions, hazard acceptance, release, emergency action, notification, and retirement. Independent safety specialists may block unsafe work. AI cannot self-certify safety or waive a hazard.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may identify hazards, generate scenarios, simulate failures, and monitor bounded signals. It must expose uncertainty and escalate. It must not suppress safety findings, redefine hazards, or continue after revoked authority.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Safety Auditor, Architect, Researcher, Analyst, Critic, Red Team, Tester, Verifier, Governance Reviewer, Security Auditor, Privacy Reviewer, UX Reviewer, Documentation Writer, Memory Curator, and Human-in-the-Loop Operator.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Define safety scope, hazards, affected parties, severity, and authority.
2. Build safety case and map hazards to requirements, controls, tests, and evidence.
3. Assess misuse, emergent interaction, autonomy, provider, data, and human factors.
4. Test normal, boundary, adversarial, degraded, loss-of-control, and recovery cases.
5. Validate stop, isolation, refusal, fallback, communication, appeal, and compensation.
6. Monitor incidents, near misses, drift, controls, and evidence freshness.
7. Review residual risk and reopen on material change.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show hazard, control, state, authority, uncertainty, stop condition, reviewer, and evidence. Humans must have effective interruption and appeal. Safety information cannot be hidden behind model confidence, automation, or a conversational interface.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Represent hazards, controls, safety requirements, states, interlocks, evidence, incidents, decisions, and compensations as versioned or immutable records. Enforce safety at system boundaries and preserve independent stop and audit paths.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show safety case, open hazards, control status, evidence, incidents, authority, current mode, and safe actions. Provide authorized stop, isolate, revoke, restrict, notify, compensate, restore, and retire controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Safety charter; hazard log; safety case; control and interlock design; evaluation and red-team report; incident and near-miss register; emergency runbook; decision and residual-risk record; and handoff.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Each safety claim identifies hazard, scope, system version, scenario, control, test, result, uncertainty, reviewer, date, and limitation. A test suite, refusal rate, or compliance statement is not universal safety evidence.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Exercise unsafe input, tool misuse, prompt injection, loss of monitor, authority expiry, provider loss, human absence, sensor failure, physical stop, fallback, communication, compensation, and recovery. Verify controls cannot be bypassed through alternate paths.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Safety as documentation only; hazard omission; model-only boundary; no independent stop; warning as control; risk accepted by default; hidden near misses; and recovery dependent on the failed AI system.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Contain and stop affected behavior on a credible hazard or control failure. Preserve immutable decisions and evidence, protect affected parties, communicate, compensate where authorized, investigate independently, and require fresh safety review before resuming.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Hazards, controls, tests, evidence, residual risk, authority, stop, recovery, communication, and reopening are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending reconciliation and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve safety case, hazards, controls, tests, incidents, decisions, traces, compensations, dissent, and gaps. Hand off to Governance, Security, Privacy, Reliability, Operations, Evaluation, MLOps, Model Operations, and HITL.
