SECTION 074 — Robotics Engineering

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define engineering of robots and autonomous physical agents that perceive, plan, decide, move, manipulate, communicate, and interact with people under bounded authority and safe operating conditions.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

 - A scoped robot, environment, mission, human, sensor, actuator, autonomy, safety, security, and lifecycle model.
 - Traceable behavior, motion, task, interaction, update, recovery, verification, field, and acceptance evidence.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Robots couple uncertain perception and planning to physical action near people and assets. A successful demonstration can hide collision, localization, battery, communication, manipulation, recovery, or authority failures.

APPLICABILITY

Applies to mobile, stationary, collaborative, industrial, service, medical, aerial, marine, space, and simulated robots with material autonomy or physical effect.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Concept art or simulation with no claim about physical embodiment, autonomy, safety, task success, or deployment; or an unchanged approved robot baseline with impact recorded. Record reopening conditions.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Robot and environment models, mission and task requirements, hardware, control, perception, maps, safety zones, human factors, communications, power, security, test facilities, field rules, and authority.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Robot and environment models, mission and task requirements, hardware, control, perception, maps, safety zones, human factors, communications, power, security, test facilities, field rules, and authority.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Humans approve mission, autonomy, workspace, safety envelope, interaction, data, emergency stop, remote control, fleet release, acceptable risk, and field acceptance. They retain override, isolation, recovery, recall, and retirement authority.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may generate plans, analyze logs, detect obstacles or drift, and propose tests. It must reveal uncertainty and authority boundaries; it must not select unapproved missions, enter unsafe space, alter limits, or command physical action without explicit delegation.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Architect, Analyst, Implementer, Tester, Verifier, Observer, Safety Auditor, Security Auditor, Governance Reviewer, User Experience Reviewer, Critic, Red Team, Planner, Coordinator, Documentation Writer, Memory Curator, Optimizer, and Decision Arbiter Advisory.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Define mission, environment, embodiment, task, human interaction, autonomy modes, sensors, actuators, maps, localization, planning, control, communication, power, safety, security, diagnostics, update, and recovery. Test collision, loss, ambiguity, adversarial conditions, interruption, and recovery.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show robot identity, mission, location, map freshness, autonomy, operator authority, sensor confidence, plan, physical effect, battery, communications, alarms, and stop or teleoperation controls. AI plans remain proposals until authorized and tested.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Separate mission, perception, localization, planning, control, safety monitor, human interface, and physical actuation. Define bounded autonomy, geofencing, collision avoidance, safe stop, degraded navigation, loss of link, power failure, audit, and recovery.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Expose fleet or robot identity, mode, workspace, task, plan, human authority, safety zones, telemetry, evidence, and controls to pause, stop, isolate, teleoperate, recover, recall, or retire.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Robot architecture and mission model; behavior and autonomy contracts; environment and safety model; perception, planning, control, interaction, update, security, recovery, test, field, incident, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify robot, hardware, software, model, map, environment, task, operator, time, sensors, configuration, method, result, uncertainty, and limitation. A demo, simulation, or average success rate proves only bounded conditions.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify safe stop, geofence, collision response, localization, obstacle and human detection, plan bounds, link loss, power loss, sensor faults, adversarial input, manual control, update rollback, recovery, and independent field reproduction.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Treating autonomy as authority; testing only open space; hiding recovery labor; unbounded exploration; stale maps; no independent safety monitor; remote control without latency limits; and measuring task completion while ignoring human or environmental harm.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Stop and isolate the robot, preserve physical and telemetry evidence, notify affected people, use approved teleoperation or recovery, and quarantine affected software or maps. Resume only after safety, security, authority, environment, and evidence review.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Mission, environment, embodiment, autonomy, human authority, safety, behavior, evidence, tests, update, recovery, field limits, open risks, and acceptance are explicit. Remain CONTENT-COMPLETE pending reconciliation, hostile review, and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve robot and mission identity, maps, models, firmware, logs, plans, decisions, incidents, field population, evidence, and next safe action. Hand off to Embedded, Control, Signal, IoT, AI, Safety, Security, Operations, Testing, and Retirement.
