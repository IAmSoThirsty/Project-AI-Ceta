SECTION 185 — Model Operations

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define operation of models in live and controlled environments, including serving, routing, versioning, monitoring, drift, incidents, access, cost, human review, fallback, and retirement.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- An operational model inventory and serving contract with owner, version, scope, authority, dependencies, and lifecycle.
- Reliable observability of model, service, data, policy, user, and outcome behavior.
- Safe stop, fallback, incident, compensation, and retirement paths.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Operational model behavior changes with traffic, data, providers, prompts, tools, and policy. Availability alone cannot show whether the model remains valid, safe, or authorized.

APPLICABILITY

Applies to deployed, hosted, embedded, batch, edge, shadow, or human-reviewed model use. Depth increases with live learning, external mutation, sensitive data, and high consequence.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

May be non-applicable to an unserved research artifact with no operational claim. Record artifact scope, owner, evidence date, and reopening trigger.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Requires model contract, deployment identity, evaluation, monitoring, access, incident, safety, privacy, security, operations, and governance authority. Depends on Sections 167, 180, 182-184, 301-318, and 188.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Model and endpoint; version; data; provider; prompts; tools; users; load; SLOs; quality and drift metrics; policy; capability; traces; incidents; fallback; compensation; and retirement plan.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Humans own operating mode, traffic, release, thresholds, incidents, communications, risk, and retirement. Operators may pause, route away, revoke, or compensate but cannot erase operational evidence.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may summarize telemetry, detect anomalies, and recommend actions. It must distinguish signal from inference and cannot suppress alerts, change routing, close incidents, or accept risk without authority.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Operations Engineer, Architect, Analyst, Observer, Security Auditor, Privacy Reviewer, Safety Auditor, Governance Reviewer, Tester, Verifier, Critic, Planner, Documentation Writer, Memory Curator, and HITL Operator.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Register model, endpoint, owner, scope, version, dependencies, and authority.
2. Define serving, routing, latency, capacity, quality, cost, and evidence contracts.
3. Instrument requests, outputs, errors, policy, tools, users, drift, and incidents.
4. Monitor quality, safety, privacy, security, availability, cost, and outcome signals.
5. Exercise provider loss, model mismatch, data shift, authority expiry, and fallback.
6. Manage incidents, communication, compensation, requalification, and retirement.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show model, provider, request, output, policy, authority, review, confidence limits, trace, and operational status. Require human approval for material routing or model changes and preserve manual service and stop paths.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Version endpoints, weights, runtime, prompts, policies, data, routing, thresholds, telemetry, and decisions. Enforce identity, capability, rate, data, and output controls at serving boundaries. Preserve append-only traces and compensation links.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show live model state, traffic, drift, quality, incidents, dependencies, policy, cost, authority, and evidence. Provide authorized pause, drain, route, revoke, fallback, quarantine, compensate, restore, and retire controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Model operations charter; inventory; serving and SLO contract; dashboards and alert rules; drift and incident runbook; provider and dependency map; access and policy records; decision, audit, trace, and retirement package.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Claims identify model, endpoint, version, environment, traffic, data, metric, observation period, method, actor, result, and limitation. Uptime, latency, or output samples do not prove model correctness or safety.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Test version mismatch, stale data, drift, provider loss, load, malformed input, unauthorized access, policy conflict, monitor failure, alert noise, stop, fallback, compensation, and retirement. Reproduce operational claims.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Uptime equals quality; unowned drift; alert suppression; silent routing; untracked provider change; model-output logs without privacy control; no manual path; and incident closure without evidence.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Restrict or stop affected model behavior on drift, incident, policy, security, privacy, safety, or evidence failure. Preserve state, notify owners, use qualified fallback, compensate impacts, re-evaluate, and resume only with authority.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Inventory, serving, authority, observability, quality, drift, incidents, recovery, compensation, evidence, and lifecycle are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending reconciliation and acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve endpoints, versions, telemetry, alerts, decisions, traces, incidents, compensations, dissent, and gaps. Hand off to MLOps, Operations, Evaluation, Safety, Security, Privacy, Governance, and HITL.
