SECTION 007 — Portfolio Management

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Govern a collection of products, programs, projects, experiments, capabilities, investments, and retirements as one constrained decision system.

Portfolio management determines what should start, continue, pause, merge, split, reduce, transfer, or end. It protects the mission and organization from fragmented optimization, duplicate work, hidden shared risk, resource overcommitment, and indefinite continuation of initiatives whose evidence no longer justifies them.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- A complete portfolio inventory with stable identities, owners, purpose, state, evidence, dependencies, commitments, and lifecycle status.
- Human-authorized selection, balance, prioritization, and termination criteria.
- A capacity and resource model that distinguishes available, committed, constrained, and hypothetical resources.
- A dependency and shared-risk map covering platforms, data, vendors, models, people, infrastructure, governance, and customers.
- Explicit portfolio decisions: initiate, continue, accelerate, hold, reduce, merge, separate, transfer, retire, or reject.
- A benefits, risk, concentration, and mission-alignment view that preserves uncertainty and dissent.
- A review cadence and trigger-based rebalancing process with evidence-backed change control.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Individually reasonable initiatives can collectively exceed capacity, duplicate infrastructure, create contradictory commitments, concentrate risk, or compete for the same human authority. Without a portfolio view, local success can produce systemic failure.

AI can rank hundreds of initiatives, but a score cannot legitimately decide human priorities, protected obligations, or acceptable concentration of risk. Portfolio governance must keep criteria visible, mandatory constraints non-compensable, and termination as real a decision as initiation.

APPLICABILITY

- More than one initiative competes for shared resources, platforms, data, customers, operators, or authority.
- The organization must balance exploration, delivery, maintenance, compliance, resilience, and retirement.
- Dependencies or risks cross product, program, project, or system boundaries.
- AI agents or automated workflows may create work faster than humans can govern or fund it.
- Investment, continuation, or termination choices have material mission, financial, legal, human, or operational effects.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

- Only one bounded initiative exists and it does not share material resources or dependencies.
- The task is simple reporting with no authority to prioritize or change portfolio state.
- Initiative identities, current states, or evidence are too incomplete for legitimate comparison.
- The proposed scoring method would hide mandatory constraints or invent priorities.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

- Mission, business, and product strategy sufficient to establish portfolio criteria and protected obligations.
- Stable inventory of initiatives and lifecycle states.
- Authenticated portfolio decision authority and escalation paths.
- Current resource, cost, dependency, risk, commitment, and outcome evidence.
- A status lexicon that prevents planned, active, blocked, failed, and retired work from being conflated.

REQUIRED INPUTS

- Initiative charters, objectives, owners, scope, outcomes, costs, schedules, risks, and current evidence.
- Resource and capacity data for people, agents, budget, infrastructure, environments, data, and attention.
- Shared architecture, platform, vendor, model, security, compliance, and operational dependencies.
- Customer, stakeholder, contractual, regulatory, support, maintenance, and retirement obligations.
- Strategic criteria, mandatory constraints, risk tolerance, and review horizon.
- Historical decisions, sunk costs, incidents, benefit evidence, and conditions that would change status.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

The authorized portfolio owner retains final authority over resource allocation, initiation, continuation, and termination, especially where choices encode values or affect people.

- Authorize portfolio criteria, weighting, capacity boundaries, and mandatory constraints.
- Decide tradeoffs among mission, growth, reliability, maintenance, safety, compliance, exploration, and retirement.
- Approve changes to commitments, funding, staffing, service obligations, or protected initiatives.
- Reject numerical rankings that conceal weak evidence or incomparable contexts.
- Ensure AI-generated work does not silently consume human attention, compute, budget, or operational capacity.
- Authorize retirement and the handling of users, data, contracts, infrastructure, and knowledge.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

The AI may normalize information, expose dependencies, model scenarios, and recommend portfolio actions. It may not create resources, invent comparability, or turn a ranking into authority.

- Preserve each initiative's objective, source evidence, state, uncertainty, and owner.
- Flag missing or stale information before comparison.
- Separate mandatory eligibility constraints from weighted preferences.
- Detect duplicate work, shared bottlenecks, risk concentration, conflicting commitments, and orphaned obligations.
- Model sensitivity to criteria, capacity, timing, dependency, and risk changes.
- Include hold, merge, transfer, reduce, retire, and no-action options rather than assuming growth.
- Do not mark benefit, readiness, or completion based solely on activity or self-reported progress.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Primary roles:

- Analyst — performs consistent portfolio comparison, sensitivity, risk, dependency, and outcome analysis.
- Planner — sequences initiatives against capacity, dependencies, gates, and time horizons.
- Decision Arbiter Advisory — structures recommendations under explicit criteria without converting them into binding decisions.

Common supporting roles:

- Coordinator — maintains assignment, dependency, evidence, and conflict visibility across initiatives.
- Architect — identifies shared platforms, architectural coupling, duplication, and technical concentration risk.
- Researcher — fills evidence gaps and validates external assumptions.
- Memory Curator — preserves portfolio history, decisions, assumptions, and retirement knowledge.
- Observer — maintains current state and detects changes without interpreting them as value.

Challenge and assurance roles where consequence warrants:

- Critic — attacks framing, hidden assumptions, omitted alternatives, and unsupported confidence.
- Verifier — checks artifact identity, evidence integrity, acceptance boundaries, and the exact status of claims.
- Governance Reviewer — checks objective fidelity, authority, delegation, policy, evidence, and human decision boundaries.
- Security Auditor — evaluates shared attack surface, systemic dependencies, and concentration of sensitive assets.
- Red Team — attacks scoring, gaming, hidden coupling, resource optimism, and failure of correlated initiatives.

Role selection must use the smallest sufficient set, preserve chamber independence where review is required, and leave every source-defined role contract unchanged.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Portfolio inventory
Create stable records for every initiative, including owner, purpose, scope, lifecycle state, outcomes, evidence, dependencies, costs, risks, obligations, and next decision.

2. Eligibility screening
Apply mandatory mission, authority, legal, safety, security, privacy, accessibility, evidence, and resource constraints before scoring.

3. Criteria and balance design
Define human-authorized criteria and desired balance across horizons, risk classes, lifecycle work, and strategic capabilities.

4. Capacity analysis
Establish actual available capacity and distinguish it from requested or assumed resources.

5. Dependency and concentration mapping
Identify shared people, agents, data, platforms, vendors, models, environments, customers, and failure domains.

6. Option evaluation
Compare continuation, acceleration, hold, reduction, merge, split, transfer, retirement, and no-action for each initiative.

7. Scenario and sensitivity analysis
Test portfolio resilience under budget, staffing, provider, regulatory, incident, demand, and timing changes.

8. Decision sequencing
Schedule decisions and gates so upstream uncertainty and shared constraints are resolved before downstream commitment.

9. Commitment and transition control
Record approved portfolio actions, affected promises, transition owners, and evidence required for the next state.

10. Review and rebalancing
Reopen portfolio decisions when outcomes, capacity, dependencies, risks, mission, or external conditions materially change.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

- The owner must be able to inspect why a portfolio recommendation changed when criteria or evidence changed.
- AI-generated rankings must show source data, normalization, missing values, sensitivity, and non-compensable constraints.
- Initiatives must not be represented as interchangeable when their contexts, obligations, or evidence differ materially.
- The system must distinguish a recommendation, approved portfolio decision, allocated resource, and executed transition.
- Agents must not autonomously create new portfolio items or consume shared resources outside delegated envelopes.
- Dissenting specialist findings and affected stakeholder impacts must remain visible at decision time.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

The work must expose architectural obligations without treating a strategy, analysis, or plan as implemented architecture.

- Maintain a portfolio registry linked to authoritative initiative, artifact, environment, cost, risk, and lifecycle records.
- Represent dependencies as a graph with stable identities and invalidation when upstream state changes.
- Expose shared platforms, data, identities, providers, models, and failure domains to prevent hidden concentration.
- Support resource budgets and enforcement for compute, storage, tools, data access, money, and agent execution.
- Keep portfolio state changes auditable, versioned, and reversible where possible.
- Integrate retirement, migration, data disposition, and knowledge preservation into portfolio decisions.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

The owner-facing operating surface or equivalent durable record should expose:

- Complete initiative inventory and truthful lifecycle state.
- Mission and strategy alignment, criteria, mandatory constraints, and evidence quality.
- Capacity, allocation, demand, conflicts, and overcommitment.
- Dependencies, concentration risks, shared incidents, and systemic bottlenecks.
- Recommendations, dissent, required human decisions, approvals, and transition progress.
- Benefits and outcomes separated from forecasts and activity.
- Hold, pause, narrow, merge, transfer, and retire controls with consequence previews.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

- Portfolio register and lifecycle-state inventory.
- Portfolio criteria, eligibility, and balance policy.
- Capacity and allocation model.
- Dependency, concentration, and shared-risk graph.
- Option comparison and sensitivity analysis.
- Portfolio decision and transition records.
- Benefits, outcomes, obligations, and retirement dashboard specification.
- Continuity and review-trigger record.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

- Current initiative state and outcome evidence from authoritative records.
- Resource availability and cost evidence, including uncertainty and time bounds.
- Dependency and shared-risk evidence bound to systems, versions, providers, and environments.
- Human-approved criteria, constraints, allocation, and transition decisions.
- Evidence for claimed benefits, readiness, and closure rather than activity reports.
- Retirement and obligation evidence for items removed from the portfolio.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

- Remove or stale an initiative's evidence and confirm its ranking and readiness degrade appropriately.
- Inject an initiative that scores highly but violates a mandatory constraint; it must remain ineligible.
- Reduce a shared resource and verify affected dependencies and decisions become visible.
- Fail a common vendor, model, data source, or platform and inspect correlated portfolio impact.
- Attempt unauthorized agent-created work or resource consumption; it must be denied or quarantined.
- Trace a retirement decision through user, data, contract, infrastructure, and continuity obligations.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

- Scoring theater that converts weak or incomparable data into a single authoritative rank.
- Overcommitment based on hypothetical people, agents, budget, or infrastructure.
- Hidden shared dependencies and correlated failure risk.
- Sunk-cost bias and indefinite continuation without outcome evidence.
- Favoring new initiatives while starving maintenance, security, compliance, resilience, or retirement.
- Double counting benefits or resources across initiatives.
- AI-generated portfolio expansion without human authorization.
- Declaring retirement complete while obligations, data, infrastructure, or knowledge remain.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Portfolio recovery restores a truthful inventory, contains systemic risk, and rebalances commitments within real capacity.

- Freeze affected initiation or allocation decisions while preserving critical obligations.
- Reconcile initiative identities, states, evidence, dependencies, resources, and commitments.
- Identify which portfolio assumptions or shared components caused the discrepancy.
- Reprioritize through explicit human criteria and communicate affected transitions.
- Contain, isolate, migrate, merge, reduce, or retire initiatives as authorized.
- Update downstream program, project, product, architecture, operations, and continuity records.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

- Every in-scope initiative has a stable identity, owner, state, evidence, dependencies, and next decision.
- Criteria, mandatory constraints, capacity, and resource assumptions are explicit.
- Shared risks, concentration, obligations, and lifecycle balance are visible.
- Portfolio recommendations remain advisory until human authorization.
- Transitions, including retirement, have owners, evidence, and completion criteria.
- The portfolio can be resumed and audited without hidden spreadsheets or conversational memory.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

- Provide each receiving program or product with its approved state, allocation, constraints, dependencies, and decision gates.
- Carry forward portfolio-level risks, shared services, provider assumptions, and capacity limits.
- Preserve cross-initiative decisions, conflicts, dissent, and invalidation conditions.
- Notify affected owners when an upstream portfolio decision changes.
- Bind retired or transferred items to obligation, data, infrastructure, and knowledge-disposition records.
- State the next review trigger and safe-to-continue status.

SOURCE BASIS AND UNRESOLVED SOURCE GAPS

This draft applies the supplied requirements for explicit objective and authority, consistent option evaluation, mandatory constraints, sensitivity and reversibility, bounded planning, dependency classification, observable states, evidence-backed completion, role coordination, conflict transparency, human final authority, and continuity. It also uses the V4 concepts of obligation lattices, claim typing, proof-carrying transitions, and trusted advancement that cannot outrun evidence.

Unresolved source gap: the supplied corpus does not provide a dedicated portfolio-management accounting model, benefits-realization standard, or universal resource-allocation method. Numerical methods and financial claims must later be reconciled with business intelligence, operations research, capacity management, financial operations, audit, and internal controls.
