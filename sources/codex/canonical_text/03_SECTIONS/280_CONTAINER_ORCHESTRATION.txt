SECTION 280 — Container Orchestration

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Schedule, connect, secure, scale, observe, update, and recover container workloads across nodes, services, tenants, and failure domains.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

An orchestration model with cluster, control plane, nodes, workloads, identity, network, storage, scheduling, health, policy, upgrades, capacity, and recovery ownership.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Orchestration concentrates control over many workloads. A control-plane compromise, bad policy, node failure, rollout, or scheduling assumption can affect broad service and data scope.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when a control plane schedules or manages containerized workloads across nodes or environments.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability when no orchestrator, cluster, workload scheduling, policy, or control-plane behavior changes and an approved exact baseline covers it.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Use container, platform, network, identity, storage, configuration, security, capacity, resilience, release, and operations baselines.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Cluster, nodes, workloads, policies, identities, images, network, storage, scheduling, health, capacity, upgrades, telemetry, and recovery.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Cluster and workload owners approve access, policies, placement, upgrade, scale, disruption, recovery, and retirement. Humans retain emergency control over the control plane.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may inspect authorized cluster state and manifests, identify scheduling or resource issues, and draft policies and runbooks. It must not grant cluster access, change workloads, drain nodes, or delete resources without authority.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Orchestration Engineer, Container Engineer, Platform Engineer, Security Auditor, Network Engineer, Storage Administrator, Tester, Verifier, and Operations Engineer.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Define cluster and tenancy; secure control plane; manage nodes and workload policy; schedule; observe; scale; roll out; upgrade; test disruption; recover; and retire.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show cluster, namespace or tenant, workload, identity, policy, node, action, evidence, and limitation. Require human approval for privileged, disruptive, production, and destructive actions.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Use control-plane isolation, least privilege, network policy, resource quotas, disruption budgets, health checks, storage recovery, staged upgrades, node diversity, and audit.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show cluster health, workload, node, policy, capacity, rollout, incidents, evidence freshness, owner, and authorized cordon, drain, scale, pause, rollback, restore, and retire controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Cluster charter; control-plane and node model; workload and policy; capacity and upgrade plan; observability, disruption, recovery, decision, evidence, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify cluster, node, workload, image, policy, identity, version, method, time, result, and limitation. Control-plane health proves neither application outcome nor data integrity.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify access, isolation, scheduling, resource, network, storage, health, rollout, upgrade, node loss, control-plane loss, backup, restore, and recovery.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Cluster admin everywhere, namespace isolation assumed, unbounded resources, unsafe drain, health check theater, control-plane single point, and upgrade without rollback.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Restrict control plane, preserve state, isolate workloads, restore or fail over, repair nodes, and reassess data and service impact. Reopen on cluster, policy, workload, provider, or version change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Cluster, nodes, workloads, policy, identity, capacity, updates, evidence, recovery, owners, limits, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending review and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve cluster state, manifests, policies, versions, incidents, upgrades, decisions, evidence, exceptions, and open gaps. Hand off to Containers, Platform, Network, Storage, Security, Release, and Operations.
