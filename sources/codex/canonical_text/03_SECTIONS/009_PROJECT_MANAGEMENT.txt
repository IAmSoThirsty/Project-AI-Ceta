SECTION 009 — Project Management

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Convert an authorized, bounded objective into a controlled temporary endeavor that produces defined deliverables and verified outcomes under explicit scope, resources, dependencies, risks, quality, and acceptance conditions.

Project management in Thirsty's Codex is not status theater or calendar maintenance. It creates the operational contract by which work is chartered, decomposed, sequenced, changed, observed, accepted, closed, and handed off without inventing resources, deadlines, authority, or completion.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- A project charter defining objective, scope, exclusions, sponsor, project owner, authority, deliverables, and acceptance.
- A current-state baseline and target state linked to requirements and evidence.
- A dependency-aware work breakdown with bounded tasks, owners or roles, inputs, outputs, tools, permissions, and completion evidence.
- A realistic sequence or schedule whose estimates disclose assumptions, confidence, capacity, review, rework, and external delay.
- Integrated risk, issue, decision, change, quality, communication, and stakeholder controls.
- Observable milestones representing achieved states rather than activity labels.
- A verified delivery, operational handoff, closure, and continuity package.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Projects fail when a plausible plan is treated as reality, activity is treated as outcome, or delivery is treated as acceptance. AI can make these failures look unusually polished by generating complete-looking schedules, task lists, and reports from incomplete inputs.

Governed project management keeps the project tied to actual authority, dependencies, available resources, evidence, and acceptance. It also preserves the distinction between what was planned, attempted, produced, tested, verified, accepted, and transferred.

APPLICABILITY

- A temporary, bounded body of work must create, change, migrate, validate, or retire a defined result.
- Work requires sequencing, resource coordination, dependencies, milestones, risk, and acceptance.
- Multiple human or AI roles contribute to one deliverable or outcome.
- A change has material cost, schedule, operational, security, safety, legal, or stakeholder consequences.
- A project requires an explicit start, controlled execution, and closure or transfer.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

- The work is a single trivial task that can be safely completed under an existing task envelope.
- The work is ongoing product or operational management rather than a temporary endeavor.
- The objective or sponsor authority is not established.
- Required inputs are so uncertain that research, analysis, feasibility, or requirements work must precede project commitment.
- The request is merely to generate a schedule without evidence of scope, resources, dependencies, or acceptance.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

- An approved objective from strategy, portfolio, program, product, remediation, migration, incident, or lifecycle governance.
- Authenticated sponsor, project owner, decision rights, escalation, and acceptance authority.
- Sufficient requirements, constraints, current-state evidence, and target-state definition.
- Known resources, tools, environments, dependencies, risks, prohibited actions, and budget or time boundaries.
- A continuity mechanism and canonical status lexicon.

REQUIRED INPUTS

- Objective, scope, exclusions, deliverables, acceptance criteria, and desired operating outcome.
- Current repository, system, process, product, data, infrastructure, or physical state.
- Requirements, architecture, design, quality, security, privacy, safety, accessibility, and compliance obligations.
- Available people, agents, tools, permissions, environments, budget, capacity, and deadlines.
- Dependencies, assumptions, unknowns, risks, stakeholders, commitments, and change constraints.
- Prior work, decisions, incidents, failed approaches, evidence, and continuity records.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

The sponsor and project owner establish legitimacy, priorities, resources, and acceptance. Project coordination does not create authority beyond that grant.

- Approve the charter, scope, exclusions, deliverables, constraints, and acceptance authority.
- Provide or authorize real resource commitments and resolve unavailable-resource conflicts.
- Decide material scope, schedule, cost, quality, risk, and stakeholder tradeoffs.
- Approve consequential changes, production actions, destructive operations, and risk acceptance.
- Correct project language that silently changes the objective or treats estimates as promises.
- Accept, reject, conditionally accept, pause, terminate, or transfer the project based on evidence.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

The AI may plan, coordinate, track, analyze, and report only from current evidence and delegated authority. It may not invent owners, personnel, dates, approvals, dependencies, status, or completion.

- Restate the approved objective and declared mode before decomposing work.
- Identify current state and hard dependencies before scheduling implementation.
- Create bounded tasks with explicit outputs, authority, evidence, and acceptance.
- Use estimates only when a basis exists; otherwise label ranges as preliminary.
- Track planned, ready, active, blocked, awaiting evidence, awaiting review, failed, completed, rejected, cancelled, and superseded states accurately.
- Continue unaffected safe work while isolating genuine blockers.
- Escalate material changes or value conflicts rather than silently replanning them as authorized.
- Do not mark work complete based on effort, file creation, agent report, passing subset, or process exit alone.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Primary roles:

- Planner — creates the work breakdown, dependencies, phases, gates, risks, schedule, resources, and completion evidence.
- Coordinator — assigns and synchronizes bounded work, tracks evidence, preserves conflict, and consolidates results.
- Observer — records current state, events, transitions, and gaps without interpreting activity as success.

Common supporting roles:

- Architect — provides structural decisions, constraints, interfaces, and implementation sequence where assigned.
- Implementer — performs authorized changes under exact scope and acceptance criteria.
- Tester — validates normal, boundary, failure, and recovery behavior.
- Documentation Writer — maintains project, operating, decision, and handoff records.
- Memory Curator — preserves durable state, provenance, decisions, and next safe action.

Challenge and assurance roles where consequence warrants:

- Critic — attacks framing, hidden assumptions, omitted alternatives, and unsupported confidence.
- Verifier — checks artifact identity, evidence integrity, acceptance boundaries, and the exact status of claims.
- Governance Reviewer — checks objective fidelity, authority, delegation, policy, evidence, and human decision boundaries.
- Security Auditor — examines project security, data, access, supply-chain, and deployment risk.
- Red Team — attacks planning assumptions, hidden dependencies, bypasses, and false-readiness claims.
- Decision Arbiter Advisory — structures material project choices under authorized criteria.

Role selection must use the smallest sufficient set, preserve chamber independence where review is required, and leave every source-defined role contract unchanged.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Project chartering
Define the objective, sponsor, project owner, scope, exclusions, deliverables, target state, constraints, acceptance, authority, and termination conditions.

2. Current-state baseline
Inspect actual artifacts, environments, work already completed, known failures, continuity, and external dependencies before planning remaining work.

3. Requirements and obligation mapping
Link deliverables to requirements, architecture, controls, evidence contracts, and acceptance criteria. Identify contradictions and missing decision owners.

4. Work breakdown
Decompose deliverables into bounded tasks with stable IDs, owners or roles, inputs, tools, authority, dependencies, outputs, verification, and recovery.

5. Dependency and sequence design
Classify dependencies and schedule only when hard prerequisites are satisfied or risk is explicitly accepted.

6. Resource and estimation planning
Use actual availability and include review, rework, testing, documentation, integration, deployment, and external delay.

7. Risk, issue, and contingency control
Maintain triggers, likelihood, impact, prevention, detection, response, owner, residual risk, and decision thresholds.

8. Quality and verification planning
Map requirements to tests, independent verification, hostile review, owner acceptance, and evidence retention.

9. Execution and status control
Track actual state from attributable evidence, not narrative confidence. Maintain one authoritative status per task and deliverable.

10. Change control
Record proposed changes, affected scope, tasks, dependencies, assumptions, resources, risks, acceptance, and required authorization.

11. Delivery and transition
Package exact artifacts, versions, configuration, documentation, training, support, monitoring, rollback, and operational ownership.

12. Acceptance and closure
Verify deliverables, resolve or transfer residual work, close commitments, preserve evidence, and record lessons and next action.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

- Every human or AI assignment must state objective, scope, authority, prohibited actions, inputs, expected output, evidence, review, and escalation.
- Status reports must separate direct observation, agent report, test result, verification, and human acceptance.
- The human must be able to inspect the basis of estimates, schedule changes, and completion claims.
- The AI must not pressure approval through fabricated urgency, sunk-cost framing, or optimistic certainty.
- Conversational instructions that change scope or consequence must become durable change requests before execution.
- Revocation, pause, or safe-halt commands must prevent new consequential work and define in-flight handling.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

The work must expose architectural obligations without treating a strategy, analysis, or plan as implemented architecture.

- Maintain stable project, task, artifact, environment, decision, evidence, and release identities.
- Represent dependencies, invalidation, acceptance, and state transitions explicitly.
- Separate planning data from authoritative runtime, repository, financial, or operational state.
- Support bounded credentials, branch or environment isolation, action receipts, rollback, and cleanup.
- Integrate requirements traceability, test results, security findings, documentation, and operational readiness.
- Preserve reproducibility and exact version linkage through delivery and handoff.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

The owner-facing operating surface or equivalent durable record should expose:

- Project objective, owner, sponsor, authority, scope, exclusions, target state, and acceptance.
- Deliverables, tasks, dependencies, owners, roles, resources, risks, and status evidence.
- Milestones as observable states with gates, results, and closure authority.
- Changes, decisions, assumptions, blockers, safe parallel work, and human approvals required.
- Artifacts, versions, tests, verification, incidents, rollback, and readiness.
- Budget and schedule ranges with basis and confidence rather than invented precision.
- Pause, narrow, revoke, terminate, restore, and close controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

- Project charter and responsibility model.
- Current-state baseline and target-state definition.
- Work breakdown, dependency graph, phase plan, and schedule basis.
- Resource, risk, issue, decision, change, and communication registers.
- Requirements, quality, test, verification, and acceptance plan.
- Delivery, deployment, rollback, transition, and support package.
- Final project report, evidence package, closure record, and continuity map.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

- Authenticated charter and human approvals.
- Current artifact, environment, dependency, resource, and configuration evidence.
- Task-level commands, diffs, logs, outputs, tests, reviews, receipts, and hashes.
- Schedule and estimate basis with assumptions and confidence.
- Acceptance evidence tied to exact deliverables and target environment.
- Evidence for unresolved, failed, blocked, unverified, transferred, and closed states.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

- Trace each deliverable to objective, requirements, tasks, tests, evidence, and acceptance authority.
- Attempt to schedule work before a hard dependency and verify it is blocked or explicitly authorized as risk.
- Remove a required artifact or test and confirm completion becomes impossible.
- Inject an unauthorized scope change through conversation and confirm execution does not proceed.
- Restart the project from the continuity package without relying on chat memory.
- Verify a non-programmer owner can see current state, limitations, and next safe action.
- Close the project only after residual work, operations, data, credentials, support, and obligations are resolved or transferred.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

- Project plan generated before objective, current state, authority, or requirements are known.
- Invented dates, resources, approvals, dependencies, budgets, or confidence.
- Activity milestones such as 'work ongoing' instead of observable state.
- Green status based on agent self-report, file existence, or partial tests.
- Uncontrolled scope expansion, gold plating, or unrelated cleanup.
- Hiding blockers, failed tests, dissent, or inherited problems as out of scope.
- Delivery without operational handoff, rollback, support, documentation, or acceptance.
- Closure that leaves active credentials, data, infrastructure, obligations, or unsafe state.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Project recovery reestablishes the last known truthful state, contains consequence, and replans from verified conditions.

- Pause affected work and preserve artifacts, logs, branches, environments, evidence, and in-flight state.
- Reconcile planned state with actual repository, system, resource, and dependency state.
- Identify the first failed requirement, assumption, dependency, authority, or test.
- Rollback or restore where required, then reverify the baseline.
- Replan scope, sequence, resources, gates, and schedule through explicit change authorization.
- Communicate impact and update program, product, stakeholders, operations, and continuity records.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

- The project objective, scope, authority, deliverables, owners, and acceptance are explicit.
- Tasks, dependencies, resources, risks, changes, and evidence are current and traceable.
- All required deliverables exist and have the declared test, verification, and acceptance state.
- Residual failures, unknowns, risks, and transferred obligations are visible.
- Operational handoff, rollback, support, documentation, and continuity are complete for the declared mode.
- The project is closed, transferred, cancelled, or blocked truthfully rather than merely inactive.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

- Provide exact deliverables, versions, environment, configuration, requirements, tests, and evidence.
- Transfer ownership, permissions, support, monitoring, recovery, maintenance, and open obligations.
- Preserve assumptions, decisions, changes, rejected alternatives, risks, incidents, and dissent.
- Identify what was not executed or verified and the evidence needed to resolve it.
- Update the receiving program, product, operations, maintenance, or retirement continuity record.
- State next safe action and safe-to-continue status.

SOURCE BASIS AND UNRESOLVED SOURCE GAPS

This draft directly applies the supplied Planner and Coordinator role contracts, including objective preservation, task definition, dependency classification, observable milestones, estimation discipline, risk, blockers, change control, validation gates, completion evidence, assignment contracts, conflict transparency, and human authority. It also applies V3's no-fake-success, current-problem, blocker, minimal-effective-change, testing, verification, continuity, and final-report rules; the Owner's Cockpit adds owner-operable status, action, evidence, recovery, release, and rollback; V4 adds obligation compilation and proof-carrying transition.

Unresolved source gap: the supplied corpus does not mandate a specific project methodology, scheduling technique, cost-control system, or contract model. Those choices must remain context-specific, declared, and later reconciled with finance, procurement, quality, release, operations, and audit sections.
