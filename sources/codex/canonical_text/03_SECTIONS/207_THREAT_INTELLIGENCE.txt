SECTION 207 — Threat Intelligence

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Collect, evaluate, contextualize, protect, and communicate information about threats, actors, techniques, campaigns, exposures, and defensive implications relevant to the declared system and decision scope.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A threat-intelligence process with source governance, collection requirements, confidence and provenance, relevance, analysis, dissemination, privacy, handling, action mapping, feedback, and explicit uncertainty.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Threat information can be stale, manipulated, duplicated, overgeneralized, or irrelevant to the target environment. Treating an indicator, headline, vendor claim, or model-generated narrative as verified intelligence can cause wasted effort, unsafe blocking, or false attribution.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when security decisions depend on external or internal information about threats, adversaries, techniques, vulnerabilities, campaigns, abuse, fraud, geopolitical or sector conditions, or emerging risk.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability only when no threat-informed decision, protected asset, intelligence source, or risk change is in scope and an approved exact baseline supports it.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Use security operations, threat modeling, vulnerability, privacy, legal, source-handling, incident, and governance baselines. Define consumers, decisions, source authority, handling restrictions, classification, and action owners.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Intelligence requirements, assets, sectors, threats, source inventory, indicators, reports, telemetry, vulnerabilities, incidents, confidence model, handling rules, consumers, decision thresholds, and unresolved questions.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Owners define intelligence requirements, source acceptance, handling, confidence, dissemination, collection limits, attribution posture, action thresholds, and risk acceptance. Humans approve sensitive collection, sharing, attribution, blocking, notification, and use in consequential decisions.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may retrieve authorized sources, normalize indicators, compare reports, identify contradictions, draft analytic questions, and map intelligence to declared assets and controls. It must preserve provenance, freshness, handling, confidence, source limitation, and uncertainty. It must not fabricate sources, expose restricted intelligence, infer attribution, or trigger blocking or response without delegated authority and human review.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Threat Intelligence Analyst, Researcher, Analyst, Security Auditor, Observer, Architect, Security Operations Analyst, Verifier, Critic, Red Team, Governance Reviewer, Documentation Writer, and Memory Curator.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Define intelligence requirements; inventory and qualify sources; collect within authority; preserve provenance; normalize and correlate; assess relevance, confidence, and contradiction; produce decision-ready analysis; disseminate by need and handling; map to controls; solicit feedback; expire stale intelligence; record decisions.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show source, date, collection method, handling, confidence, corroboration, contradiction, target relevance, and recommended use. Require human review for attribution, sensitive dissemination, blocking, incident declaration, or high-impact risk decisions. Keep intelligence separate from fact, assumption, and action.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Separate collection, source identity, storage, analysis, classification, dissemination, indicator use, audit, retention, and destruction. Bind indicators to context and expiry, prevent untrusted input from becoming executable control, and protect sources and affected people.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show intelligence requirement, source, freshness, confidence, relevance, handling, affected assets, contradictions, action state, owner, and evidence. Provide authorized accept, reject, expire, restrict, share, investigate, and reopen controls with rationale.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Intelligence requirements; source and handling register; collection and confidence model; analytic products; indicator and context catalog; action mapping; contradiction, decision, evidence, exception, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify source, publisher or origin, collection time, version, handling, method, corroboration, target, analyst, confidence, result, and limitation. A feed hit or generated summary proves only the source claim and declared context.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify source identity, freshness, provenance, handling, indicator context, expiry, contradiction, relevance, dissemination, access, and action mapping. Test poisoned or unavailable sources, conflicting reports, stale indicators, false positives, and unauthorized sharing.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Source laundering, false attribution, indicator without context, stale feed treated as current, vendor or model narrative treated as fact, uncontrolled collection, indiscriminate sharing, and intelligence used to bypass due process.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Quarantine questionable products or indicators, retract or annotate dissemination, preserve provenance, notify consumers, and reassess actions based on corrected or corroborated evidence. Reopen on source compromise, threat change, target change, or material contradiction.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Requirements, sources, handling, provenance, confidence, relevance, outputs, consumers, actions, evidence, uncertainty, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending independent verification and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve source and product identity, handling, decisions, indicators, expiry, contradictions, evidence, dissemination, incidents, and open gaps. Hand off to Threat Modeling, Security Operations, Vulnerability, Incident Response, Privacy, Legal, and Governance.
