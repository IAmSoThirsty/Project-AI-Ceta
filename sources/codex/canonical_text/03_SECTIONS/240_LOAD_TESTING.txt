SECTION 240 — Load Testing

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Validate system behavior under expected and planned concurrent workload, including throughput, latency, resource use, queues, dependencies, and user-visible outcome.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A load-test plan with workload model, ramp and duration, environment, data, service objectives, instrumentation, safety controls, results, bottlenecks, and capacity decision.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Expected load is not a single number. Arrival pattern, concurrency, data mix, cache state, dependency behavior, and duration determine whether a service remains useful or degrades.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when concurrent users, requests, jobs, devices, events, or transactions can affect capacity, latency, cost, or reliability.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability only when no concurrent workload or capacity claim changes and an approved exact baseline covers the scope.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Use workload, performance, architecture, capacity, cost, reliability, rate-limit, provider, data, and operations baselines. Establish authorization, isolation, and stop conditions.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Arrival and concurrency model, duration, data mix, users, dependencies, environment, limits, service objectives, metrics, baseline, and acceptance criteria.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Owners approve workload, environment, cost, production-like testing, stop conditions, and capacity decision. Operators protect shared services and decide emergency stop.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may draft workload models, scripts, analysis, and bottleneck hypotheses. It must not launch unapproved load, use real sensitive users, overload dependencies, or claim capacity from a synthetic mismatch.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Load Test Engineer, Performance Engineer, Tester, Architect, Analyst, Operations Engineer, Observer, Verifier, Quality Engineer, and Documentation Writer.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Define expected load and success; prepare isolated environment and data; validate generator; ramp and sustain; monitor service and dependencies; capture resource and user outcome; stop safely; analyze; tune; rerun; and record capacity and limitations.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show workload, generator, target, dependency, environment, rate, duration, metric, result, cost, and stop state. Require human approval before execution and tuning.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Provide rate limiting, backpressure, queue visibility, idempotency, autoscaling, resource isolation, dependency protection, synthetic identity, and cleanup.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show active load, rate, concurrency, duration, target, dependency health, metrics, cost, safety state, and authorized pause, stop, scale, rollback, accept, and reopen controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Load model; generator and data record; environment and authorization; telemetry; results; bottleneck and capacity analysis; decision, evidence, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify workload, rate, concurrency, duration, version, environment, data, dependency, metric, method, result, and limitation. One load profile proves only that profile.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify model realism, generator accuracy, safety, service objectives, queues, resource, dependency impact, autoscaling, degradation, recovery, cost, and repeatability.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Load against shared production without authority, generator bottleneck, unrealistic traffic, ignored dependency impact, throughput-only success, and load test that becomes an outage.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Stop load, protect dependencies, restore resources, preserve telemetry, and reassess capacity. Reopen on workload, architecture, provider, configuration, or objective change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Workload, authorization, environment, results, service objectives, safety, bottlenecks, capacity, evidence, limitations, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending review and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve workload, generator, environment, telemetry, results, tuning, decisions, evidence, exceptions, and open gaps. Hand off to Performance, Stress, Scalability, Reliability, Release, and Operations.
