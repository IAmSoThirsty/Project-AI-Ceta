SECTION 296 — Feature Management

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Govern the activation, targeting, experimentation, monitoring, rollback, retirement, and evidence of conditional product, service, policy, or operational behavior.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A feature model with owner, purpose, population, rule, dependency, default, state, metrics, safety, access, expiry, rollback, and retirement.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Flags and experiments are production control surfaces. Stale or conflicting flags, unsafe targeting, hidden defaults, and unowned experiments can create inconsistent behavior and permanent complexity.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when behavior can be enabled, disabled, varied, targeted, experimented with, or retired independently of a full artifact release.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability when no conditional behavior, target population, experiment, configuration, or activation state changes and an approved exact baseline covers it.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Use release, configuration, authorization, experimentation, privacy, analytics, safety, support, and operations baselines.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Feature, purpose, owner, population, rule, default, dependency, data, metric, expiry, approval, rollout, rollback, and retirement.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Owners approve purpose, target, data, experiment, activation, kill switch, expiry, exception, and retirement. Humans decide user and safety impact.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may analyze authorized flag state and metrics, draft targeting and test cases, and identify stale or conflicting features. It must not activate, target, infer sensitive attributes, hide cohorts, or retire flags without authority.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Feature Manager, Product Manager, Release Engineer, Analyst, Experimenter, Privacy Reviewer, Security Auditor, Tester, Verifier, and Documentation Writer.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Define purpose and owner; design safe default and targeting; review data and privacy; test; activate progressively; monitor; pause or roll back; expire; measure outcome; and remove.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show feature, rule, population, data, owner, state, metric, evidence, and limitation. Require human approval for activation, targeting, sensitive cohort, experiment, rollback, and retirement.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Use typed flags, safe defaults, evaluation audit, targeting isolation, kill switch, dependency handling, expiry, configuration version, and behavior observability.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show feature state, population, rule, owner, metric, incidents, evidence freshness, expiry, and authorized activate, pause, roll back, restrict, extend, and retire controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Feature catalog; targeting and privacy record; test and rollout; metrics and experiment; rollback and retirement; decision, evidence, exception, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify feature, version, rule, population, data, environment, state, method, time, result, owner, and limitation. Activation or engagement proves neither benefit nor safety.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify default, targeting, authorization, privacy, accessibility, metrics, kill switch, rollback, expiry, conflict, and removal. Test missing context and evaluation service loss.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Stale flag, permanent experiment, unsafe default, sensitive targeting, conflicting flags, no owner, kill switch untested, and engagement treated as outcome.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Pause or disable, preserve state and cohort evidence, communicate if needed, correct targeting, and reassess users and data. Reopen on metric, population, policy, dependency, or feature change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Purpose, owner, state, rule, population, data, metric, expiry, rollback, evidence, limitations, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve catalog, states, targeting, experiments, metrics, incidents, decisions, evidence, exceptions, and open gaps. Hand off to Release, Configuration, Analytics, Privacy, Product, Support, and Operations.
