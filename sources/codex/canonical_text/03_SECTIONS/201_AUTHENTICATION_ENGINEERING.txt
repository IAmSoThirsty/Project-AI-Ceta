SECTION 201 — Authentication Engineering

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Engineer trustworthy authentication that establishes the assurance, binding, freshness, and session context required before an identity is allowed to request access.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

An authentication design covering assurance levels, authenticators, enrollment, recovery, phishing and replay resistance, federation, session management, step-up, failure handling, logging, user support, and retirement.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Authentication can fail through stolen credentials, weak recovery, session theft, phishing, replay, device compromise, clock or provider failure, and poor user comprehension. A successful challenge proves only what the challenge and binding actually establish.

APPLICABILITY

Apply whenever an identity must be established before access, transaction, administration, delegation, account recovery, or other consequential action.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability only when no identity assertion, authenticator, session, recovery, federation, or protected action is affected and an approved exact baseline applies.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Use identity, authorization, privacy, cryptography, key, device, application, platform, accessibility, support, and incident baselines. Define identity source, assurance target, user population, authenticators, provider, protected actions, and recovery authority.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Identity types, assurance levels, authenticators, enrollment, proofing, device binding, federation, session rules, step-up triggers, recovery, rate limits, telemetry, user journeys, accessibility needs, threats, and acceptance criteria.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Owners approve assurance levels, acceptable authenticators, recovery risk, step-up, provider trust, lockout, support verification, exceptions, and user communication. Humans decide disputed identity, high-impact recovery, emergency access, and residual risk.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may inspect authorized authentication configuration, event records, test results, and user-flow evidence; identify patterns; draft controls, tests, and explanations; and propose bounded improvements. It must preserve subject, authenticator, provider, device, session, time, and source boundaries. It must not authenticate a person, reset credentials, bypass a challenge, approve recovery, or label a user fraudulent without delegated authority and human review.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Architect, Identity Engineer, Security Auditor, User Experience Reviewer, Accessibility Reviewer, Researcher, Tester, Verifier, Governance Reviewer, Observer, Critic, Implementer, Documentation Writer, and Memory Curator.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Define assurance and threat scope; select authenticators; design enrollment, binding, federation, session, step-up, recovery, lockout, rate limiting, notification, logging, support, and retirement; test phishing, replay, credential theft, provider loss, offline use, accessibility, and user recovery; record evidence and exceptions.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show the claimed assurance, authenticator, provider, device, session, risk signal, evidence, and limitation. Require human review for high-impact recovery, emergency access, lockout reversal, provider change, and suspected fraud. Preserve accessible alternatives and appeal without weakening protected actions.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Separate identity proofing, authenticator enrollment, challenge, assertion, session, authorization, recovery, notification, and audit. Use replay resistance, secure binding, short-lived sessions, explicit reauthentication, rate limits, safe error messages, and fail-safe recovery that does not become an attack bypass.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show authentication methods, assurance, devices, sessions, provider state, recovery state, recent events, evidence freshness, lockouts, and active risks. Provide authorized revoke, reauthenticate, recover, report, appeal, and support controls with clear effect and audit.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Authentication assurance and threat model; authenticator and enrollment design; session and step-up rules; recovery and support procedures; user and accessibility flows; test, event, exception, decision, evidence, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify subject, authenticator, provider, device, challenge, session, environment, policy version, method, timestamp, result, and limitation. A login event proves only that the declared mechanism accepted the declared assertion.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify enrollment, binding, phishing and replay resistance, session expiry, revocation, step-up, recovery, lockout, rate limits, federation, accessibility, logging, and provider failure. Test credential theft, device loss, clock skew, duplicate identity, and support social engineering within authorized scope.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Password-only high-risk access, shared authenticators, weak recovery, reusable sessions, silent provider trust, informative errors that aid guessing, lockout denial of service, and treating MFA presence as sufficient assurance for every action.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Preserve events, narrow affected sessions, notify the owner and user as appropriate, and use approved reauthentication, recovery, provider failover, or manual review. Reassess identity, user harm, privacy, and authorization before restoring access. Reopen on authenticator, provider, population, threat, or assurance change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Assurance, authenticators, enrollment, sessions, step-up, recovery, support, accessibility, evidence, tests, owners, exceptions, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending independent verification and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve assurance and policy versions, authenticator inventory, provider agreements, event and recovery records, findings, evidence, exceptions, and open gaps. Hand off to IAM, Authorization, Cryptography, Key Management, Security Operations, Support, Testing, and Incident Response.
