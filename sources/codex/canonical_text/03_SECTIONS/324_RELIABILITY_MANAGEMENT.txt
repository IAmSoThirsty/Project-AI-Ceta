SECTION 324 — Reliability Management

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Manage reliability as a sustained outcome through failure prevention, detection, response, recovery, maintenance, change, capacity, and learning.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A reliability model with user and service outcomes, failure modes, indicators, objectives, error budget, dependencies, maintenance, incident, recovery, and improvement.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Reliability degrades through change, dependency, workload, aging, operator burden, and hidden failure. A clean period does not prove future reliability or that recovery works.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when service or system reliability, durability, correctness, continuity, or recovery is material.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability when no reliability, failure, maintenance, or recovery outcome changes and an approved exact baseline covers it.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Use SRE, reliability testing, resilience, availability, performance, capacity, incident, problem, maintenance, and recovery baselines.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Service, user outcome, failure modes, indicators, objectives, budget, dependencies, maintenance, incidents, tests, recovery, and owner.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Owners set reliability objectives, budget, change, maintenance, staffing, incident, recovery, and risk. Humans decide tradeoffs and protect sustainable operation.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may analyze authorized failures and indicators, identify patterns, and draft improvement. It must not suppress failure, alter service, close incident, or accept reliability without authority.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Reliability Engineer, SRE, Architect, Problem Manager, Incident Responder, Capacity Engineer, Tester, Verifier, Analyst, and Critic.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Define outcomes and modes; identify failure; set objectives; monitor; test; manage error budget; maintain; respond; recover; reduce toil; review incidents; and improve architecture and process.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show service, outcome, failure, indicator, objective, budget, evidence, action, owner, and limitation. Require human decision for change, risk, incident, recovery, and budget.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Use redundancy, graceful degradation, health, recovery, rollback, dependency isolation, capacity, maintenance, and independent control and evidence.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show reliability, failures, SLO, budget, dependencies, incidents, maintenance, evidence freshness, owner, and authorized pause, rollback, scale, recover, and reopen controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Reliability charter; failure and dependency map; objectives and budget; test and maintenance; incident, recovery, decision, evidence, exception, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify service, outcome, failure, period, indicator, source, method, result, owner, uncertainty, and limitation. Reliability estimate requires workload, duration, and exposure context.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify objective, failure mode, indicators, tests, maintenance, dependency, error budget, recovery, incident learning, and operator workload. Exercise failure and degraded mode.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Uptime-only, reliability as blame, no failure budget, untested recovery, dependency hidden, toil ignored, and green period treated as durable reliability.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Stabilize, reduce change, fail over or restore, preserve evidence, communicate, and reassess objectives and architecture. Reopen on failure, workload, dependency, service, or recovery change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Outcomes, failure modes, objectives, budget, owners, dependencies, evidence, recovery, maintenance, limitations, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve reliability history, incidents, tests, maintenance, budgets, decisions, evidence, exceptions, and open gaps. Hand off to SRE, Availability, Performance, Capacity, Continuity, and Operations.
