SECTION 274 — Tooling Engineering

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Engineer tools that make development, testing, delivery, operations, analysis, and support more reliable, observable, secure, accessible, and maintainable.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A tooling model with users, purpose, interfaces, dependencies, permissions, data, versions, installation, support, telemetry, quality, lifecycle, and fallback.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Tools become part of the system and workflow. Unmaintained or overprivileged tools can leak data, corrupt source, create hidden coupling, or make recovery dependent on one person or provider.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when a custom or shared tool materially affects engineering, testing, delivery, operations, governance, or user control.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability when the tool has no material user, access, data, automation, quality, security, or lifecycle effect and an approved exact baseline covers it.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Use user, platform, source, package, security, identity, privacy, testing, documentation, support, and operations baselines.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Tool purpose, users, workflow, interfaces, dependencies, permissions, data, version, installation, telemetry, support, failure, and retirement.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Tool owners approve purpose, access, data, release, support, exceptions, and retirement. Users report defects and unsafe behavior; humans decide shared adoption and risk.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may draft tool code, docs, tests, and diagnostics from authorized context. It must not execute privileged actions, access secrets, alter shared tools, or infer user authorization without explicit control.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Tooling Engineer, Architect, Developer Experience Engineer, Implementer, Tester, Verifier, Security Auditor, Analyst, Critic, and Documentation Writer.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Define users and purpose; design interfaces and access; implement; test behavior, security, accessibility, and recovery; package; document; support; observe; update; and retire.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show tool, version, user, permission, data, action, evidence, and limitation. Require human review for privileged tool behavior, shared release, data handling, and destructive operations.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Use least privilege, clear APIs, isolation, reproducible install, versioning, safe defaults, audit, offline or fallback path, and dependency transparency.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show tool health, version, users, permissions, dependencies, incidents, evidence freshness, owner, and authorized upgrade, restrict, rollback, disable, and retire controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Tool charter; user and workflow model; interface and access design; build and test; documentation; support, incident, decision, evidence, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify tool, version, user, environment, method, source, result, time, actor, and limitation. Installation or successful invocation proves neither safety nor usefulness.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify install, permissions, data, functionality, accessibility, security, upgrade, failure, recovery, support, compatibility, and rollback. Test least-privilege and degraded paths.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Overprivileged tool, undocumented behavior, hidden network access, stale dependency, no fallback, personal ownership, and tool usage metric treated as outcome.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Disable or roll back, protect data and credentials, preserve evidence, provide alternate path, and reassess users and workflows. Reopen on tool, dependency, platform, or risk change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Purpose, users, permissions, data, version, dependencies, tests, support, evidence, recovery, owners, limitations, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending review and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve source, packages, versions, docs, tests, incidents, decisions, evidence, exceptions, and open gaps. Hand off to DX, Platform, Build, Security, Testing, Support, and Operations.
