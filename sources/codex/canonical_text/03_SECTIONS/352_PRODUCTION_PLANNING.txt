SECTION 352 — Production Planning

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Translate demand, design, capacity, materials, labor, equipment, quality, safety, and delivery commitments into an executable and adaptable production plan.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A production plan with demand, priority, materials, capacity, labor, equipment, sequence, quality, safety, constraints, contingency, evidence, and owner decision.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Plans trade among delivery, cost, quality, safety, workers, inventory, and resilience. A schedule can be feasible on paper while relying on unavailable inputs or unsafe load.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when production, assembly, manufacturing, provisioning, service capacity, or delivery is planned or materially re-sequenced.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability when no demand, production, capacity, material, workforce, safety, or delivery behavior changes and an approved exact baseline covers it.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Use product, manufacturing, supply chain, capacity, workforce, safety, quality, logistics, finance, and customer commitment baselines.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Demand, product, materials, inventory, capacity, labor, equipment, sequence, quality, safety, lead time, commitments, constraints, and alternatives.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Planning owners approve priority, schedule, overtime, substitution, safety, customer commitment, cost, and contingency. Humans resolve competing demand and worker impact.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may analyze authorized demand and capacity, draft schedules, and identify constraints. It must not commit delivery, change priority, infer worker availability, or substitute material without authority.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Production Planner, Supply Chain Manager, Manufacturing Engineer, Capacity Analyst, Quality Engineer, Safety Reviewer, Researcher, Analyst, and Verifier.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Forecast demand; map materials and capacity; set priority; schedule; validate constraints; coordinate production; monitor; adjust; manage exception; communicate; and review plan accuracy.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show demand, product, material, capacity, labor, equipment, priority, commitment, evidence, and limitation. Require human approval for schedule, safety, overtime, substitution, and customer promise.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Use versioned plan, inventory and capacity data, constraint, quality gate, safety, traceability, change, alternative, and contingency modeling.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show demand, schedule, inventory, capacity, constraints, quality, safety, evidence freshness, owner, and authorized prioritize, reschedule, hold, substitute, expedite, and cancel controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Production plan; demand and capacity; materials and sequence; quality and safety; exception and contingency; decision, evidence, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify demand, product, plan version, material, capacity, source, method, time, result, owner, and limitation. Forecast or schedule proves intent, not execution or delivery.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify constraints, materials, capacity, labor, equipment, safety, quality, schedule, contingency, customer commitment, and plan changes. Test shortage and equipment failure.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Schedule without material, safety or quality sacrificed, forecast as fact, hidden overtime, single-source plan, priority drift, and customer promise without evidence.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Replan, protect safety and quality, communicate, use approved alternative, preserve decisions, and reassess commitments. Reopen on demand, input, capacity, workforce, equipment, or product change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Demand, plan, materials, capacity, people, equipment, quality, safety, evidence, owners, contingencies, limitations, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve plans, versions, demand, exceptions, decisions, evidence, safety, quality, and open gaps. Hand off to Manufacturing, Assembly, Supply Chain, Logistics, Finance, Sales, and Operations.
