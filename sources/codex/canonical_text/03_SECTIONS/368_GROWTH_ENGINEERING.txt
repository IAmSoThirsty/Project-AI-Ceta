SECTION 368 — Growth Engineering

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Design and operate responsible mechanisms for adoption, activation, retention, referral, expansion, or reach while protecting user agency, trust, accessibility, and product value.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A growth model with objective, audience, journey, intervention, metric, experiment, privacy, safety, guardrails, feedback, and stop or rollback criteria.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Growth systems can optimize behavior rather than benefit and can create dark patterns, spam, exclusion, dependency, or harm. Growth is not inherently evidence of value.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when product, service, communication, pricing, feature, referral, onboarding, or lifecycle interventions aim to change adoption or customer behavior.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability when no user behavior, audience, adoption, retention, commercial, or intervention outcome changes and an approved exact baseline covers it.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Use product, customer, analytics, privacy, trust and safety, ethics, legal, experimentation, accessibility, and commerce baselines.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Objective, audience, journey, intervention, metric, cohort, data, experiment, risk, guardrail, feedback, and stop condition.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Growth owners define protected outcomes and guardrails; product, privacy, legal, ethics, and user owners review interventions. Humans approve high-impact targeting and communication.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may analyze authorized aggregate behavior, propose hypotheses, draft experiments, and identify anomalies. It must not target sensitive traits, manipulate users, expose data, or optimize a metric without human review.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Growth Engineer, Product Manager, Product Analyst, Researcher, Experimenter, Privacy Reviewer, Ethics Reviewer, User Experience Reviewer, Verifier, and Critic.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Define value and guardrails; understand journey; design intervention; assess harm and privacy; experiment; monitor; support opt-out; stop or roll back; analyze outcome; and retire.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show objective, audience, intervention, data, metric, guardrail, evidence, uncertainty, and limitation. Require human approval for targeting, communication, pricing, consent, and stop decisions.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Use consent, audience controls, feature flags, experiment isolation, frequency limits, accessibility, privacy, opt-out, rollback, and harm monitoring.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show intervention, audience, metric, guardrail, complaints, evidence freshness, owner, and authorized pause, narrow, opt out, rollback, compensate, and retire controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Growth charter; audience and journey; intervention and guardrail; experiment and metric; feedback, decision, evidence, exception, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify objective, audience, intervention, cohort, metric, period, source, method, result, harm, and limitation. Growth metric proves only behavior change, not value or consent.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify targeting, consent, accessibility, privacy, guardrails, experiment, harm, opt-out, communication, rollback, and user outcome. Test adverse and vulnerable cohorts.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Dark pattern, metric gaming, sensitive targeting, spam, coercive retention, growth as value, no opt-out, and intervention continued after harm signal.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Pause intervention, protect users, preserve evidence, communicate, reverse or compensate, and reassess goal and guardrails. Reopen on audience, product, policy, metric, or harm change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Objective, audience, intervention, metrics, guardrails, evidence, privacy, owners, stop conditions, limitations, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve interventions, cohorts, experiments, feedback, incidents, decisions, evidence, exceptions, and open gaps. Hand off to Product, Analytics, Experimentation, Customer Success, Privacy, Ethics, and Marketing.
