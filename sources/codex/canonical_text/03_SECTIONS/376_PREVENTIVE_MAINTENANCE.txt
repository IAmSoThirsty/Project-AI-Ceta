SECTION 376 — Preventive Maintenance

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Perform planned inspection, update, cleaning, calibration, replacement, testing, and adjustment to reduce foreseeable failure, degradation, risk, or obsolescence.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A preventive-maintenance model with asset, condition, interval, trigger, procedure, resources, safety, evidence, effectiveness, deferral, and review.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Calendar work can waste resources or miss condition-based risk. Preventive maintenance must be tied to failure evidence and protected outcomes, not activity count alone.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when planned work can prevent failure, preserve quality, maintain safety, improve security, or extend useful life.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability when no preventable failure, condition, inspection, update, calibration, or lifecycle outcome changes and an approved exact baseline covers it.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Use maintenance, reliability, safety, security, quality, asset, supplier, change, and operations baselines.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Asset, condition, failure history, interval, trigger, procedure, parts, skills, safety, window, evidence, and effectiveness measure.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Owners approve interval, procedure, safety, deferral, resources, and effectiveness. Qualified staff execute and record; humans decide when evidence warrants change.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may analyze authorized condition and failure history, draft schedules, and identify interval risk. It must not infer safety, schedule critical work, alter asset, or close maintenance without authority.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Preventive Maintenance Engineer, Reliability Engineer, Hardware Engineer, Software Engineer, Safety Reviewer, Planner, Tester, Verifier, and Operations Engineer.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Identify failure and condition; define trigger or interval; plan; secure window; execute; inspect and test; record; measure effectiveness; adjust; manage deferral; and retire.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show asset, condition, failure basis, interval, procedure, safety, evidence, owner, and limitation. Require human approval for safety, production, access, and interval change.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Use asset history, condition telemetry, maintenance mode, redundancy, calibration, update rollback, parts, work instruction, evidence, and safe return.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show due work, condition, risk, interval, backlog, safety, evidence freshness, owner, and authorized schedule, defer, execute, verify, and retire controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Preventive-maintenance charter; asset and failure basis; schedule and procedure; execution and test; effectiveness and deferral; decision, evidence, exception, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify asset, condition, interval, source, procedure, method, time, operator, result, effectiveness, and limitation. Completion count proves only activity, not reduced failure.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify failure basis, interval, procedure, safety, execution, calibration, test, effectiveness, deferral, and return to service. Compare recurrence and condition evidence.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Calendar theater, interval never reviewed, maintenance skipped, condition ignored, unsafe window, activity metric as reliability, and work without effectiveness evidence.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Protect asset, perform corrective work, preserve evidence, revise interval, communicate, and reassess risk. Reopen on failure, condition, environment, asset, supplier, or design change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Asset, failure basis, interval, procedure, safety, evidence, effectiveness, owners, deferral, limitations, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending qualified acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve schedules, asset history, work, tests, effectiveness, deferrals, incidents, decisions, evidence, exceptions, and open gaps. Hand off to Corrective, Adaptive, Reliability, Quality, Safety, and Operations.
