SECTION 065 — Industrial Design

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define the governed design of products and physical experiences so form, function, usability, manufacture, service, accessibility, safety, identity, and lifecycle reinforce the intended outcome.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A documented industrial design baseline linking user needs, form, ergonomics, materials, interfaces, production, quality, service, evidence, and lifecycle decisions.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Appearance alone does not establish usability, safety, accessibility, manufacturability, durability, or responsible disposal. Early form decisions can create lasting cost and harm.

APPLICABILITY

Applies to physical products, devices, enclosures, controls, packaging, tools, public-facing artifacts, and connected products where human use or physical realization matters.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability only when no material effect exists on form, ergonomics, physical interaction, accessibility, manufacture, service, identity, safety, or lifecycle, naming baseline and reopening trigger.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Requirements, user research, domain and capability outcomes, functional and mechanical boundaries, manufacturing constraints, materials, accessibility, safety, sustainability, cost, service, and lifecycle obligations.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Audience, use context, journeys, environments, anthropometric range, task evidence, brand or identity constraints, materials, production process, tolerances, hazards, maintenance, packaging, and disposal constraints.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Human owners approve intended users, protected outcomes, aesthetic and functional tradeoffs, accessibility, safety, cultural claims, production release, exceptions, and acceptance. Design capability does not create authority.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may synthesize research, generate alternatives, check dimensions, compare materials, and draft evaluation plans. It must label invention, preserve source evidence, avoid stereotyping, and never approve safety, accessibility, production, or user acceptance.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Architect, Researcher, Analyst, Critic, User Experience Reviewer, Implementer, Tester, Verifier, Planner, Documentation Writer, Governance Reviewer, Red Team, Optimizer, and Memory Curator under existing role contracts.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Define users, context, outcomes, constraints, and lifecycle. 2. Translate research into form, interaction, material, ergonomic, accessibility, and service requirements. 3. Develop alternatives and record tradeoffs. 4. Review prototype, production, quality, safety, repair, and disposal implications. 5. Test with representative users and qualified specialists. 6. Baseline evidence, dissent, decisions, and gaps.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show source research, assumptions, affected populations, alternatives, uncertainty, and evaluation status. Human review is required for identity, inclusion, safety, accessibility, cultural meaning, and production decisions. Preserve manual and accessible paths.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Represent form, dimensions, materials, interfaces, ergonomics, controls, accessibility, manufacturing revision, as-built state, and lifecycle as controlled artifacts. Changes trigger impact analysis across mechanical, electronics, software, packaging, service, and testing.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show design revision, intended users, constraints, prototype or production state, evidence, unresolved risks, approvals, and authorized revise, quarantine, recall, repair, or retire actions.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Design brief; user and context model; sketches and CAD; material and finish register; ergonomic and accessibility analysis; prototype records; manufacturing and service review; test plan; decision and evidence register.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Bind claims to user population, context, prototype or production revision, method, environment, sample limits, reviewer, result, and unresolved interpretation. Renderings and preference surveys do not prove safe or effective use.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify fit, reach, comprehension, accessibility, control use, hazards, durability, cleaning, assembly, service, production variation, packaging, and disposal with representative conditions and recorded results.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Aesthetic-first design; token user testing; inaccessible controls; hidden maintenance burden; unsafe edges or force; prototype-only claims; unapproved material substitution; and treating brand consistency as user success.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Stop release or isolate affected products, preserve samples and evidence, identify affected users, and use approved redesign, repair, recall, retrofit, or withdrawal. Reopen after user, material, production, hazard, or evidence change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Design intent, users, form, function, accessibility, safety, manufacture, service, evidence, acceptance limits, and open gaps are explicit; status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE until reconciliation and acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve revision, rationale, research, prototypes, materials, approvals, tests, deviations, and next action. Hand off to Mechanical, Electronics, UX, UI, Visual, Manufacturing, Testing, Operations, and Retirement.
