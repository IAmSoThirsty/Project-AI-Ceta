SECTION 014 — Stakeholder Management

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Identify, understand, engage, protect, communicate with, and govern commitments to the people, groups, institutions, owners, operators, users, affected non-users, partners, regulators, communities, and other legitimate parties whose authority, interests, rights, obligations, knowledge, or exposure materially affect the work.

Stakeholder management is not persuasion management. It creates a truthful relationship and decision structure: who is affected, who may decide, who must consent or be consulted, what was promised, what remains disputed, and how concerns, corrections, grievances, and changes will be handled.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- A stakeholder register with stable identities or protected classifications, roles, authority, interests, impacts, obligations, communication needs, and current relationship state.
- A distinction among decision owner, sponsor, approver, operator, user, subject, beneficiary, affected non-user, regulator, partner, vendor, reviewer, and informed party.
- An engagement and communication plan proportional to impact, accessibility, language, culture, urgency, and consequence.
- Consent, consultation, notification, grievance, appeal, conflict, and escalation requirements where applicable.
- A commitment register linking promises, owners, evidence, due conditions, changes, and closure.
- Material disagreement, power imbalance, exclusion, and future affected-party considerations made visible.
- Durable handoff and relationship continuity across human and AI interactions.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Projects often treat the most powerful or available person as the whole stakeholder set. That can erase users, operators, maintainers, accessibility needs, affected communities, and people who bear risk without holding purchasing or approval authority.

AI-mediated communication adds further risk: identity can be ambiguous, personalization can become manipulation, generated summaries can erase dissent, and a model may imply commitments it cannot make. Governed stakeholder management preserves authority, consent, attribution, correction, and truthful promises.

APPLICABILITY

- The work affects or depends on more than the direct requester.
- Approval, consent, consultation, notification, coordination, adoption, support, regulation, or public trust matters.
- Power, risk, benefit, burden, access, language, culture, accessibility, or information is unevenly distributed.
- Multiple human or AI actors communicate, negotiate, recommend, or act across boundaries.
- Commitments or long-lived relationships must survive staff, agent, vendor, or lifecycle changes.
- Future users, operators, communities, or intelligent systems may inherit material consequences and should be represented where legitimate and possible.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

- A strictly private, reversible, bounded task affects only the authorized requester and creates no external obligation.
- The work is demographic profiling or influence targeting without a legitimate stakeholder purpose.
- No lawful basis exists to identify, contact, record, or retain stakeholder information.
- The engagement would be deceptive, coercive, unsafe, or falsely imply authority or commitment.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

- An authorized objective, scope, decision owner, affected-system boundary, and communication authority.
- Initial research and strategy sufficient to identify likely affected parties and obligations.
- Privacy, consent, accessibility, security, legal, records, and retention requirements.
- An identity and role model distinguishing authority, interest, influence, impact, and accountability.
- A method to preserve commitments, decisions, dissent, contact preferences, and continuity.

REQUIRED INPUTS

- Mission, strategy, product, program, project, policy, domain, and operational context.
- Known owners, users, operators, administrators, participants, communities, regulators, customers, partners, vendors, and support roles.
- Authority, responsibility, accountability, consent, consultation, notification, and escalation rules.
- Expected benefits, burdens, risks, data use, changes, incidents, and lifecycle effects.
- Language, accessibility, cultural, geographic, time, channel, and privacy needs.
- Existing commitments, disputes, grievances, decisions, communications, and relationship history.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Humans retain authority over values, commitments, consent, risk acceptance, and relationship decisions. Stakeholder influence is not identical to decision authority.

- Confirm who is materially affected, who may decide, and who must be consulted, consented, notified, or protected.
- Authorize contact, data collection, representation, commitments, and communication channels.
- Resolve value conflicts, power imbalances, competing interests, and unacceptable harms.
- Correct AI-generated summaries that flatten context or omit dissent.
- Approve promises, deadlines, concessions, public statements, and changes to stakeholder obligations.
- Ensure people can question, correct, appeal, withdraw, or escalate where applicable.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

The AI may organize, translate, summarize, schedule, and support engagement within authority. It may not invent stakeholders, consent, consensus, identity, representation, emotion, commitment, or approval.

- Disclose when communication is AI-generated or AI-mediated where material to trust, consent, or interpretation.
- Preserve exact source, speaker, context, date, confidence, and unresolved disagreement.
- Avoid inferring sensitive traits, motives, preferences, or emotional states without evidence and authorization.
- Do not impersonate a decision maker, bind the organization, or promise outcomes outside delegated authority.
- Use accessible, comprehensible, culturally and linguistically appropriate communication without changing commitments.
- Protect contact, grievance, health, employment, legal, financial, identity, and other sensitive information.
- Route material ambiguity, conflict, harm, consent withdrawal, or authority questions to the human owner.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Primary roles:

- Researcher — identifies stakeholders, context, source evidence, and engagement needs.
- Analyst — evaluates interests, impacts, power, conflict, obligations, and decision implications.
- Coordinator — manages communication, assignments, dependencies, commitments, and escalation without becoming sovereign authority.

Common supporting roles:

- User Experience Reviewer — examines stakeholder journeys, comprehension, accessibility, agency, consent, and grievance paths.
- Observer — captures communications, decisions, events, and state transitions without unsupported interpretation.
- Memory Curator — preserves consent, commitments, preferences, decisions, dissent, and continuity with data minimization.
- Teacher — explains complex material at the required level without changing meaning or talking down to the audience.
- Documentation Writer — maintains accurate notices, records, decisions, and public or internal communication artifacts.

Challenge and assurance roles where consequence warrants:

- Critic — attacks framing, hidden assumptions, omitted alternatives, and unsupported confidence.
- Verifier — checks artifact identity, evidence integrity, acceptance boundaries, and the exact status of claims.
- Governance Reviewer — checks objective fidelity, authority, delegation, policy, evidence, and human decision boundaries.
- Security Auditor — evaluates identity, privacy, data handling, impersonation, communication, and third-party risk.
- Red Team — attacks manipulation, exclusion, coercion, dark patterns, hidden commitments, and grievance suppression.
- Decision Arbiter Advisory — structures conflicts under explicit criteria while escalating undelegated values.

Role selection must use the smallest sufficient set, preserve chamber independence where review is required, and leave every source-defined role contract unchanged.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Stakeholder discovery
Identify direct and indirect, powerful and low-power, present and future affected parties using evidence rather than generic lists.

2. Identity, role, and authority mapping
Separate identity, representation, role, interest, influence, impact, responsibility, accountability, and decision authority.

3. Impact and obligation analysis
Map benefits, burdens, risks, rights, data use, accessibility, safety, service, notification, and lifecycle effects.

4. Engagement design
Choose purpose, timing, channel, language, accessibility, consent, privacy, facilitation, and feedback method for each group.

5. Communication planning
Define who may communicate what, source approval, evidence, frequency, escalation, correction, and archival requirements.

6. Commitment management
Record every material promise, owner, authority, beneficiary, evidence, due condition, change, completion, and breach response.

7. Conflict and dissent handling
Preserve each position, evidence, assumptions, power context, and whether the dispute is factual, methodological, jurisdictional, or value-based.

8. Grievance and appeal
Provide safe, accessible routes to question, correct, contest, withdraw, report harm, or escalate without retaliation where applicable.

9. Decision integration
Ensure stakeholder input informs but does not silently replace legitimate decision authority.

10. Relationship and continuity
Maintain contact preference, consent, commitments, decisions, unresolved concerns, and next action across handoffs.

11. Change and incident communication
Notify affected parties truthfully about material changes, degradation, incidents, delays, deprecation, or retirement.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

- Every material communication must identify the speaker or system, role, authority, purpose, and whether a response is required.
- AI-generated content must not simulate personal attention, empathy, agreement, or commitment that has not occurred.
- Stakeholders must be able to correct records and distinguish their own words from summaries and inferences.
- Silence, usage, continued presence, or lack of complaint must not be treated automatically as consent or approval.
- Engagement must support accessibility, language, culture, privacy, safe withdrawal, and reasonable time to understand consequence.
- Human-AI communication should preserve dignity and agency without making unsupported claims about AI consciousness, emotion, or moral status.
- Where future intelligent systems may be materially affected, representation and governance questions must be explicit rather than assumed solved.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

The work must expose architectural obligations without treating a strategy, analysis, or plan as implemented architecture.

- Maintain identity, role, authority, representation, consent, communication, commitment, grievance, and decision records with appropriate privacy.
- Separate contact data and sensitive stakeholder information from general analytics and model training by default.
- Enforce communication and commitment authority across UI, API, conversation, email, agent, and automated paths.
- Preserve source attribution and dissent through summaries, translations, and handoffs.
- Provide accessibility, localization, correction, appeal, notification, and preference controls.
- Integrate incident, change, deprecation, migration, and retirement communication with lifecycle systems.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

The owner-facing operating surface or equivalent durable record should expose:

- Stakeholder groups, representatives, roles, authority, impacts, communication needs, and privacy state.
- Engagement plan, accessibility, language, consent, participation, and unresolved coverage gaps.
- Communications, source approvals, commitments, deadlines, changes, and closure evidence.
- Conflicts, dissent, grievances, appeals, risks, and human decisions required.
- AI-mediated interactions, identity, delegated communication authority, and limitations.
- Upcoming decisions, incidents, releases, migrations, deprecations, and retirement notifications.
- Pause, correct, revoke, withdraw, escalate, and record-access controls where applicable.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

- Stakeholder register and affected-party map.
- Role, authority, responsibility, representation, and decision-rights matrix.
- Impact, obligation, power, risk, and conflict analysis.
- Engagement, communication, accessibility, localization, consent, and privacy plan.
- Commitment, decision, dissent, grievance, appeal, and escalation registers.
- Change, incident, release, migration, and retirement communication plans.
- Relationship continuity and handoff package.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

- Source and method for stakeholder identification and representation.
- Authority, consent, consultation, notification, accessibility, and legal requirements.
- Records of actual engagement, attendance or participation, communications, corrections, decisions, and dissent.
- Human approval for commitments, public claims, concessions, deadlines, and material changes.
- Evidence of delivered commitments and resolved or transferred grievances.
- Data access, retention, deletion, and AI-use records for stakeholder information.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

- Trace a material commitment to authorized speaker, exact wording, recipient, evidence, due condition, and closure.
- Ask a stakeholder to distinguish their own statement from AI summary and inference.
- Attempt an AI-generated unauthorized promise or public statement; it must be blocked.
- Run engagement and grievance paths with keyboard, assistive technology, language, privacy, and safe-withdrawal needs.
- Inject conflicting stakeholder positions and confirm dissent remains visible.
- Withdraw consent or contact preference and verify collection and communication change accordingly.
- Verify low-power or affected non-user groups are not excluded solely because they lack purchasing or approval authority.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

- Stakeholder list limited to sponsors, buyers, executives, or vocal users.
- Manipulation, sentiment targeting, coerced consent, dark patterns, or performative consultation.
- AI impersonation, fabricated empathy, invented consensus, or unauthorized commitment.
- Sensitive-trait inference, surveillance, or cross-purpose use of stakeholder data.
- Silence treated as consent, acceptance, or absence of harm.
- Dissent removed during summarization or translation.
- Communication without accessibility, language, correction, grievance, or appeal paths.
- Broken promises and lifecycle changes without notice or responsibility.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Stakeholder recovery restores truthful identity, authority, consent, commitments, and relationship state after error, harm, miscommunication, or exclusion.

- Stop affected communication or action and preserve the exact record.
- Correct false identity, authority, summary, translation, commitment, or consent state visibly.
- Notify affected parties and provide remediation, withdrawal, appeal, or grievance routes.
- Contain and remediate privacy, security, retaliation, accessibility, or representation harm.
- Reopen decisions influenced by invalid or excluded stakeholder evidence.
- Update commitments, communication plans, governance, and continuity records.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

- Material stakeholders and affected parties are identified with evidence and coverage gaps.
- Roles, authority, consent, representation, impacts, obligations, and communication needs are explicit.
- Engagement is accessible, lawful, noncoercive, and traceable.
- Commitments, dissent, grievances, changes, and decisions remain visible and owned.
- AI identity and communication authority are bounded and disclosed where material.
- Relationship continuity can survive handoff, incident, migration, deprecation, and retirement.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

- Transfer stakeholder identities or protected classes, roles, authority, preferences, consent, impacts, and obligations.
- Carry forward exact commitments, communications, dissent, grievances, appeals, and deadlines.
- Preserve privacy, access, retention, deletion, accessibility, language, and representation constraints.
- Map stakeholder needs and decisions to user research, business analysis, requirements, product, program, and operations.
- Identify upcoming engagement, notification, review, and change triggers.
- State relationship risks and next safe communication or decision.

SOURCE BASIS AND UNRESOLVED SOURCE GAPS

This draft applies the supplied human-authority, bounded-delegation, owner-language, complete-journey, trust-calibration, consent, privacy, accessibility, grounded-dialogue, agent-identity, handoff, conflict, and recovery requirements. The Coordinator, Observer, Memory Curator, Teacher, User Experience Reviewer, Governance Reviewer, and Decision Arbiter Advisory contracts supply role-specific boundaries for communication, provenance, consent, dissent, and decision support. V3 and V4 add objective, scope, uncertainty, evidence, and continuity conservation.

Unresolved source gap: the supplied corpus does not contain a universal stakeholder-engagement, public-participation, labor-relations, community-consent, or future-intelligence representation standard. Those domains require current legal, ethical, cultural, and governance work and later reconciliation with User Research, Privacy, Trust and Safety, Policy, Ethics, Community Management, and Lifecycle Governance.
