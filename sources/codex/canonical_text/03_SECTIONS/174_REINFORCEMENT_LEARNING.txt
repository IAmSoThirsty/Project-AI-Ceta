SECTION 174 — Reinforcement Learning

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define governed engineering for agents that learn policies through interaction, reward, feedback, or planning, with explicit environment, objective, constraint, exploration, oversight, and deployment boundaries.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- A formal task and environment contract with states, actions, observations, rewards, constraints, termination, and authority.
- Evidence that behavior meets protected outcomes beyond reward optimization in simulation or training.
- Controlled exploration, evaluation, deployment, monitoring, interruption, and recovery.

WHY THIS MATTERS

An agent can maximize a reward while exploiting its proxy, harming users, or violating constraints. Interactive learning also creates data, state, and action effects that are difficult to reverse.

APPLICABILITY

Applies to reinforcement learning, preference optimization, online learning, planning agents, adaptive control, bandits, and systems that learn from actions or feedback. Depth increases with physical action, external users, autonomy, or live exploration.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

May be non-applicable to static supervised inference or offline conceptual discussion with no policy, action, or learning claim. Record exact scope and reopening trigger.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Requires environment and domain models, reward authority, safety constraints, evaluation, simulation validity, security, privacy, operations, and human interruption. Depends on Sections 073, 074, 164, 167, 180, 182, 184, and 188.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Task; state; observation; action; reward; hard constraints; environment; simulator; feedback; exploration budget; safety envelope; policy; human approval; monitoring; termination; and compensation.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Humans define protected outcomes, constraints, reward legitimacy, exploration limits, deployment, intervention, and acceptable residual risk. They may pause, override, revoke, or retire; the learner cannot redefine its objective.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may learn policies and propose actions within a sandbox or delegated boundary. It must expose reward and uncertainty and must not explore live without authority, hide reward hacking, or treat success in simulation as safe operation.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Architect, Researcher, Analyst, Planner, Implementer, Safety Auditor, Security Auditor, Privacy Reviewer, Tester, Verifier, Critic, Red Team, Governance Reviewer, Optimizer, Documentation Writer, and HITL Operator.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Define environment, task, state, actions, reward, constraints, and authority.
2. Validate simulator, feedback, reward, data, and safety envelope.
3. Isolate exploration and record seeds, trajectories, policies, and configuration.
4. Evaluate reward hacking, distribution shift, rare events, adversarial behavior, and subgroups.
5. Test human stop, override, fallback, termination, and compensation.
6. Gate promotion from simulation to offline, shadow, limited, and live operation.
7. Monitor, investigate, and retire policy with evidence.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show current state, action, policy, reward, constraint, authority, uncertainty, and review. Require confirmation for consequential actions and preserve an immediate stop path. Human feedback must be attributable and cannot be silently converted into policy.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Version environments, simulators, policies, trajectories, reward functions, constraints, seeds, feedback, checkpoints, actions, and outcomes. Enforce safety constraints outside the policy where possible and preserve idempotent interruption and compensation.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show policy, environment, current state, action queue, reward, constraints, exploration mode, reviewer, incidents, and evidence. Provide authorized pause, stop, override, reduce scope, revoke, fallback, compensate, rollback configuration, and retire.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

RL task charter; environment and simulator specification; reward and constraint register; trajectory and policy records; evaluation and red-team report; promotion gates; intervention runbook; decision record; and continuity package.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Claims identify environment, policy, reward, constraint, scenario, trajectory, seed, evaluator, outcome, uncertainty, and limitation. Reward, episode count, or simulation success does not prove protected outcome or safe deployment.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Test reward misspecification, constraint violation, exploration, rare state, adversarial input, simulator gap, feedback poisoning, action interruption, authority revocation, fallback, compensation, and live promotion gates. Reproduce accepted runs.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Reward hacking; proxy-only success; unrestricted exploration; simulator overconfidence; hidden policy state; unsafe retries; feedback poisoning; no human stop; and destructive rollback of prior policy evidence.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Stop policy actions on constraint violation, reward anomaly, unsafe trajectory, drift, feedback poisoning, or authority loss. Preserve state and immutable decisions, use safe fallback or compensation, review impact, and requalify before resuming.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Task, environment, reward, constraints, policy, exploration, evaluation, intervention, recovery, evidence, and authority are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending reconciliation and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS
