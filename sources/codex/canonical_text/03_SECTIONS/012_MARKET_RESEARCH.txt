SECTION 012 — Market Research

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Establish a truthful, current, and methodologically bounded understanding of external demand, populations, segments, behaviors, alternatives, channels, pricing or funding conditions, adoption barriers, and market structure for a specific decision.

Market research does not manufacture a market narrative. It defines who or what was studied, how evidence was obtained, what the evidence supports, which populations were not represented, and how quickly findings may become stale.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- A decision-linked market research question, scope, target population, geography, jurisdiction, and timeframe.
- A research design with source hierarchy, sampling, inclusion, exclusion, collection, analysis, privacy, consent, and quality controls.
- A current market and alternative baseline with definitions that prevent category inflation.
- Segment, need, behavior, adoption, channel, price or funding, and barrier findings bounded by evidence.
- Conflicting evidence, sampling limitations, bias, uncertainty, and time sensitivity made visible.
- A conclusion and recommendation whose confidence does not exceed the method.
- A reproducible evidence and continuity package for later strategy and product decisions.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Market claims are unusually easy to inflate. Search snippets, vendor forecasts, social media activity, synthetic respondents, and model-generated summaries can create the appearance of broad demand without representative evidence.

Governed market research prevents strategic commitments from resting on fictional populations or stale categories. It also protects participants and users by making consent, privacy, inclusion, accessibility, and data provenance part of the research method rather than cleanup after collection.

APPLICABILITY

- A business, product, portfolio, investment, market-entry, pricing, channel, or positioning decision depends on external evidence.
- The size, characteristics, needs, behavior, willingness, adoption, or constraints of a population are uncertain.
- Market conditions, regulations, competitors, substitutes, or technologies may have changed.
- A product may serve new geographies, cultures, languages, accessibility contexts, or user groups.
- Claims of demand, growth, pricing, market share, or adoption will influence material commitments.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

- The question concerns a single known user's workflow and belongs to User Research.
- The question concerns internal processes and belongs to Business Analysis.
- The request is merely to collect promotional statistics without a defined decision or method.
- Lawful access, participant protection, or sufficient source quality cannot be established.
- Synthetic or model-generated responses are the only evidence but the claim concerns real market behavior.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

- An authorized decision question, population, scope, horizon, criteria, and research owner.
- Definitions for market, category, customer, user, buyer, beneficiary, competitor, substitute, adoption, and demand as applicable.
- Privacy, consent, accessibility, security, legal, licensing, and jurisdictional review for collection and use.
- A source and data-management plan preserving identity, date, version, method, and limitations.
- A distinction between primary research, secondary research, observed behavior, stated preference, forecast, and simulation.

REQUIRED INPUTS

- Mission, business, product, portfolio, pricing, channel, and investment questions.
- Existing market studies, primary data, public records, industry sources, surveys, interviews, observations, transactions, support, and product evidence.
- Population and segment definitions, geographies, jurisdictions, languages, accessibility needs, and time windows.
- Competitor, substitute, distribution, regulatory, economic, technological, and cultural context.
- Collection tools, sampling frame, budget, schedule, participant access, and data restrictions.
- Known biases, conflicts of interest, missing populations, and evidence gaps.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

The human research owner defines the decision, acceptable method, protected populations, budget, and whether evidence is sufficient for commitment.

- Approve the question, population, method, participant protections, and use of findings.
- Resolve value choices about inclusion, segmentation, acceptable uncertainty, and affected populations.
- Authorize access to proprietary, purchased, personal, regulated, or sensitive data.
- Correct category definitions, cultural assumptions, and market interpretations.
- Reject conclusions that overgeneralize beyond sample, geography, timeframe, or method.
- Decide whether the evidence supports experiment, entry, pricing, investment, or no action.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

The AI may search authorized sources, structure data, analyze patterns, and propose interpretations. It may not fabricate participants, responses, citations, sample representativeness, demand, market size, or willingness to pay.

- Record exact sources, dates, versions, methods, populations, and access boundaries.
- Separate primary from secondary evidence and observed behavior from stated intent.
- Identify sponsor bias, vendor methodology, circular citation, category expansion, and stale data.
- Disclose sampling, measurement, nonresponse, coverage, survivorship, selection, and model limitations.
- Treat synthetic data and AI-generated respondents as simulations, never observations of real people.
- Avoid scraping or combining data in ways that violate law, terms, consent, privacy, or reasonable expectations.
- Present minority and contradictory findings rather than smoothing them into false consensus.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Primary roles:

- Researcher — designs and executes source-grounded market inquiry and evaluates source quality.
- Analyst — performs segmentation, comparison, uncertainty, sensitivity, and decision analysis.
- Observer — captures authorized market or behavioral observations with provenance and limitations.

Common supporting roles:

- Planner — sequences collection, sampling, analysis, review, and decision gates.
- User Experience Reviewer — checks inclusion, research interaction, comprehension, accessibility, and participant burden.
- Memory Curator — preserves datasets, definitions, source state, decisions, and expiration.
- Decision Arbiter Advisory — compares market options under authorized criteria.

Challenge and assurance roles where consequence warrants:

- Critic — attacks framing, hidden assumptions, omitted alternatives, and unsupported confidence.
- Verifier — checks artifact identity, evidence integrity, acceptance boundaries, and the exact status of claims.
- Governance Reviewer — checks objective fidelity, authority, delegation, policy, evidence, and human decision boundaries.
- Security Auditor — reviews data acquisition, storage, access, reidentification, provider, and leakage risk.
- Red Team — attacks sampling, category, sponsor, confirmation, survivorship, synthetic-evidence, and market-size assumptions.

Role selection must use the smallest sufficient set, preserve chamber independence where review is required, and leave every source-defined role contract unchanged.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Decision and question definition
State what decision the research supports, target population, unit of analysis, geography, jurisdiction, timeframe, and required confidence.

2. Definition and category control
Define market boundaries, customer, user, buyer, substitute, adoption, demand, and other terms before estimating them.

3. Method design
Choose primary and secondary methods, sampling, inclusion, exclusion, collection, consent, accessibility, privacy, and analysis.

4. Source hierarchy
Prefer direct, transparent, current evidence; identify secondary citation chains, sponsor interests, and inaccessible methodology.

5. Sampling and coverage
Define sampling frame, sample size basis, recruitment, nonresponse, weighting, missing populations, and generalization limits.

6. Data collection
Collect only authorized data, preserve provenance, minimize sensitive information, and document deviations.

7. Quality and bias assessment
Check validity, reliability, representativeness, measurement error, duplicate records, bot or synthetic contamination, and conflicts.

8. Market and alternative analysis
Examine needs, behaviors, segments, channels, price or funding, barriers, competitors, substitutes, regulation, and change drivers.

9. Uncertainty and sensitivity
Use ranges and scenarios; show how category, source, sample, time, or assumption changes affect conclusions.

10. Triangulation
Compare multiple methods and evidence types without treating quantity as quality.

11. Decision synthesis
State findings, limitations, confidence, implications, recommendations, and evidence that would change them.

12. Expiration and continuity
Assign freshness, review triggers, dataset controls, and a reproducible handoff.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

- Participants must know who is collecting data, why, what will happen to it, and how to decline or withdraw where applicable.
- AI identity and use in recruitment, interviewing, transcription, analysis, or generation must be disclosed when material to consent or interpretation.
- The AI must not impersonate a human researcher or create synthetic participant quotations.
- The human must be able to inspect source excerpts, methods, sample limits, and contradictory evidence behind a market claim.
- Research interfaces must support accessibility, language, cultural context, and correction.
- Operational recommendations must remain distinct from research findings and human decisions.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

The work must expose architectural obligations without treating a strategy, analysis, or plan as implemented architecture.

- Use a research data model preserving source, participant or record identity controls, method, consent, purpose, retention, jurisdiction, and access.
- Separate raw data, cleaned data, derived variables, synthetic data, model outputs, and published findings.
- Protect against reidentification, leakage, cross-purpose reuse, unauthorized enrichment, and provider retention.
- Version definitions, sampling frames, instruments, code, weights, and analysis outputs.
- Support reproducibility and expiration of time-sensitive conclusions.
- Ensure market findings cannot directly trigger consequential product or commercial actions without downstream authority.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

The owner-facing operating surface or equivalent durable record should expose:

- Research question, population, geography, timeframe, decision owner, method, and current status.
- Sources, sample, recruitment, consent, inclusion, exclusions, coverage, and missing populations.
- Raw, cleaned, derived, synthetic, and inferred evidence states.
- Findings, uncertainty, conflicts, bias, freshness, and generalization limits.
- Data access, privacy, retention, deletion, and incident state.
- Recommendations, human decisions required, expiration, and next research action.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

- Market research brief and method plan.
- Definitions, population, sampling, source, and data-management records.
- Research instruments and participant-protection materials.
- Market, segment, need, behavior, price or funding, channel, and barrier analysis.
- Source-quality, bias, uncertainty, and limitation assessment.
- Decision synthesis and recommendation.
- Dataset, analysis, evidence, expiration, and continuity package.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

- Exact source identity, date, version, publisher, methodology, population, sponsor, and limitations.
- Sampling frame, recruitment, sample size, inclusion, exclusion, nonresponse, weighting, and missing-data records.
- Participant consent, privacy, accessibility, and data-use records where applicable.
- Raw and transformed data provenance, code or procedure, and reproducibility evidence.
- Evidence distinguishing observed behavior, stated preference, forecast, inference, and simulation.
- Contradictory findings, uncertainty intervals or ranges, and expiration conditions.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

- Trace every material market claim to a current source and bounded population.
- Change the category definition or remove a dominant source and inspect sensitivity.
- Attempt to include synthetic responses as real observations; the evidence system must reject or relabel them.
- Check whether excluded or missing populations could materially change the decision.
- Reproduce a key analysis from retained data, method, code, and definitions.
- Expire a time-sensitive source and verify dependent conclusions degrade to stale or unknown.
- Confirm no participant or protected data was collected, retained, or reused outside authorization.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

- Market-size theater using undefined categories and vendor forecasts.
- Search snippets, social activity, model output, or source count treated as demand evidence.
- Synthetic respondents, fabricated quotations, or invented surveys.
- Convenience samples presented as representative populations.
- Stated preference treated as observed purchase or adoption behavior.
- Circular citation, stale data, sponsor bias, and hidden methodology.
- Unlawful or nonconsensual scraping, enrichment, reidentification, or data reuse.
- Overgeneralization across geography, culture, jurisdiction, accessibility, or timeframe.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Market-research recovery withdraws unsupported claims, protects participants, and rebuilds the evidence chain from the first invalid source or method.

- Stop use of affected data or conclusions and preserve evidence of the defect.
- Contain privacy, consent, security, or licensing incidents and follow required notification or deletion procedures.
- Identify invalid definitions, samples, sources, transformations, analyses, or generalizations.
- Recollect, reweight, reanalyze, narrow the claim, or mark the question unresolved.
- Propagate invalidation to strategy, product, portfolio, pricing, and roadmap decisions.
- Update source, dataset, decision, and continuity records with the corrected boundary.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

- The decision question, population, method, sources, sample, timeframe, and authority are explicit.
- Data collection and use satisfy consent, privacy, security, accessibility, legal, and licensing requirements.
- Findings are traceable and bounded by sample, method, geography, jurisdiction, and time.
- Synthetic, inferred, forecast, and observed evidence remain distinct.
- Bias, conflicts, limitations, uncertainty, dissent, and expiration are visible.
- The conclusion and recommendation do not exceed the evidence and are ready for downstream decision review.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

- Provide exact research question, definitions, population, method, sources, sample, data, and analysis version.
- Carry forward limitations, missing populations, bias, uncertainty, freshness, and expiration.
- Map findings to the strategy, product, stakeholder, feasibility, or pricing decision they support.
- Preserve participant, privacy, licensing, retention, and deletion obligations.
- Identify what evidence would change the conclusion and when research must reopen.
- State whether downstream use is safe within the declared limits.

SOURCE BASIS AND UNRESOLVED SOURCE GAPS

This draft applies the supplied Researcher discipline for primary sources, dates, versions, source authority, conflicting evidence, terminology, uncertainty, and traceable citations; the Analyst discipline adds baselines, sampling limitations, alternative explanations, sensitivity, and proportionate conclusions; the Observer role adds provenance, uncertainty, absence-versus-nonobservation, privacy, and integrity; V3, the Owner's Cockpit, and V4 add consent, data protection, evidence, human authority, and continuity.

Unresolved source gap: the supplied corpus does not contain a dedicated survey, sampling, econometric, market-sizing, or pricing-research standard. Methods must later be reconciled with Statistics, Data Science, Experimentation, Privacy Engineering, Legal, Regulatory Compliance, and Financial Operations.
