SECTION 154 — Data Modeling

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define stable data meaning, structure, relationships, identifiers, constraints, and representations that support authorized processes, functions, systems, analysis, and lifecycle decisions.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

Conceptual, logical, and physical models with controlled mappings, definitions, ownership, constraints, variants, lineage, quality rules, change impact, and acceptance boundaries.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Ambiguous entities and identifiers create duplicate identity, invalid joins, privacy mistakes, and incompatible systems. A diagram is not proof that meanings are shared or enforced.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when data entities, events, relationships, schemas, identifiers, classifications, or transformations materially affect behavior or decisions.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

No material meaning, structure, relationship, identifier, constraint, interface, quality, or lifecycle behavior changes and an exact model baseline covers the work. Record scope and trigger.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Domain and information architecture, requirements, processes, functions, data subjects, source systems, contracts, jurisdictions, quality, privacy, security, records, and lifecycle rules.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Business terms, entities, events, identifiers, relationships, cardinality, constraints, states, classifications, sample records, consumers, source authority, variants, assumptions, and disputes.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

The human owner approves canonical meaning, keys, classifications, relationships, exceptions, quality thresholds, compatibility, and acceptance; assigns domain, data, privacy, security, and records authorities.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may inspect authorized glossaries, requirements, domain evidence, source schemas, contracts, representative records, lineage, mappings, and model versions. It may propose terms, entities, relationships, identifiers, constraints, model mappings, compatibility views, test cases, and impact analyses, and may surface ambiguity, disagreement, privacy risk, and unresolved assumptions for human adjudication.

AI must distinguish source-defined meaning from inference and must not declare a canonical term, resolve identity, approve classifications, expose records, alter a model or schema, or claim semantic agreement from a diagram, sample, or schema inspection. Any mutation-capable proposal must identify the authorized owner, model level and version, affected consumers and mappings, validation evidence, immutable decision and audit records, and forward migration, compatibility, or compensation path; unresolved meaning, identity, authority, or evidence conflicts require preservation of the prior version and owner review.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Architect, Analyst, Researcher, Observer, Governance Reviewer, Security Auditor, User Experience Reviewer, Implementer, Tester, Verifier, Critic, Documentation Writer, Memory Curator, and Red Team.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Establish vocabulary, scope, subjects, contexts, and model levels.
2. Define entities, events, identifiers, relationships, states, constraints, classifications, and ownership.
3. Map conceptual to logical and physical structures, contracts, lineage, variants, and migration impact.
4. Validate with domain owners and test representative, ambiguous, missing, duplicate, and incompatible records.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

AI may propose models and mappings from authorized evidence, but must distinguish source meaning from inference. It must not declare a canonical term, infer identity, expose records, or alter schemas without authority.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Keep meaning, model level, schema, storage, interface, and implementation distinct. Version identifiers, constraints, mappings, lineage, ownership, compatibility, and lifecycle states; make critical constraints enforceable.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show model version, domain owner, scope, mappings, terms, identifiers, constraints, consumers, conflicts, evidence, and authorized approve, supersede, restrict, migrate, and retire controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Model charter; glossary and data dictionary; conceptual, logical, physical, event, state, and lineage models; contracts; mapping and compatibility records; decisions, tests, evidence, dissent, and handoff.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Claims identify source, definition, model level, version, scope, population, method, owner, and limitation. A sample, diagram, or schema inspection proves only its bounded observation.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify term consistency, key uniqueness, cardinality, constraints, null and unknown handling, temporal behavior, mappings, schema compatibility, access implications, and independent reproduction.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Entity soup, overloaded identifiers, hidden many-to-many relations, accidental null semantics, physical design masquerading as meaning, copied definitions, and unowned model changes.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Freeze affected changes when meaning or identity conflicts arise; preserve prior versions and evidence. Use owner adjudication, mapping, quarantine, controlled migration, compatibility views, or compensation, then reassess consumers.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Terms, entities, relationships, constraints, owners, mappings, consumers, tests, evidence, gaps, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE until later gates.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Hand off models and contracts to Database, Data, Integration, API, Application, Analytics, ML, Privacy, Security, Records, Testing, Migration, Operations, and Retirement work.
