SECTION 013 — Competitive Intelligence

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Produce lawful, ethical, current, and decision-relevant understanding of competitors, substitutes, adjacent actors, ecosystem forces, and alternative approaches without misrepresenting public claims as verified capability or crossing security, privacy, contractual, or trade-secret boundaries.

Competitive intelligence supports strategy by revealing differences, dependencies, likely responses, and evidence gaps. It is not espionage, imitation, marketing-summary collection, or permission to define the product through competitors.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- A bounded intelligence question, scope, named subjects, timeframe, jurisdiction, authority, and collection rules.
- A source-grounded profile of competitors, substitutes, alternatives, ecosystems, and relevant capabilities.
- A comparison framework using consistent criteria and explicit evidence states.
- Clear separation among claimed, documented, demonstrated, independently verified, inferred, and unknown capability.
- Analysis of positioning, business model, technology, product, operations, partnerships, risks, likely responses, and strategic implications where relevant.
- Legal, ethical, security, privacy, licensing, and source-handling compliance.
- A recommendation and monitoring plan that does not turn comparison into imitation.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Competitive decisions are often made from websites, demos, press releases, analyst reports, rumors, and benchmark claims that describe different versions, environments, or criteria. AI can aggregate these sources quickly while also blending marketing language with inference and reproducing unverified claims as fact.

Governed intelligence protects strategy from both complacency and reactive imitation. It identifies what is actually known, what matters to the mission and user, and which competitor behavior should trigger review without granting competitors control over product direction.

APPLICABILITY

- Strategy, positioning, product, partnership, procurement, investment, pricing, or risk decisions depend on external actors or alternatives.
- A competitor or substitute may materially change user expectations, market structure, technology, regulation, supply, or security.
- Claims of differentiation, parity, advantage, or threat require evidence.
- The ecosystem includes providers, platforms, models, standards, distributors, integrators, open-source projects, or adjacent entrants.
- Continuous monitoring is required for defined high-impact signals.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

- The purpose is covert access, impersonation, social engineering, unauthorized testing, theft, or acquisition of protected information.
- The decision can be made from user, mission, and feasibility evidence without competitor analysis.
- The request is to copy a competitor feature or message without establishing product need.
- Source legality, license, authorization, or integrity cannot be established.
- The named subject is a private individual rather than a legitimate organizational or product intelligence target.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

- An authorized intelligence question, decision owner, collection boundary, jurisdiction, and prohibited methods.
- Mission, product, business, or procurement criteria defining why the comparison matters.
- A source hierarchy and evidence-state model.
- Legal, security, privacy, contractual, licensing, and ethical constraints.
- A monitoring and retention plan for time-sensitive intelligence.

REQUIRED INPUTS

- Public filings, official documentation, product pages, terms, pricing, release notes, standards, repositories, demonstrations, support materials, and published research.
- Independent tests, reviews, customer evidence, incidents, legal actions, procurement evidence, and technical analysis where lawfully available.
- Competitor, substitute, ecosystem, provider, partner, and channel definitions.
- Human-authorized comparison criteria, decision context, horizon, and risk tolerance.
- Current internal capability and product evidence for like-for-like comparison.
- Known rumors, disputed claims, missing evidence, and monitoring triggers.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

The authorized human defines legitimate targets, collection methods, comparison criteria, and strategic response. Competitive pressure does not create permission.

- Approve the intelligence question, subjects, methods, source use, and legal or ethical boundaries.
- Define which differences matter to mission, users, business, risk, or procurement.
- Resolve tradeoffs and decide whether to respond, monitor, partner, differentiate, or take no action.
- Reject pressure to obtain protected information or conduct unauthorized access or testing.
- Correct internal capability evidence and prevent unfair or misleading comparison.
- Authorize any external communication, claim, negotiation, or response based on intelligence.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

The AI may collect authorized public information, normalize evidence, compare, and monitor. It may not impersonate, infiltrate, solicit secrets, bypass controls, fabricate sources, or present inference as observed competitor fact.

- Record exact source, publisher, date, version, product tier, environment, jurisdiction, and access method.
- Distinguish official claim, documentation, demonstration, independent test, incident, inference, rumor, and unknown.
- Compare equivalent scopes and disclose where configurations, populations, dates, or methods differ.
- Avoid copying competitor language, code, data, design, or protected material beyond lawful and licensed use.
- Do not autonomously contact employees, customers, partners, or systems to obtain intelligence.
- Identify confirmation bias, halo effects, category mismatch, benchmark gaming, and outdated evidence.
- Preserve internal mission and user criteria rather than recommending imitation by default.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Primary roles:

- Researcher — collects and assesses lawful, traceable, current competitive sources.
- Analyst — performs consistent comparison, alternative explanation, scenario, and strategic implication analysis.
- Security Auditor — defines and reviews collection boundaries, sensitive handling, and prohibited access.

Common supporting roles:

- Architect — compares structural approaches, interfaces, dependencies, and operational implications without assuming implementation parity.
- User Experience Reviewer — compares complete journeys and user control rather than screenshot similarity.
- Observer — records defined public signals and changes with timestamps and provenance.
- Memory Curator — preserves source state, monitoring history, decisions, and superseded findings.
- Decision Arbiter Advisory — structures strategic response under authorized criteria.

Challenge and assurance roles where consequence warrants:

- Critic — attacks framing, hidden assumptions, omitted alternatives, and unsupported confidence.
- Verifier — checks artifact identity, evidence integrity, acceptance boundaries, and the exact status of claims.
- Governance Reviewer — checks objective fidelity, authority, delegation, policy, evidence, and human decision boundaries.
- Red Team — attacks source reliability, deceptive claims, comparison bias, likely competitor response, and internal overreaction.

Role selection must use the smallest sufficient set, preserve chamber independence where review is required, and leave every source-defined role contract unchanged.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Question and boundary
Define decision, subjects, timeframe, geography, jurisdiction, collection methods, protected information, prohibited actions, and authority.

2. Subject and alternative map
Identify direct competitors, substitutes, internal alternatives, open-source options, providers, partners, and likely entrants.

3. Source inventory
Collect lawful sources with identity, date, version, scope, method, sponsor, access, and limitations.

4. Capability-state classification
Classify each claim as claimed, documented, demonstrated, independently tested, observed in production, inferred, disputed, or unknown.

5. Comparison design
Use human-authorized criteria and comparable units, tiers, environments, workflows, and timeframes.

6. Product and journey analysis
Compare user outcomes, complete journeys, accessibility, agency, trust, failure, support, and recovery, not surface resemblance alone.

7. Technical and operational analysis
Examine architecture, dependencies, performance, reliability, security, privacy, deployment, support, and lifecycle only to the extent evidence allows.

8. Business and ecosystem analysis
Assess positioning, channels, pricing, partnerships, lock-in, regulatory posture, supply, and incentives.

9. Scenario and response analysis
Consider likely moves, market change, standards, provider shifts, incidents, and no-response options.

10. Adversarial review
Seek contradictory evidence, benchmark limitations, hidden costs, missing tiers, stale claims, and internal bias.

11. Decision synthesis
Provide implications, options, residual uncertainty, recommendation, rejected responses, and evidence that would change it.

12. Monitoring
Define lawful signals, source cadence, expiration, alert thresholds, and review authority.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

- AI collection must remain within authorized public or licensed sources and must expose the access method.
- The AI must not impersonate a person, customer, applicant, researcher, or employee to obtain information.
- The human must be able to inspect original source context behind every material comparison.
- Marketing claims and model-generated summaries must be visibly distinct from independent evidence.
- Comparative recommendations must use the organization's authorized mission and product criteria, not admiration or fear.
- Any external action based on intelligence remains a separate governed decision.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

The work must expose architectural obligations without treating a strategy, analysis, or plan as implemented architecture.

- Maintain source, subject, product, version, tier, environment, criterion, claim, and evidence identities.
- Keep competitor intelligence data isolated according to legal, licensing, confidentiality, and retention constraints.
- Support time-based invalidation and monitoring without silently overwriting historical findings.
- Separate intelligence collection from security testing, penetration testing, procurement, and external communication authority.
- Represent internal and external capability states using the same evidence discipline.
- Prevent unverified competitor claims from entering product requirements or marketing as facts.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

The owner-facing operating surface or equivalent durable record should expose:

- Intelligence question, subjects, decision owner, collection authority, methods, and prohibited actions.
- Source inventory, dates, versions, tiers, access, evidence states, and freshness.
- Comparison criteria, results, mismatches, uncertainty, and dissent.
- Signals, changes, alerts, monitoring cadence, and expiration.
- Strategic options, recommendations, human decisions, and rejected responses.
- Legal, privacy, security, licensing, and confidentiality status.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

- Competitive-intelligence charter and collection plan.
- Competitor, substitute, ecosystem, and signal map.
- Source and capability-evidence register.
- Comparable product, journey, technical, operational, and business analysis.
- Scenario, threat, opportunity, and response analysis.
- Decision synthesis and monitoring plan.
- Legal and ethical compliance record and continuity package.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

- Exact source, publisher, date, version, tier, configuration, environment, and access method.
- Evidence state for every competitor and internal capability claim.
- Independent test method and comparability where performance or quality is compared.
- Human-approved criteria and strategic response decisions.
- Legal, licensing, privacy, security, and collection-authorization records.
- Contradictory evidence, missing information, expiration, and monitoring history.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

- Trace each competitive claim to an original examined source and correct evidence state.
- Change version, tier, environment, or comparison criterion and verify results update or become incomparable.
- Remove marketing sources and inspect whether claimed differentiation remains supported.
- Attempt an unauthorized access, impersonation, or contact step; the workflow must block it.
- Compare an internal prototype to a competitor production claim and verify the mismatch is exposed.
- Expire a source and confirm dependent intelligence becomes stale or unknown.
- Verify no competitor finding automatically creates a product requirement or external claim.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

- Espionage, impersonation, unauthorized access, social engineering, or solicitation of protected information.
- Marketing pages, demos, rumors, or analyst summaries treated as verified capability.
- Apples-to-oranges comparison across tier, version, configuration, population, or environment.
- Benchmark fixation and metric gaming.
- Competitor-led product strategy and reactive feature copying.
- Copyright, license, trade-secret, privacy, contract, or terms violations.
- AI-fabricated sources, quotations, feature claims, pricing, incidents, or strategic intent.
- Stale intelligence retained as current truth.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Competitive-intelligence recovery withdraws invalid findings, contains legal or security exposure, and restores the source boundary.

- Stop affected collection or use and preserve evidence of the defect.
- Contain and report any unauthorized access, privacy, confidentiality, licensing, or security incident.
- Identify invalid source, subject, version, comparison, inference, or evidence classification.
- Retract or correct downstream strategy, product, procurement, or communication decisions.
- Recollect from lawful sources, narrow the claim, or mark the question unknown.
- Update monitoring, decision, and continuity records.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

- The question, subjects, methods, authority, criteria, timeframe, and prohibited actions are explicit.
- Sources are lawful, examined, current, and traceable.
- Claims, documentation, demonstrations, independent tests, inferences, and unknowns remain distinct.
- Comparisons are materially comparable or explicitly qualified.
- Recommendations remain mission- and user-grounded rather than competitor-controlled.
- Monitoring, expiration, evidence gaps, and human decisions are preserved.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

- Transfer source identities, versions, tiers, evidence states, criteria, findings, and expiration.
- Carry legal, licensing, privacy, security, and confidentiality constraints.
- Map implications to business strategy, product strategy, product management, feasibility, risk, or procurement.
- Preserve rejected responses, dissent, unknowns, and evidence that would change the recommendation.
- Identify monitoring signals and decision owner.
- State the safe boundary for downstream use.

SOURCE BASIS AND UNRESOLVED SOURCE GAPS

This draft applies the supplied Researcher requirements for primary sources, exact disagreement, dates, versions, terminology, and no invented citations; the Analyst requirements for consistent criteria, alternatives, uncertainty, sensitivity, and advisory conclusions; the Security Auditor and Red Team boundaries for lawful access and hostile review; V3 and V4 add source identity, objective fidelity, evidence, human authority, and continuity.

Unresolved source gap: the supplied corpus does not contain a dedicated competitive-intelligence legal standard or jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction collection policy. Every collection program must obtain current legal and contractual review where required and later reconcile with Threat Intelligence, Legal, Intellectual Property, Licensing, Privacy, and Security Operations.
