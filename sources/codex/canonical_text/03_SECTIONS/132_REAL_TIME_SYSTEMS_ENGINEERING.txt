SECTION 132 - Real-Time Systems Engineering

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define governed engineering of systems whose correctness depends on bounded response time, scheduling, deadlines, jitter, synchronization, or timely interaction with physical or operational processes.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A real-time baseline with timing classes, workloads, clocks, scheduling, resource budgets, safety margins, evidence, degradation, and recovery ownership.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Late output can be wrong output. Average latency hides deadline misses, priority inversion, overload, clock drift, and unsafe degraded behavior.

APPLICABILITY

Applies to hard, firm, or soft real-time software, control, communications, trading, telemetry, interactive, embedded, or operational systems.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

May be non-applicable where timing has no material correctness, safety, service, or user consequence and no deadline claim is made. Record scope, owner, evidence, and reopening trigger.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Requires timing requirements, clock and synchronization model, workload, hardware, scheduler, resource budgets, priority policy, safety and security constraints, measurement, and recovery authority.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Provide deadlines, periods, jitter, latency, load, clock, priority, resource, data, failure, safety, user, observability, and degraded-mode requirements.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Humans approve timing class, risk, priority, overload policy, safety margin, release, and acceptance. Humans decide whether a missed deadline is tolerable and when to stop operation.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may analyze traces, schedules, workloads, and test scenarios. It must not infer safety from averages, alter priority, claim deadline compliance, or change live scheduling without authority.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Architect, Analyst, Observer, Safety Auditor, Security Auditor, Implementer, Tester, Verifier, Governance Reviewer, Optimizer, Documentation Writer, Coordinator, Memory Curator, and Red Team. Existing role contracts remain controlling.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Classify timing and define deadlines, clocks, workloads, resources, and failure modes.
2. Design scheduling, synchronization, admission, overload, degradation, monitoring, and recovery.
3. Measure distributions and worst cases under representative and adversarial load.
4. Gate release, monitor deadlines, and retire obsolete timing assumptions.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show timing class, deadline, current load, clock, authority, evidence, and consequence. Human review is required for safety or material deadline exceptions; a status path remains available when automation fails.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Define clock source, synchronization, scheduling, priority, budget, admission, backpressure, timeout, safe state, and evidence capture. Keep control and observability paths bounded and independent.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show deadline distributions, misses, jitter, clock health, queue depth, CPU and memory, mode, evidence freshness, incidents, and controls to shed work, degrade, pause, or stop.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Timing model, workload and resource budget, scheduler design, measurement plan, deadline evidence, overload and recovery runbook, safety review, release record, and retirement assumptions.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify hardware, OS, build, clock, workload, configuration, sampling, percentile and worst-case method, result, and limits. Average latency never replaces deadline and miss evidence.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify clocks, scheduling, priority inversion, overload, queueing, jitter, deadline misses, resource exhaustion, dependency delay, fail-safe mode, recovery, and representative worst-case workload.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Using averages only; unbounded queues; priority inversion; synchronized timeouts; ignoring clock drift; overload without safe mode; and declaring timing correct from a lab run.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Reduce load, shed noncritical work, enter approved safe mode, preserve timing evidence, stop hazardous outputs, compensate effects where possible, and reopen budgets and acceptance.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Timing classes, budgets, evidence, controls, safe mode, ownership, and recovery are explicit. Keep CONTENT-COMPLETE until independent verification and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve timing baseline, workload, clock, build, traces, misses, decisions, evidence, and next safe action. Hand off to Runtime, Embedded, Control, Network, Performance, Safety, Reliability, Testing, and Operations.
