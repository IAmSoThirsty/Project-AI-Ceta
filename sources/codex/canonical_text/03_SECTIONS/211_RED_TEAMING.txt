SECTION 211 — Red Teaming

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Conduct authorized, adversary-oriented exercises that test whether people, processes, technology, facilities, and governance detect, resist, contain, communicate, and recover from realistic attack paths.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A governed red-team exercise with objective, threat scenario, authorization, scope, safety boundaries, independent control, rules, evidence, defender interaction, findings, impact, remediation, lessons, and owner decisions.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Security controls can pass isolated tests while failing as a system. Red teaming exposes assumptions and interactions, but it can also harm people or operations if secrecy, safety, privacy, legal authority, and stop conditions are weak. Exercise success is not proof of total resilience.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when an authorized adversary-emulation exercise can reveal material weaknesses in prevention, detection, response, decision-making, physical security, social process, or recovery.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability when the consequence, scope, or authority cannot support a safe exercise and a lower-risk test provides the required evidence, or when no material adversarial path is in scope. Record the alternative and reopening condition.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Require executive or accountable-owner authorization, objective and success criteria, independent exercise control, target and exclusion list, rules of engagement, legal and privacy review, safety contacts, emergency stop, data handling, and defender coordination appropriate to the scenario.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Threat model, intelligence, assets, people, environments, objectives, scenarios, constraints, access, test identities, communications, safety limits, evidence plan, response capability, and acceptance criteria.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Owners authorize objectives, scope, acceptable risk, secrecy, safety, communication, and stop conditions. Exercise control can halt activity. Humans decide whether physical, social, production, or high-impact actions are allowed and how affected people are protected.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may help analyze threats, draft scenarios, review authorized exercise evidence, identify control gaps, and propose safe tests. It must preserve authorization, target, identity, time, and handling boundaries. It must not autonomously conduct intrusion, deception, social engineering, persistence, exfiltration, destructive action, or scope expansion without explicit authorization and human supervision.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Red Team Lead, Security Auditor, Threat Intelligence Analyst, Threat Modeler, Researcher, Architect, Observer, Tester, Verifier, Governance Reviewer, Critic, Coordinator, Documentation Writer, and Memory Curator.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Define objective and scenario; obtain authority; establish control and safety; map targets and exclusions; prepare communications and stop paths; execute bounded emulation; record observations; protect participants and evidence; deconflict; analyze detection and response; remediate; retest; conduct post-exercise review.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show exercise authority, scenario, target, method, safety state, identity, evidence, and stop control. Require human approval for every escalation into physical, social, production, sensitive-data, or external-party activity. Maintain a reachable controller and immediate stop mechanism.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Separate exercise control, test identity, target, telemetry, evidence, communication, real incident response, and production authority. Preserve enough defender visibility to evaluate detection and enough safety isolation to prevent uncontrolled harm. Define how exercise artifacts expire and are destroyed.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show authorization, objective, scope, active phase, safety state, contacts, evidence, discoveries, impact, and defender status. Provide authorized pause, stop, narrow, deconflict, escalate, disclose, remediate, and close controls with an immutable timeline.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Exercise charter; authorization and rules; threat scenario; target and exclusion register; safety and communication plan; observation and evidence record; findings; remediation and retest; participant impact; decision, postmortem, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify authority, scenario, target, actor, method, time, environment, observation, source, impact, confidence, and limitation. Protect sensitive exercise details and personal information; do not retain operationally dangerous material beyond the approved need.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify authorization, scope, emergency stop, participant protection, evidence integrity, deconfliction, detection, response, communication, cleanup, remediation, retest, and owner acceptance. Confirm that exercise results are distinguished from real incidents.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Unapproved or indefinite exercise, unclear controller, unsafe secrecy, scope drift, harmful deception, uncontrolled persistence, real data exposure, confusing exercise with incident, and celebrating access without measuring detection or recovery.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Stop and declare an incident path on unexpected impact, exposure, or scope conflict. Preserve evidence, notify the controller and owner, clean up or restore, protect affected people, assess legal and operational consequences, and revise the model and rules before resuming.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Authority, objective, scenario, scope, safety, evidence, findings, defender behavior, remediation, retest, lessons, limitations, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending independent review and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve exercise identity, rules, timeline, findings, evidence references, impact, decisions, remediation, and open gaps with appropriate handling. Hand off to Security Operations, Blue Team, Threat Modeling, Vulnerability, Incident Response, Governance, Testing, and Owners.
