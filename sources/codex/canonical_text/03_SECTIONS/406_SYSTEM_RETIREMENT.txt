SECTION 406 — System Retirement

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Retire a system completely and responsibly when it no longer serves an approved purpose, replacing or ending it without losing required continuity, evidence, rights, safety, security, or accountability.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A system-retirement model with reason, scope, owner, users, dependencies, successor, data, records, access, contracts, finances, operations, migration, decommissioning, disposal, evidence, and final acceptance.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Retirement is more than shutdown. A retired system can leave data, credentials, certificates, DNS, backups, vendors, subscriptions, devices, support promises, undocumented dependencies, and people without a safe path forward. Declaring retirement without proving those boundaries creates hidden liability.

APPLICABILITY

Apply whenever a system, product, service, platform, application, process, device fleet, data capability, or organizational technology is permanently removed, replaced, or made unavailable for its prior purpose.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability when no system, service, asset, data, user, dependency, contract, access, operation, or lifecycle state is ending and an approved exact baseline covers the scope. A temporary pause, feature disablement, or environment shutdown is not automatically retirement.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Use lifecycle governance, sunset, deprecation, version, compatibility, migration, data retention, archival, preservation, disposal, deletion, account, service, infrastructure, hardware, legal, financial, security, privacy, support, and continuity baselines. An accountable retirement owner and final approval authority must be named.

REQUIRED INPUTS

- Retirement reason, decision authority, scope, dates, owner, users, affected parties, obligations, successor or accepted non-replacement, and acceptable residual risk.
- Complete system, service, application, platform, infrastructure, device, data, identity, certificate, DNS, network, vendor, license, subscription, billing, support, backup, archive, and monitoring inventory.
- Consumer and dependency map, migration and export requirements, accessibility and support plan, communication, incident and recovery state, legal holds, retention, and disposal rules.
- Evidence of current versions, configurations, active access, live endpoints, data copies, contracts, costs, and operational commitments.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

The accountable human owner:

- states why retirement is necessary and what purpose, population, and scope end;
- approves the successor, migration, export, support, communication, timing, risk, and exceptions;
- ensures users, operators, customers, suppliers, affected communities, and accountable specialists can raise concerns and obtain recovery or appeal;
- authorizes access revocation, subscription and contract closure, data retention or deletion, certificate and DNS cleanup, infrastructure destruction, hardware disposition, and final evidence review;
- confirms that legal holds, records, privacy, safety, financial, contractual, regulatory, licensing, accessibility, and environmental obligations are addressed;
- personally or through an authorized accountable body accepts the final retirement decision.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may inventory authorized system metadata, discover dependencies, compare successor options, draft migration and communication plans, identify stale access or unresolved artifacts, summarize evidence, and produce a bounded retirement checklist.

AI must preserve the difference between intended retirement, planned action, executed action, verified absence, and unresolved uncertainty. It must identify source, time, version, owner, and limitation for every material claim.

AI must not decide that a system should retire, declare a successor equivalent, announce closure, migrate or delete data, revoke access, disable services, destroy infrastructure or hardware, release legal holds, close contracts or billing, or certify complete retirement without explicit delegated authority, human review, audit, and recovery controls. It must not use retirement to erase evidence, debt, dissent, incidents, or unresolved ownership.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

System Retirement Planner, Architect, Lifecycle Governance Reviewer, Researcher, Analyst, Migration Engineer, Data Engineer, Security Auditor, Privacy Reviewer, Legal Advisor, Records Manager, Operations Engineer, Support Reviewer, Verifier, Critic, Coordinator, and Memory Curator. Select roles proportionally and preserve the role contracts defined in Section 02.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Establish the retirement objective, authority, scope, reason, dates, system identity, owner, successor, and non-goals.
2. Freeze or control consequential change while preserving the current baseline, evidence, records, configuration, and recovery option.
3. Inventory users, operators, customers, interfaces, jobs, data, copies, backups, archives, models, prompts, tools, identities, credentials, keys, certificates, DNS, network paths, vendors, contracts, licenses, subscriptions, billing, costs, devices, infrastructure, and monitoring.
4. Identify hidden, downstream, shared, third-party, physical, regulatory, safety, accessibility, environmental, and human dependencies.
5. Define migration, export, successor, coexistence, support, accessibility, communication, training, appeal, and recovery paths; test them with affected owners and representative users.
6. Apply retention, legal hold, preservation, archival, deletion, secure destruction, environmental disposal, and records controls to every data and asset class.
7. Close or transfer accounts, entitlements, subscriptions, billing, contracts, licenses, support commitments, vendor relationships, certificates, DNS, endpoints, scheduled jobs, and operational alerts.
8. Drain work, complete or cancel transactions, migrate or reconcile data, revoke access, disable service, remove infrastructure, dispose of hardware, and verify absence or residual state in controlled stages.
9. Monitor after shutdown for unexpected traffic, access, billing, data, alerts, user reports, dependency failures, and recovery requests during the defined observation period.
10. Conduct an independent final review, record unresolved gaps and accepted residual risk, update lifecycle and knowledge records, and authorize closure only when completion evidence is sufficient.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

- Show system identity, retirement phase, scope, owner, authority, affected people, dependencies, data state, active access, evidence freshness, and the next irreversible action.
- Require human confirmation before migration, shutdown, deletion, revocation, destruction, public communication, contract closure, and final completion.
- Preserve accessible user and operator status, support, appeal, export, recovery, and compensation paths through the transition.
- Separate observed absence from unobserved or unverified absence; a quiet dashboard, deleted resource, or successful script is not proof of complete retirement.
- Record material AI assistance, model or provider version, retrieved sources, generated recommendations, human decisions, executed tools, and limitations.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

- Represent the system, components, interfaces, identities, data, artifacts, dependencies, owners, contracts, evidence, and lifecycle states as versioned linked records.
- Provide dependency discovery, drain and shutdown sequencing, data export and reconciliation, access and key revocation, certificate and DNS cleanup, billing and contract closure, and post-shutdown observation.
- Preserve legal hold, records, archive, backup, recovery, and evidence boundaries; do not treat backup deletion, archive deletion, or cryptographic erasure as interchangeable without an explicit decision.
- Support staged retirement, coexistence, successor fallback, rollback or reopening window, and independent control when the retiring system is also the source of authority or recovery.
- Ensure retirement invalidates or requalifies affected tests, evidence, training, support, commitments, credentials, integrations, and owner cockpit state.
- Capture residual state such as retained records, external copies, vendor systems, immutable backups, shared infrastructure, environmental material, and unresolved risk.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show retirement reason, phase, system identity, owner, authority, successor, affected users, dependencies, data and record state, access, contracts, billing, support, evidence freshness, open findings, irreversible actions, recovery window, and final completion status.

Provide authorized controls to pause, extend, migrate, export, preserve, restrict, revoke, disable, restore, reopen, communicate, approve exception, and finalize. Every consequential control must show actor, authority, target scope, evidence, expected impact, reversibility, and recovery path.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

- Retirement charter and authority record.
- System, user, dependency, data, asset, identity, contract, vendor, financial, and obligation inventory.
- Successor, migration, export, accessibility, communication, support, training, and recovery plan.
- Data retention, archive, preservation, deletion, secure destruction, and environmental-disposition records.
- Access, key, certificate, DNS, infrastructure, device, subscription, billing, license, monitoring, and support closure records.
- Execution timeline, verification results, post-retirement observation, unresolved-gap register, residual-risk decision, final review, and continuity update.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Every material claim identifies system and version, environment, owner, scope, source, method, actor, timestamp, result, coverage, and limitation. Evidence may include inventories, dependency queries, access reports, migration reconciliation, export checks, hold checks, vendor certificates, billing closure, DNS and certificate scans, resource absence, hardware custody, user confirmation, and post-shutdown monitoring.

Evidence must distinguish planned, executed, observed, independently verified, stale, partial, contradictory, and unavailable states. “No users found,” “no traffic observed,” “resource deleted,” “invoice closed,” or “script succeeded” proves only the bounded observation.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

- Reconstruct the retirement scope, authority, baseline, successor, dependencies, affected people, and obligations without relying on chat history.
- Verify migration, export, data quality, retention, holds, archive, deletion, backup, and recovery for every declared data class.
- Verify account, identity, session, key, secret, certificate, DNS, endpoint, scheduled-job, service, vendor, license, subscription, billing, monitoring, and support closure.
- Verify infrastructure, storage, database, network, device, media, and environmental disposition with exact asset and custody evidence.
- Test successor and fallback journeys, accessibility, user communication, appeal, support, recovery, and post-shutdown observation.
- Search for unexpected access, traffic, dependencies, data, billing, alerts, copies, external references, and user reports during the defined observation period.
- Obtain independent review and accountable human acceptance of completion, residual risk, unresolved gaps, and records that remain intentionally retained.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

- Shutdown treated as retirement.
- Successor assumed equivalent without validation.
- Users, operators, customers, suppliers, or affected communities omitted.
- Data deleted before hold, retention, export, archive, or legal review.
- Access, keys, certificates, DNS, jobs, vendors, billing, licenses, or backups left active.
- Shared infrastructure or external copies missed.
- AI or automation performs irreversible retirement without human authority.
- Completion claimed from a checklist, green dashboard, deleted resource, or quiet support queue.
- Technical debt, incidents, dissent, decisions, or evidence erased with the system.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

On unexpected dependency, user harm, active access, traffic, billing, data, evidence, legal hold, safety issue, failed migration, or incomplete cleanup, pause further irreversible action and preserve all remaining state and evidence.

Identify the recovery owner, safe work that may continue, authority required, affected people, evidence needed, and review deadline. Reopen the system or successor path where feasible, restore or extend support, reconcile data, revoke unsafe access, correct public or customer communication, and reassess legal, privacy, financial, safety, environmental, and continuity impact.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Retirement reason, authority, scope, owner, users, successor, dependencies, data, records, access, contracts, finances, support, migration, decommissioning, disposal, evidence, post-shutdown observation, residual risk, unresolved gaps, and final acceptance are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE until independent verification and owner acceptance are separately completed.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve the retirement baseline, decision authority, inventory, migration, export, records, holds, archive, destruction, access revocation, contracts, billing, vendor, support, communication, incidents, evidence, exceptions, residual risk, and open gaps.

Hand off successor ownership, retained records, archive and preservation duties, support and appeal, security monitoring, financial close, legal obligations, environmental certificates, and the exact next safe action. State which sections must be reopened if data, users, dependencies, obligations, successor behavior, or retained systems change.
