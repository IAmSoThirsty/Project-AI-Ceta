SECTION 103 — Accessibility Design

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define inclusive experiences, interfaces, content, environments, and controls that people with varied abilities can perceive, understand, operate, and recover from.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

 - A scoped population, task, context, technology, access need, standard, risk, and lifecycle model.
 - Accessibility requirements, alternatives, evidence, tests, exceptions, and owner controls.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Accessibility is an outcome and a rights concern, not a final visual check. Exclusion often occurs in error, recovery, timing, content, and support paths.

APPLICABILITY

Applies to products, services, content, physical or digital interfaces, devices, documents, environments, and owner or operator controls.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

No material access, task, content, control, assistive, timing, or recovery effect; or an unchanged approved accessibility baseline with impact recorded.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Users and disability research, requirements, interaction and content models, target technology, applicable standards, devices, assistive tools, privacy, and acceptance authority.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Humans approve inclusion goals, standards, risk, accommodations, exceptions, resources, and acceptance. They must include affected people and retain correction, appeal, and stop authority.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

AI may identify barriers and generate test ideas. It must not infer disability, claim accessibility from heuristics, or replace lived-experience review and qualified testing.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may inspect interfaces, content, documents, and workflows for potential barriers; map findings to approved standards; propose equivalent alternatives; and generate bounded test cases for keyboard, assistive technology, sensory, cognitive, timing, and recovery paths. It must preserve user agency, privacy, dignity, access context, uncertainty, and the distinction between automated evidence and lived experience, and must identify where qualified accessibility review is required. It must not infer a person's disability, expose sensitive access information, declare conformance from heuristics alone, remove an accommodation, or make an inaccessible path the only path without authorized human review and recorded acceptance.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Accessibility Designer, User Experience Reviewer, Researcher, Interaction, UI, Content, and Visual Designers, Tester, Verifier, Governance Reviewer, Critic, Implementer, and Documentation Writer.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Define access needs and tasks; map barriers; set standards; design alternatives; test with assistive technologies and affected users; record exceptions; remediate; and retest.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show access mode, user control, state, actor, automation, alternative, consequence, and recovery. AI suggestions remain advisory; accessible non-AI paths remain first-class.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Support semantic structure, keyboard and alternative input, focus, contrast, text resizing, captions, audio description, timing, error, localization, privacy, and equivalent outcomes.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show population, standard, coverage, assistive technology, findings, exceptions, evidence, and controls to remediate, provide accommodation, restrict, or reopen.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Accessibility requirements, user and task records, standard mapping, design annotations, alternatives, test evidence, exception and accommodation records, decisions, and handoff.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify population, access mode, technology, standard, task, method, result, limitation, and date. Automated scans and conformance claims prove bounded coverage only.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify keyboard, screen reader, magnification, contrast, captions, timing, input, focus, language, error, recovery, cognitive load, physical access, and representative lived experience.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Treating a scan as acceptance; inaccessible recovery; color-only meaning; inaccessible authentication; accommodations hidden; and claiming compliance without version, scope, or evidence.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Provide equivalent path or accommodation, preserve user progress, remediate and communicate barriers, record exceptions, and retest before removing restrictions.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Population, tasks, standards, alternatives, evidence, tests, exceptions, recovery, risks, and acceptance authority are explicit. Remain CONTENT-COMPLETE pending review and acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve standards, findings, user evidence, assistive matrix, exceptions, decisions, evidence, and next action. Hand off to HCI, UX, UI, Content, Localization, Testing, Operations, and Governance.
