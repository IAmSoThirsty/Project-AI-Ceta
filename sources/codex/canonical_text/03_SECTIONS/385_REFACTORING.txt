SECTION 385 — Refactoring

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Change internal structure without changing intended external behavior while improving clarity, maintainability, testability, performance, security, or operability.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A refactoring model with invariant, baseline, scope, dependency, tests, increments, review, evidence, rollback, and explicit distinction from behavior change.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Refactoring can reduce debt but can also change behavior through hidden coupling, timing, errors, data, or security. “No behavior change” is a claim requiring evidence.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when internal code, architecture, schema, configuration, or structure changes with an intended stable external contract.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability when no internal structure, maintainability, or behavior-related change is made and an approved exact baseline covers the scope.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Use requirements, contracts, tests, architecture, configuration, dependency, quality, security, performance, and change baselines.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Invariant, current code, tests, dependencies, affected users, performance, security, scope, rollback, and acceptance criteria.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Owners and reviewers confirm invariant and risk; implementers preserve behavior; humans approve scope, test gaps, performance, security, and merge or release.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may propose mechanical refactors, map dependencies, draft tests, and analyze diff. It must not claim behavior preservation, alter protected interfaces, remove tests, or merge without human review.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Refactorer, Architect, Implementer, Tester, Verifier, Quality Engineer, Security Auditor, Analyst, Critic, and Documentation Writer.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

State invariant; baseline; map impact; make small changes; run tests; inspect behavior and performance; review security and data; compare; roll back if needed; and document residual risk.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show invariant, diff, dependencies, tests, results, evidence, uncertainty, and limitation. Require human review for semantic, public, security, data, and performance change.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Preserve contracts, migration, observability, rollback, version, test seams, data compatibility, and feature control. Keep refactor and behavior change separately traceable.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show refactor, invariant, diff, tests, failures, evidence freshness, owner, and authorized review, pause, rollback, merge, release, and reopen controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Refactoring charter; invariant and baseline; impact and diff; tests and results; review, rollback, decision, evidence, exception, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify baseline, invariant, source version, diff, test, environment, method, time, result, reviewer, and limitation. Passing tests do not prove no hidden behavior change.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify contract, tests, data, errors, performance, security, compatibility, observability, deployment, rollback, and user outcome. Test hidden coupling and boundary behavior.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Large rewrite called refactor, invariant unstated, tests removed, behavior change hidden, performance assumed, and mechanical transform accepted without review.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Rollback, preserve evidence, isolate behavior change, correct tests or implementation, communicate, and reassess scope. Reopen on failed invariant, dependency, contract, or user impact.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Invariant, baseline, scope, diff, tests, evidence, owners, rollback, limitations, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve refactor history, tests, diffs, decisions, evidence, exceptions, and open gaps. Hand off to Technical Debt, Quality, Security, Release, Operations, and Maintenance.
