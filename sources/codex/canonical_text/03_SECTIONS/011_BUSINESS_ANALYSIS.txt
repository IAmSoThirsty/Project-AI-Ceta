SECTION 011 — Business Analysis

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Establish a precise, evidence-grounded understanding of organizational needs, current and target states, actors, capabilities, processes, information, rules, decisions, constraints, and gaps before those needs are converted into requirements, architecture, or implementation.

Business analysis translates among mission, strategy, stakeholders, users, domain, operations, policy, and technology without silently replacing any of them. It identifies what must change and why while preserving the distinction between need, rule, requirement, solution, and decision authority.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- A bounded analysis question, scope, decision owner, affected parties, and current-state baseline.
- A stakeholder, actor, capability, process, information, decision, rule, and obligation model.
- A target-state definition expressed as outcomes and capabilities rather than an assumed solution.
- A gap and impact analysis covering people, process, information, technology, policy, risk, and operations.
- Options evaluated consistently with assumptions, dependencies, constraints, feasibility, risk, and reversibility.
- Traceable business needs and rules ready for domain analysis and requirements engineering.
- A decision and continuity package preserving unresolved questions, dissent, and evidence.

WHY THIS MATTERS

A request often arrives in solution language: build a dashboard, add AI, automate approval, migrate to the cloud. If the underlying need, rule, authority, or failure is not established, the system may implement the request perfectly and still make the organization less capable or less safe.

Business analysis prevents requirements from becoming a transcript of assumptions. It also protects human authority in AI-assisted work by ensuring that model-generated interpretations, process optimizations, and recommendations do not silently become organizational policy or decision rights.

APPLICABILITY

- A strategic, product, program, project, process, policy, or system change must be understood before design.
- Stakeholder statements conflict or use ambiguous business and domain terminology.
- Current workflows, rules, decisions, data, roles, or pain points are not adequately documented.
- A technology or AI proposal may alter work, authority, accountability, risk, or user outcomes.
- Requirements must be derived from real organizational outcomes and constraints.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

- The need, rules, current state, target state, and requirements are already authoritative, current, and unaffected by the task.
- The assignment is a bounded implementation or verification action with no business interpretation.
- The analyst lacks access or authority to examine the affected organization, process, policy, or evidence.
- The work is purely market or user research without an organizational decision question.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

- An authorized objective, analysis question, scope, decision owner, and intended downstream use.
- Relevant research, strategy, stakeholder, user, market, domain, and current-state sources.
- Access to authoritative policies, procedures, records, systems, data definitions, roles, and operational evidence.
- A method to preserve terminology, assumptions, decisions, conflicts, and source traceability.
- Privacy, consent, security, labor, legal, and access controls for organizational information.

REQUIRED INPUTS

- Mission, strategy, product, program, project, policy, and governance documents.
- Stakeholder and user needs, jobs, journeys, pain, workarounds, decisions, and recovery behavior.
- Current processes, roles, capabilities, systems, data, reports, controls, incidents, and performance evidence.
- Business rules, regulations, contracts, service obligations, approvals, exceptions, and escalation paths.
- Proposed target outcomes, constraints, resources, dependencies, and solution ideas.
- Known assumptions, risks, conflicts, and evidence gaps.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

The authorized human confirms organizational intent, policy, values, decision rights, and acceptable change. The analyst does not become the business owner.

- Define the decision or change the analysis must support.
- Identify authoritative owners for processes, rules, data, policy, and target outcomes.
- Correct terminology, undocumented practice, exception handling, and political or cultural context.
- Resolve value conflicts and approve material target-state or policy decisions.
- Authorize access to sensitive operational, personnel, customer, financial, or regulated information.
- Decide whether evidence is sufficient to convert a need into a requirement or commitment.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

The AI may structure, compare, model, and expose gaps. It may not invent business rules, authority, stakeholder consensus, data meaning, policy, or target-state approval.

- Preserve source wording and distinguish stated need, observed practice, inferred rule, policy, recommendation, and approved requirement.
- Identify contradictions, overloaded terms, exception paths, hidden approvals, and undocumented dependencies.
- Model current and target states without assuming the proposed solution is necessary.
- Separate measured performance from estimates and complaints from demonstrated root cause.
- Expose which conclusions depend on incomplete stakeholder access or stale organizational records.
- Avoid optimizing a process by removing controls, human judgment, accessibility, recovery, or legitimate exceptions.
- Route unresolved policy, value, and authority questions to the human owner.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Primary roles:

- Analyst — structures current state, target state, gaps, options, impacts, and decision implications.
- Researcher — verifies organizational, regulatory, market, policy, and source claims.
- Observer — records actual workflows, states, events, handoffs, and exceptions without unsupported causation.

Common supporting roles:

- Architect — identifies business capabilities, boundaries, interfaces, authority flows, and structural implications.
- User Experience Reviewer — examines human tasks, journeys, language, accessibility, agency, and recovery.
- Planner — sequences analysis, decisions, dependencies, validation, and handoff.
- Memory Curator — preserves canonical terminology, business rules, decisions, and change history.
- Decision Arbiter Advisory — compares options under human-authorized criteria.

Challenge and assurance roles where consequence warrants:

- Critic — attacks framing, hidden assumptions, omitted alternatives, and unsupported confidence.
- Verifier — checks artifact identity, evidence integrity, acceptance boundaries, and the exact status of claims.
- Governance Reviewer — checks objective fidelity, authority, delegation, policy, evidence, and human decision boundaries.
- Security Auditor — examines sensitive business information, access, fraud, control, and process-abuse risks.
- Red Team — attacks assumptions, process gaming, hidden incentives, bypasses, and harmful optimization.

Role selection must use the smallest sufficient set, preserve chamber independence where review is required, and leave every source-defined role contract unchanged.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Analysis framing
Record question, objective, scope, exclusions, decision owner, affected parties, constraints, and required confidence.

2. Source and terminology inventory
Identify authoritative documents, systems, data, owners, definitions, versions, dates, and conflicting terms.

3. Stakeholder and actor analysis
Map owners, users, operators, approvers, affected non-users, external parties, agents, responsibilities, authority, and interests.

4. Current-state analysis
Document actual workflows, capabilities, information, decisions, controls, handoffs, workarounds, exceptions, failures, and recovery.

5. Need and outcome analysis
Separate root need, desired outcome, symptoms, requests, constraints, and solution ideas.

6. Business-rule analysis
Record rule source, owner, condition, action, exception, priority, jurisdiction, effective date, and enforcement state.

7. Capability and gap analysis
Compare current capabilities to target outcomes and identify missing, weak, duplicated, or misaligned capabilities.

8. Option and impact analysis
Evaluate process, policy, organizational, manual, technical, AI, staged, and no-action options consistently.

9. Data and decision analysis
Identify required information, quality, provenance, timing, authority, decision logic, explainability, and appeal.

10. Risk and change analysis
Assess operational, human, legal, security, privacy, safety, accessibility, financial, and cultural impacts.

11. Validation
Review findings with authoritative owners and representative users; keep disagreement and unsupported assumptions visible.

12. Handoff
Produce traceable needs, rules, models, gaps, options, and decisions ready for domain analysis and requirements engineering.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

- AI analysis must distinguish what stakeholders said, what was observed, what was inferred, and what was approved.
- The human must be able to correct terminology and hidden organizational context without losing prior versions.
- The system must not treat management title, requester volume, or model confidence as proof of authority or need.
- AI-proposed process changes must show human, control, accessibility, exception, and recovery impacts.
- Conversational agreement must not become policy or requirement without durable validation and approval.
- Where AI is a process actor, capability, permission, decision role, review, fallback, and accountability must be modeled separately.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

The work must expose architectural obligations without treating a strategy, analysis, or plan as implemented architecture.

- Business capabilities, actors, processes, decisions, information, rules, and obligations should receive stable identities for traceability.
- Separate policy source, business rule, decision logic, workflow orchestration, UI guidance, and enforcement boundary.
- Represent normal, exception, denial, escalation, recovery, and termination paths.
- Keep capability and authority separate for human and AI actors.
- Identify source-of-truth systems, data lineage, freshness, quality, retention, and jurisdiction.
- Map business outcomes and rules to requirements, architecture, tests, evidence, and owner-facing status.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

The owner-facing operating surface or equivalent durable record should expose:

- Analysis question, scope, owner, affected parties, sources, and current evidence.
- Current and target states, capabilities, processes, rules, decisions, and gaps.
- Assumptions, contradictions, exceptions, risks, unknowns, and human decisions required.
- Options, criteria, impacts, recommendations, approvals, and rejected alternatives.
- Traceability from need and rule to requirement, design, implementation, and validation.
- Source freshness, stakeholder validation, and unresolved dissent.
- Change and reopening triggers.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

- Business analysis plan and question statement.
- Source, terminology, stakeholder, actor, responsibility, and authority maps.
- Current-state and target-state models.
- Process, capability, information, decision, and business-rule models.
- Gap, option, impact, feasibility, and risk analyses.
- Validated needs, outcomes, constraints, assumptions, and decision record.
- Requirements-ready traceability and continuity package.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

- Authoritative source identity, owner, version, date, jurisdiction, and applicability.
- Observed workflow, system, data, incident, performance, and exception evidence.
- Stakeholder and user findings with method, context, and limitations.
- Human approval for target outcomes, policy, rules, priorities, and value tradeoffs.
- Evidence supporting root-cause, gap, feasibility, and impact conclusions.
- Traceability from each material need and rule to source and validation state.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

- Trace a proposed requirement back to an approved need or rule and authoritative source.
- Present the same term to different stakeholders and verify ambiguity is detected and resolved or retained.
- Run current-state models through normal, exception, denial, failure, and recovery cases.
- Remove a proposed technology and confirm the underlying need and target outcome remain meaningful.
- Attempt to convert an inferred rule into a requirement without owner approval; it must be blocked.
- Verify AI capability does not imply business decision authority.
- Have authoritative owners and representative users validate the models and record disagreements.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

- Solution-first analysis that merely justifies a preselected technology.
- Stakeholder statements treated as facts, policy, consensus, or requirements without validation.
- Business rules invented from current software behavior or AI inference.
- Happy-path process maps that omit exceptions, denial, recovery, and escalation.
- Optimizing efficiency by removing legitimate human judgment, control, accessibility, or safety.
- Confusing capability, responsibility, permission, accountability, and authority.
- Documentation that describes intended process while actual operations differ.
- Handoff that loses source, terminology, assumptions, dissent, or decision ownership.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Business-analysis recovery returns to authoritative need, source, and actual current state when models or conclusions prove wrong.

- Pause dependent requirement or design decisions affected by the invalid analysis.
- Identify which source, term, observation, rule, process, assumption, or authority was wrong or changed.
- Reobserve actual work and reconcile documentation with system and human practice.
- Revalidate with authoritative owners and affected users.
- Revise target state, options, impacts, and requirements through change control.
- Propagate invalidation and updated evidence to downstream sections and continuity records.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

- The analysis question, scope, owner, sources, and affected parties are explicit.
- Current and target states, needs, capabilities, processes, rules, information, decisions, and gaps are traceable.
- Facts, observations, inferences, assumptions, recommendations, and approvals remain distinct.
- Normal, exception, failure, denial, recovery, and human-AI authority paths are represented where applicable.
- Options and impacts are evaluated without solution substitution.
- Validated outputs are ready for Domain Analysis and Requirements Engineering with unresolved conditions visible.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

- Transfer exact source identities, terminology, models, needs, rules, constraints, and validation state.
- Carry forward actor authority, exceptions, risks, accessibility, privacy, security, and operational impacts.
- Map each need and rule to intended domain or requirement work.
- Preserve disagreements, rejected options, assumptions, unknowns, and evidence needed to resolve them.
- Identify invalidation triggers and owners for future changes.
- State whether it is safe to proceed to domain analysis or requirements.

SOURCE BASIS AND UNRESOLVED SOURCE GAPS

This draft applies the supplied Researcher and Analyst disciplines for exact questions, source quality, definitions, current baselines, consistent comparison, alternative explanations, uncertainty, and advisory recommendations. It applies the Architect's requirement for responsibilities, interfaces, authority flows, trust boundaries, failure modes, and open decisions; the Owner's Cockpit adds owner jobs, language, journeys, evidence, recovery, and decision support without seizure; V3 and V4 add objective fidelity, claim typing, obligation mapping, evidence, dissent, and continuity.

Unresolved source gap: the supplied corpus does not mandate a particular business-analysis notation or methodology. BPMN, decision models, capability maps, value streams, use cases, and other techniques must be selected based on context and later reconciled with Domain Analysis, Requirements Engineering, Process Architecture, Data Modeling, and Policy.
