SECTION 077 — Experience Design

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define the end-to-end quality of a person's experience across touchpoints, channels, states, emotions, tasks, service interactions, and recovery conditions.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

 - A scoped experience journey, population, context, touchpoints, outcomes, risks, and lifecycle.
 - Evidence-backed experience principles, journey models, prototypes, content, controls, measurement, and acceptance.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Experience quality is produced across systems and handoffs, not by a single screen. Breaks in trust, accessibility, continuity, or recovery can outweigh local usability gains.

APPLICABILITY

Applies to products, services, workplaces, public systems, physical environments, digital channels, support, onboarding, and any multi-touchpoint journey.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

No material effect on user, operator, support, accessibility, trust, journey, or recovery outcome; or an unchanged approved experience baseline with impact recorded.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Population and stakeholder research, journey evidence, service and process models, content, brand or domain constraints, accessibility, privacy, security, operational dependencies, and acceptance authority.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Population and stakeholder research, journey evidence, service and process models, content, brand or domain constraints, accessibility, privacy, security, operational dependencies, and acceptance authority.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Humans define desired experience outcomes, inclusion, dignity, consent, acceptable burden, tradeoffs, and acceptance. They resolve conflicts and retain authority over commitments, disclosure, access, and redress.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may synthesize research and propose journeys or language, but must preserve participant context, uncertainty, and dissent. It must not infer emotion, consent, satisfaction, or universal preference from proxy data.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

User Experience Reviewer, Researcher, Analyst, Architect, Interaction Designer, Service Designer, Content Designer, Tester, Verifier, Accessibility specialist, Critic, Observer, Governance Reviewer, Documentation Writer, and Memory Curator.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Map populations and journeys; identify touchpoints, backstage dependencies, expectations, emotions, accessibility, failure, support, and recovery; prototype alternatives; test with representative people; baseline findings and decisions.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show journey stage, actor, system state, automation, expectation, consequence, uncertainty, permission, and recovery. AI suggestions remain proposals, and human support and non-AI paths remain available.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Connect frontstage and backstage behavior, service contracts, content, identity, data, operations, accessibility, localization, error, escalation, continuity, and feedback loops. Preserve experience across channels and transitions.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show population, journey, touchpoint, evidence, sentiment data limits, accessibility, open findings, active incidents, and controls to correct, stop, escalate, appeal, and recover.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Experience principles, journey maps, service blueprints, personas or population records, prototypes, content and interaction specifications, research evidence, measurement plan, decision register, and handoff.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify population, context, journey, touchpoint, method, sample, date, result, limitation, and affected-party protection. Ratings and analytics are bounded signals, not universal experience truth.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify end-to-end task completion, continuity, comprehension, accessibility, trust, privacy, support, failure, recovery, channel consistency, and outcomes with representative and edge populations.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Optimizing a single touchpoint; hiding backstage labor; treating satisfaction as consent; designing for an average user; breaking continuity; inaccessible recovery; and counting engagement as benefit.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Protect participants, pause harmful journeys, preserve evidence, offer human support and alternate channels, correct misleading content, and retest affected populations. Reopen when service, population, policy, channel, or evidence changes.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Journey scope, outcomes, population, touchpoints, dependencies, inclusion, risks, evidence, tests, recovery, open findings, and acceptance owner are explicit. Remain CONTENT-COMPLETE pending reconciliation, hostile review, and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve research, journey versions, blueprints, decisions, participant limits, evidence, unresolved gaps, and next action. Hand off to HCI, Interaction, Service, UX, Content, Operations, Accessibility, and Testing.
