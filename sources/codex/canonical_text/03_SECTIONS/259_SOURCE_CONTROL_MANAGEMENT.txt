SECTION 259 — Source Control Management

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Preserve the identity, history, collaboration, review, authorization, integrity, and recoverability of source, configuration, documentation, infrastructure, and other controlled change.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A source-control model with repositories, ownership, branches, commits, review, access, signing, protection, provenance, retention, release linkage, recovery, and retirement.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Source history is evidence and an execution input. Unreviewed changes, lost provenance, exposed repositories, force pushes, generated material, or weak recovery can compromise delivery and accountability.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when versioned files, code, configuration, documentation, infrastructure, tests, models, data definitions, or artifacts are collaboratively changed or released.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability when no controlled source, history, review, release, or recovery claim is affected and an approved exact baseline covers the work.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Use identity, access, IP, licensing, secrets, build, release, records, backup, and security baselines. Identify repository owners, branch policy, review authority, retention, and emergency change.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Repositories, content, authors, ownership, branches, commits, reviews, protections, dependencies, secrets scan, release linkage, access, retention, and recovery.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Owners approve repository policy, protected changes, reviewers, access, emergency bypass, retention, and deletion. Humans review meaning, security, IP, and release risk; authors remain accountable for truthful change description.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may inspect authorized history, draft changes, tests, messages, and review summaries. It must preserve authorship and provenance, not impersonate approval, rewrite history, expose secrets, merge, delete, or bypass branch protection without explicit authority.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Source Control Manager, Implementer, Reviewer, Security Auditor, IP Analyst, Tester, Verifier, Researcher, Critic, and Documentation Writer.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Inventory repositories; assign owners; define access and protection; capture changes; review; scan; link to issues, builds, and releases; back up; recover; handle emergency changes; audit; and retire repositories with records.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show repository, ref, author, change, review, protection, evidence, and intended effect. Require human approval for merge, protected branch bypass, secret or IP handling, release, and deletion.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Use immutable or auditable history, protected branches, signed commits or equivalent provenance where warranted, review gates, access segregation, secret scanning, backup, and release traceability.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show repository health, owners, changes, reviews, protection, findings, release links, evidence freshness, and authorized approve, reject, merge, revert, restrict, recover, and retire controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Repository inventory and policy; access and protection model; review and provenance record; scan and build links; backup and recovery; exception, decision, evidence, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify repository, ref, commit, author, reviewer, time, source, change, method, result, and limitation. A commit proves history, not correctness, authorization, or safe deployment.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify access, protection, review, provenance, secret and license scanning, revert, backup, restore, release linkage, audit, and emergency change. Test unauthorized push, lost branch, compromised account, and recovery.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Shared accounts, force push without authority, unreviewed protected changes, secrets in history, source without owner, generated code without provenance, and repository deletion without records.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Restrict compromised access, preserve history, rotate secrets, revert or restore known-good refs, investigate, notify owners, and reassess downstream artifacts. Reopen on source, author, dependency, policy, or release change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Repositories, owners, access, history, review, protection, provenance, releases, recovery, evidence, exceptions, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending review and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve repository identity, refs, history, reviews, findings, releases, decisions, backups, evidence, exceptions, and open gaps. Hand off to Build, Release, Security, IP, Licensing, Records, Platform, and Operations.
