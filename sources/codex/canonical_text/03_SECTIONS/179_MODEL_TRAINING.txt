SECTION 179 — Model Training

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define governed preparation, training, fine-tuning, preference optimization, checkpointing, and selection of model artifacts with explicit data, objective, compute, security, evaluation, and release boundaries.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- A training plan connecting authorized purpose, data, objective, configuration, compute, artifacts, and evaluation.
- Reproducible runs with lineage, resource, contamination, privacy, security, and failure evidence.
- Controlled checkpoint selection and handoff; training is not deployment approval.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Training can encode data defects, leakage, unsafe capability, bias, or hidden memorization. A lower loss or preferred output does not prove the model serves its authorized purpose safely.

APPLICABILITY

Applies to pretraining, fine-tuning, transfer learning, reinforcement or preference optimization, distillation, quantization-aware training, and retraining. Depth increases with sensitive data, scale, autonomy, or external use.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

May be non-applicable to using an unchanged external model with no training or artifact change, or conceptual discussion with no run claim. Record scope and reopening trigger.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Requires data rights and quality, objective authority, compute and supply-chain controls, evaluation, safety, privacy, security, and model-operations gates. Depends on Sections 167-169, 180-184.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Training objective; data and license; population; labels or preference source; architecture; configuration; compute; budget; split; contamination controls; safety constraints; evaluator; checkpoint policy; and release authority.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Humans approve purpose, data use, objective, resource budget, safety limits, evaluation gate, checkpoint selection, publication, and residual risk. Training staff cannot expand use or declare alignment.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may draft datasets, code, configurations, schedules, and diagnostics. It must preserve run identity and cannot invent metrics, hide failed runs, or select a checkpoint for deployment without authorized review.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Architect, Researcher, Data Scientist, Implementer, Optimizer, Security Auditor, Privacy Reviewer, Safety Auditor, Tester, Verifier, Critic, Red Team, Governance Reviewer, Documentation Writer, and Memory Curator.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Define purpose, objective, prohibited capability, data, and release boundary.
2. Validate data lineage, rights, quality, representation, contamination, and split.
3. Record code, configuration, hardware, software, seeds, budget, and dependencies.
4. Run training with checkpoint, failure, resource, and access controls.
5. Evaluate generalization, robustness, subgroup, privacy, security, safety, and memorization.
6. Compare checkpoints against baseline and document selection rationale.
7. Hand off only with evidence and explicit next approval.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Expose run, data, objective, configuration, checkpoint, metrics, failures, cost, uncertainty, and reviewer. AI-generated training changes remain proposals. Preserve pause, cancel, evidence, access, and safe checkpoint handling.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Version datasets, loaders, code, libraries, hardware, configuration, seeds, checkpoints, logs, metrics, evaluators, and policies. Protect artifacts and secrets; enforce lineage, retention, access, and immutable run records.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show training state, data, objective, resources, checkpoints, metrics, failures, risks, authority, and evidence. Provide authorized pause, cancel, quarantine, revoke, compare, select, archive, and retire controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Training charter; data and rights register; objective and configuration; reproducible environment; run and checkpoint register; evaluation report; safety, privacy, and security review; selection decision; and handoff.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Each claim identifies run, data, split, objective, configuration, hardware, checkpoint, metric, evaluator, result, uncertainty, and limitation. Loss, reward, or preference score is not deployment or safety evidence by itself.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Reproduce a run; verify data split and contamination; test checkpoint integrity, dependency changes, memorization, robustness, subgroups, privacy, security, safety, and failed-run visibility; independently review selection.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Untracked data; test leakage; hidden failed runs; metric gaming; checkpoint confusion; unsafe data reuse; unauthorized compute; silent retraining; and training success treated as production readiness.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Freeze affected runs or checkpoints on data, artifact, compute, security, or safety failure. Preserve evidence, use last qualified artifact only if still valid, investigate, re-evaluate, and reopen when data, objective, or population changes.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Purpose, data, objective, run, artifact, resources, evaluation, risks, selection, recovery, evidence, and authority are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending reconciliation and acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve runs, checkpoints, data, code, environments, metrics, decisions, failed attempts, dissent, incidents, and gaps. Hand off to ML Engineering, Deep Learning, Evaluation, Alignment, MLOps, Safety, and Model Operations.
