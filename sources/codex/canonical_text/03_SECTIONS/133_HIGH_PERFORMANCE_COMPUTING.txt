SECTION 133 - High-Performance Computing

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define governed engineering for computation requiring substantial throughput, latency, memory, accelerator, energy, or scale optimization while preserving correctness, reproducibility, safety, and authorized resource use.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

An HPC baseline with workload, correctness, resource allocation, scaling, performance evidence, cost and energy limits, reproducibility, operations, and recovery.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Optimization can change numerical results, portability, cost, and fairness of resource access. A fast answer that cannot be reproduced or trusted is not an accepted outcome.

APPLICABILITY

Applies to clusters, supercomputers, accelerators, batch systems, large simulations, analytics, rendering, training, and throughput-critical services.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

May be non-applicable where workload and resource demand are bounded and no performance, scaling, accelerator, or capacity claim is made. Record scope, owner, evidence, and reopening trigger.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Requires workload definition, correctness criteria, hardware, scheduler, data, algorithms, numerical tolerance, resource and cost policy, security, reproducibility, and operations authority.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Provide problem size, input data, baseline, target, hardware, parallelism, memory, I/O, energy, cost, precision, failure, checkpoint, and acceptance requirements.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Humans approve workload priority, resource allocation, correctness tolerance, cost, energy, data, and acceptance. Humans decide tradeoffs between speed, fidelity, access, and impact.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may profile, propose algorithms, optimize code, and draft experiments. It must not trade correctness for speed silently, consume scarce resources, or claim reproducibility without captured evidence.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Architect, Analyst, Researcher, Implementer, Optimizer, Tester, Verifier, Security Auditor, Governance Reviewer, Observer, Documentation Writer, Coordinator, Memory Curator, and Red Team. Existing role contracts remain controlling.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Define workload, correctness, resources, constraints, and baseline.
2. Profile computation, memory, I/O, communication, energy, cost, and bottlenecks.
3. Optimize with controlled experiments, reproducible environments, validation, and resource gates.
4. Operate, checkpoint, recover, compare, and retire workloads and hardware assumptions.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Expose workload, code, compiler, hardware, allocation, data, method, result, and confidence. Human review is required for correctness and scarce-resource decisions; stop and checkpoint controls remain independent of AI.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Represent workload, scheduler, nodes, accelerators, memory, storage, network, checkpoint, result, and evidence boundaries. Isolate jobs, capture environment, and plan for node loss and partial output.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show job, owner, allocation, queue, progress, resource use, performance, cost, energy, checkpoint, evidence, and controls to cancel, suspend, requeue, recover, or release resources.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Workload specification, baseline profile, resource and cost plan, reproducible environment, optimization experiments, correctness evidence, checkpoint design, runbook, and retirement record.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify code, compiler, hardware, input, seed, precision, scheduler, configuration, measurement, result, uncertainty, and limitations. Separate benchmark, correctness, scalability, energy, and outcome evidence.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify correctness, numerical tolerance, reproducibility, scaling, resource limits, node loss, checkpoint, restart, data integrity, security isolation, cost, energy, and output acceptance.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Benchmarking unlike workloads; optimizing a proxy; nonreproducible environments; hidden precision loss; monopolizing resources; ignoring I/O; and treating throughput as useful outcome.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Stop or checkpoint jobs, preserve partial results and environment, requeue safely, validate outputs, release resources, compensate external effects, and reopen performance and correctness claims.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Workload, correctness, resource, evidence, reproducibility, recovery, ownership, and retirement are explicit. Keep CONTENT-COMPLETE until independent verification and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve job and environment identity, inputs, seeds, profiles, outputs, decisions, evidence, incidents, and next safe action. Hand off to Parallel, Scientific, Simulation, Platform, Performance, Capacity, Testing, and Operations.
