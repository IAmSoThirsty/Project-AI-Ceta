SECTION 184 — MLOps

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define the governed delivery and lifecycle practice for data, models, code, evaluation, deployment, monitoring, incident response, retraining, and retirement.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- Reproducible, traceable promotion of ML artifacts through controlled environments.
- Gates for data, evaluation, security, safety, policy, approval, observability, rollback, and compensation.
- Continuous evidence that distinguishes pipeline health from model or outcome validity.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Automation can accelerate an unapproved or invalid model into production. MLOps must preserve evidence and authority across frequent data, code, model, and infrastructure changes.

APPLICABILITY

Applies to ML build, registry, validation, release, serving, monitoring, retraining, promotion, rollback, incident, and retirement workflows. Depth increases with autonomy, continuous learning, sensitive data, or external effect.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

May be non-applicable to static non-ML software or an offline experiment with no pipeline, promotion, or operational claim. Record exact boundary and reopening trigger.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Requires data and model contracts, evaluation, governance, security, privacy, safety, observability, operations, and owner authority. Depends on Sections 167, 179, 180, 183, 185, 253, and 266-270.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Pipeline; artifacts; data; registry; environments; tests; evaluation gates; policy result; capability; trace; deployment strategy; monitoring; incident; rollback or compensation; and retirement conditions.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Humans approve promotion gates, exceptions, retraining, production changes, risk, and retirement. Operators may stop pipelines and revoke authority; automation cannot waive failed gates.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may generate pipeline code, diagnose failures, and recommend promotion. It must preserve logs and evidence and cannot approve its own output, suppress failures, or mutate environments without a complete binding.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Architect, Implementer, DevOps Engineer, Tester, Verifier, Governance Reviewer, Security Auditor, Privacy Reviewer, Safety Auditor, Critic, Optimizer, Planner, Documentation Writer, Memory Curator, and HITL Operator.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Define artifact, environment, promotion, and evidence contracts.
2. Version source, data, features, model, code, image, configuration, and dependencies.
3. Enforce quality, evaluation, security, safety, policy, and approval gates.
4. Promote through reproducible stages with immutable records and traces.
5. Monitor data, model, service, policy, cost, and outcome drift.
6. Exercise incident, pause, rollback configuration, fallback, compensation, and retirement.
7. Review pipeline changes and reopen invalidated claims.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show pipeline state, artifact lineage, gate result, policy evaluation, decision, authority, trace, and environment. Human approval is explicit for consequential promotion. Preserve stop, evidence, and manual recovery if automation fails.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Use immutable artifact and decision identities, append-only events, reproducible environments, segregated credentials, signed or equivalent provenance, and default-deny promotion. Keep rollback distinct from compensation for external effects.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show pipeline, model, data, gates, risks, drift, environment, approvals, traces, incidents, and evidence freshness. Provide authorized pause, deny, revoke, quarantine, promote, fallback, compensate, and retire controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

MLOps charter; pipeline and environment map; artifact registry; gate and policy records; deployment and promotion logs; monitoring and alert plan; incident and compensation runbook; decision and handoff package.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Each promotion claim identifies source, data, model, code, environment, gates, policy result, decision, trace, actor, result, and limitation. Green pipelines prove execution, not validity, safety, or accepted outcome.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Break data, tests, evaluator, registry, credentials, policy, capability, deployment, monitor, provider, and stream termination; verify promotion denies, evidence remains, alerts fire, and recovery follows approved controls.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Auto-promotion on weak metrics; mutable registry; missing lineage; silent retraining; test bypass; shared credentials; green pipeline equals model quality; and destructive rollback of governed history.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Pause affected pipeline or deployment on gate, artifact, policy, drift, or authority failure. Preserve records, use prior qualified or manual mode, compensate external effects, re-evaluate, and resume only with fresh authorization.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Artifacts, environments, gates, policy, decisions, traces, monitoring, incident, recovery, compensation, and retirement are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending reconciliation and acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve pipeline IDs, artifacts, environments, logs, decisions, bindings, traces, incidents, drift, dissent, and gaps. Hand off to Model Operations, Training, Evaluation, Security, Governance, Operations, and Retirement.
