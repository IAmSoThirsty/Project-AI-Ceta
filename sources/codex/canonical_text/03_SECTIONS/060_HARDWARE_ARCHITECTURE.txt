SECTION 060 — Hardware Architecture

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define the physical components, interfaces, materials, energy, thermal, mechanical, electrical, manufacturing, service, safety, security, and lifecycle structure of hardware that realizes approved outcomes.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- A hardware context with components, interfaces, tolerances, environments, suppliers, manufacturing, service, failure, evidence, owners, and lifecycle.
- Traceability from requirements through electrical, mechanical, embedded, software, testing, operation, maintenance, disposal, and retirement.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Physical design fixes constraints that software cannot wish away. Tolerance, heat, energy, supply, materials, manufacturing variation, serviceability, and disposal determine whether a design is safe, reliable, maintainable, and honest about its limits.

APPLICABILITY

- Physical components, equipment, devices, materials, energy, packaging, or facilities realize a material function or interface.
- Hardware failure, variation, manufacture, service, supply, environment, or disposal affects consequence.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

- No physical component, material, interface, energy, environment, supplier, manufacturing, service, or lifecycle effect changes.
- An approved baseline covers exact hardware, environment, and change with no material impact.
- The artifact is conceptual or simulated and explicitly not a certification or field-readiness claim.

Record hardware, scope, owner, evidence date, assumptions, environment, and reopening trigger.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

- Sections 019-059 provide requirements, software, embedded, robotics, safety, security, resilience, and operations context.
- Hardware, electrical, mechanical, manufacturing, supplier, environmental, safety, service, and disposal authorities are known or open.

REQUIRED INPUTS

- Functions, loads, materials, dimensions, tolerances, power, thermal, environmental, interfaces, users, manufacturing, maintenance, and lifecycle.
- Safety, security, reliability, accessibility, testing, certification, supply, repair, recycling, disposal, and evidence requirements.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Hardware and product owners approve design, materials, tolerances, suppliers, manufacturing, safety, service, exceptions, certification, and retirement. Humans retain authority to halt manufacture, quarantine, recall, repair, replace, dispose, and retire.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may analyze authorized designs, tolerances, bills of material, test data, and supplier evidence, and draft alternatives or tests. It must preserve revision and physical context. It must not approve a design, substitute material, change manufacturing, certify safety, or direct physical action without authority.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

- Architect, Researcher, Analyst, Security Auditor, Tester, Verifier, Governance Reviewer, Critic, Planner, Implementer, Documentation Writer, Memory Curator, and Red Team.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Define product, environment, loads, users, physical effects, owners, suppliers, and lifecycle.
2. Decompose components, materials, interfaces, energy, thermal, mechanical, electrical, and environmental constraints.
3. Analyze tolerance, variation, failure, hazards, service, manufacturing, supply, security, and disposal.
4. Allocate controls for safety, reliability, accessibility, embedded integration, testing, evidence, and recovery.
5. Validate with qualified hardware, electrical, mechanical, manufacturing, safety, security, service, and affected-user authorities.
6. Test prototype, production, environmental, stress, fault, aging, service, recovery, and disposal conditions.
7. Baseline revision, evidence, decisions, dissent, residual risk, and gaps.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

- Show component, revision, material, supplier, load, environment, authority, evidence, and physical consequence.
- Require human approval for material, supplier, tolerance, safety, manufacturing, recall, and irreversible changes.
- Preserve manual inspection, stop, quarantine, traceability, evidence, repair, and recovery paths.
- Do not treat optimization, simulation, or generated design as certification.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

- Separate requirement, design, drawing, revision, prototype, production unit, field configuration, test, and evidence.
- Define interface, tolerance, energy, thermal, material, manufacturing, calibration, service, and disposal semantics.
- Preserve configuration and serial traceability through manufacturing, field service, recall, replacement, and retirement.
- Protect hardware and design records, supplier data, diagnostics, and evidence under access and retention controls.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

- Show hardware revision, serial or batch, components, materials, supplier, environment, health, authority, evidence, and lifecycle.
- Expose inspect, quarantine, repair, replace, recall, update, restore, dispose, and retire actions with approval.
- Explain unsafe, out-of-tolerance, unknown, service, and recovery conditions.
- Export drawings, revisions, test results, decisions, evidence, incidents, dissent, and continuity state.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

- Hardware context, component and interface model, materials and tolerance record, environmental and failure analysis, supplier and manufacturing plan.
- Safety, security, embedded integration, testing, service, repair, recall, disposal, evidence, acceptance, and retirement package.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

- Claims identify hardware revision, unit or batch, material, configuration, environment, load, method, date, result, and limitation.
- Simulation, drawing, prototype, supplier statement, certification, inspection, or field test proves only its declared scope.
- Production, environmental, safety, reliability, service, and disposal evidence identifies sample and coverage.
- Missing revision, provenance, manufacturing, or field evidence constrains status.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

- Trace requirements through components, interfaces, manufacturing, embedded behavior, physical outcome, evidence, service, and retirement.
- Exercise tolerance, thermal, power, vibration, corrosion, aging, misuse, component loss, supplier change, counterfeit, repair, and disposal.
- Verify revision control, material identity, safety, accessibility, security, serviceability, recall, and evidence integrity.
- Reproduce representative claims from exact drawings, unit or batch, configuration, and test package.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

- Treating a drawing, prototype, simulation, supplier claim, or certification as universal field proof.
- Ignoring manufacturing variation, tolerance stack, thermal, supply, service, counterfeit, disposal, or physical access.
- Allowing AI or schedule pressure to substitute materials, skip tests, or conceal out-of-tolerance units.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

- On defect, hazard, counterfeit, supplier change, evidence loss, or out-of-tolerance condition, quarantine affected units and preserve traceability.
- Identify owner, authority, affected users, communication, evidence, service, and review condition.
- Use inspection, repair, replacement, recall, redesign, compensation, secure disposal, or retirement.
- Reopen on component, material, supplier, revision, environment, firmware, policy, or lifecycle change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

- Components, interfaces, materials, tolerances, suppliers, manufacturing, environment, evidence, service, recovery, and lifecycle are explicit.
- Fault, variation, recall, repair, disposal, embedded, and retirement paths are addressed or justified.
- Exact limits and unresolved evidence are recorded; CONTENT-COMPLETE does not imply certified or field-ready hardware.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

- Preserve revisions, units, batches, materials, suppliers, drawings, tests, incidents, decisions, evidence, dissent, and gaps.
- Hand off to Embedded, Software, Robotics, Electrical, Mechanical, Manufacturing, Security, Safety, Testing, Operations, and Retirement.
- Record changes requiring reopening affected architecture, certification, and lifecycle baselines.
