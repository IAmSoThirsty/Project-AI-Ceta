SECTION 199 — Hardware Security

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Protect hardware components, boards, manufacturing processes, physical interfaces, supply-chain integrity, device identity, and hardware-assisted trust across the lifecycle.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A hardware-security baseline covering assets, suppliers, provenance, tamper resistance, debug and service access, trust roots, physical exposure, secure provisioning, monitoring, replacement, and disposal for the declared product and environment.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Hardware is difficult to patch after deployment and can expose secrets or bypass software controls. A trusted design can be undermined by counterfeit components, altered firmware, open debug ports, insecure manufacturing, untracked substitutions, or physical access that the threat model ignored.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when hardware, boards, chips, modules, peripherals, manufacturing, provisioning, service ports, physical enclosures, or component supply chains affect identity, confidentiality, integrity, availability, safety, or recovery.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability only when no hardware asset, physical interface, component trust, supplier, manufacturing, device identity, or lifecycle behavior is affected and an approved exact baseline supports that conclusion.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Use hardware, embedded, firmware, cryptography, key, identity, safety, supplier, vulnerability, testing, operations, and disposal baselines. Identify component owners, procurement controls, bill of materials, manufacturing and provisioning facilities, service authority, and physical threat assumptions.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Hardware inventory, schematics, layouts, bill of materials, component sources, substitutions, trust anchors, manufacturing steps, provisioning records, interfaces, enclosure, service access, threat model, safety constraints, and disposal requirements.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Owners approve component sources, substitutions, physical access, debug and service policy, provisioning, residual risk, field response, replacement, and disposal. Hardware, manufacturing, supply-chain, security, safety, operations, and service authorities must be assigned.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may analyze authorized bills of materials, schematics, supplier records, configurations, test results, and provenance evidence; identify inconsistencies; and draft controls, inspections, and test plans. It must preserve component and revision identity and distinguish evidence from inference. It must not approve substitutions, alter provisioning, expose secrets, change device trust, or authorize physical intervention without delegation.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Architect, Hardware Engineer, Embedded Engineer, Security Auditor, Supply Chain Analyst, Researcher, Observer, Implementer, Tester, Verifier, Governance Reviewer, Critic, Red Team, Documentation Writer, and Memory Curator.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Inventory components and revisions; classify trust and criticality; verify provenance and substitutions; secure manufacturing and provisioning; control debug and service interfaces; assess tamper and physical threats; test identity and trust roots; monitor field changes; define replacement and disposal; record exceptions and evidence.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show component, revision, supplier, facility, device, environment, and evidence boundaries. Require human approval for source changes, substitutions, provisioning, debug access, field containment, or disposal. Do not turn a parts database, scan, or AI recommendation into a trust decision without owner review.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Separate component provenance, manufacturing, provisioning, device identity, firmware trust, service access, physical enclosure, and operational trust boundaries. Use tamper evidence or resistance where justified, protect trust roots, minimize exposed interfaces, and make component changes traceable and reversible where possible.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show hardware identity, revision, component provenance, supplier, posture, debug state, evidence freshness, open findings, field population, and authorized controls to quarantine, inspect, replace, recover, or retire. Explain physical and safety impact before action.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Hardware asset and component inventory; provenance and substitution record; threat model; secure manufacturing and provisioning design; interface and debug policy; inspection and test results; exception, incident, replacement, disposal, decision, evidence, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify device or component, revision, supplier, facility, source, method, environment, timestamp, result, owner, coverage, and limitation. Provenance, inspection, scan, certification, or bench evidence must state what it does not establish.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify bill of materials, provenance, substitution approval, identity, trust anchors, debug control, interface exposure, tamper response, provisioning, service access, replacement, and disposal. Test representative variants and hostile physical conditions within authorized scope.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Counterfeit or untraceable parts, undocumented substitutions, shared manufacturing credentials, exposed debug ports, unprotected trust roots, trust based on appearance, and treating one inspected device as representative of an uncontrolled population.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Quarantine affected lots or devices, preserve component and manufacturing evidence, revoke compromised trust, stop unsafe deployment, and use approved inspection, replacement, re-provisioning, or retirement. Reopen on supplier, component, revision, facility, physical threat, or field-condition change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Hardware assets, components, suppliers, trust boundaries, provisioning, interfaces, threats, owners, evidence, tests, recovery, disposal, limitations, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending independent verification and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve bill of materials, revision history, supplier decisions, provisioning records, findings, evidence, exceptions, field impact, recovery, replacement, and open gaps. Hand off to Embedded, Firmware, Supply Chain, Security Operations, Testing, Safety, Operations, and Disposal.
