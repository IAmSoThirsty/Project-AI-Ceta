SECTION 330 — Failover Engineering

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Transfer service, traffic, workload, or control from a failed or degraded component to an alternate while preserving safety, integrity, authority, and recovery evidence.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A failover model with trigger, authority, primary and alternate, data state, synchronization, health, sequencing, split-brain control, return, rollback, and evidence.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Failover can duplicate authority, lose data, route users to an unready target, or make recovery harder. Redundancy without tested control and reconciliation is not resilience.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when a service, network, data store, region, provider, device, or control plane has an alternate path or instance.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability when no alternate, continuity, dependency, or availability claim changes and an approved exact baseline covers it.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Use availability, reliability, continuity, DR, backup, restore, network, database, identity, application, and operational readiness baselines.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Primary, alternate, trigger, authority, data sync, health, traffic, dependencies, sequence, split-brain prevention, return, and acceptance.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Owners approve trigger, failover, data loss, priority, communication, return, and residual risk. Humans can stop unsafe or duplicate operation.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may analyze health and sync metadata, draft failover plans, and recommend action. It must not fail over, switch traffic, promote data, or declare alternate ready without authority.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Failover Engineer, SRE, DR Planner, Network Engineer, Database Administrator, Operations Engineer, Tester, Verifier, Security Auditor, and Incident Commander.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Define trigger and authority; validate alternate; maintain synchronization; test; detect failure; declare; sequence failover; monitor; reconcile; return or retire primary; and record.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show primary, alternate, trigger, health, data point, authority, traffic, evidence, and limitation. Require human approval for declaration, promotion, data, routing, and return.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Use independent failure domains, fencing, leader election, quorum, data consistency, health, traffic control, identity, observability, rollback, and split-brain prevention.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show primary and alternate, sync, health, mode, traffic, data loss, evidence freshness, owner, and authorized declare, fence, fail over, reconcile, return, and retire controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Failover charter; primary and alternate; trigger and authority; sync and health; exercise and runbook; reconciliation, decision, evidence, exception, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify primary, alternate, trigger, version, data point, authority, method, time, result, traffic, integrity, and limitation. A healthy standby proves neither safe promotion nor current data.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify trigger, fencing, sync, health, identity, traffic, data integrity, failover, user outcome, return, rollback, and operator response. Test split brain and stale standby.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Both sides active, standby untested, stale data, failover loop, traffic switched without health, no fence, return without reconciliation, and redundancy treated as recovery.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Fence unsafe primary, preserve evidence, fail over by priority, reconcile, communicate, restore or replace, and reassess data and service impact. Reopen on dependency, sync, trigger, topology, or recovery change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Primary, alternate, trigger, authority, sync, fencing, health, traffic, recovery, evidence, owners, limitations, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve failover records, sync, health, incidents, exercises, decisions, evidence, exceptions, and open gaps. Hand off to DR, Restore, Backup, Network, Database, SRE, and Operations.
