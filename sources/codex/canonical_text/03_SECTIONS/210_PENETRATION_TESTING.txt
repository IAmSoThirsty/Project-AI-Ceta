SECTION 210 — Penetration Testing

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Perform authorized, bounded security testing that validates whether real systems, controls, configurations, interfaces, and recovery paths resist declared attack scenarios.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A scoped penetration-test plan and evidence package with authorization, targets, exclusions, safety limits, methods, observations, findings, impact, reproducibility, remediation, retest, and owner acceptance.

WHY THIS MATTERS

A penetration test can reveal control interaction and real exposure that reviews or scanners miss, but an unsafe or poorly scoped test can cause outage, data exposure, legal conflict, or false confidence. A clean result is bounded by scope, time, method, and tester access.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when authorized adversarial testing can materially validate security of applications, networks, devices, APIs, identities, cloud resources, facilities, or operational controls.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability when testing would add no material information beyond current verified evidence, the scope is non-consequential and reversible, or authorization and safe test conditions cannot be established. The determination must name the alternative evidence and reopening condition.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Obtain written authorization, target and exclusion list, rules of engagement, contacts, safety and privacy controls, data handling, legal review where needed, rollback and stop conditions, environment access, and incident or support coverage.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Objective, scope, assets, versions, environments, identities, test accounts, allowed methods, exclusions, schedule, rate limits, data constraints, safety limits, threat model, prior findings, evidence, and acceptance criteria.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Owners authorize scope, methods, timing, data access, stop conditions, risk, and communication. Test leads protect evidence and people. Humans decide whether testing may affect production, whether a finding requires immediate containment, and whether residual risk is accepted.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may help draft scope, test cases, evidence plans, code or configuration review, and bounded analysis of authorized results. It must preserve authorization and target boundaries, avoid secret exposure, and distinguish observed behavior from inference. It must not initiate testing, expand scope, exploit, persist, exfiltrate, alter, or publish findings without explicit authorized execution and human oversight.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Security Auditor, Penetration Tester, Architect, Researcher, Analyst, Implementer, Observer, Tester, Verifier, Governance Reviewer, Critic, Red Team, Documentation Writer, and Memory Curator.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Define objective and rules; verify authorization; inventory and validate targets; prepare safety and stop controls; execute bounded tests; record evidence; triage findings; notify urgent risk; clean up; remediate; retest; report limitations; preserve continuity.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Display authorization, target, method, account, environment, time, rate, evidence, and stop state before consequential activity. Require human approval for scope, production testing, data access, exploit execution, containment, and disclosure. Provide immediate stop and escalation paths.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Separate test identity, test control, target, telemetry, evidence, notification, rollback, and production authority. Ensure tests are attributable, rate-limited, reversible where possible, isolated from real data, and visible to defenders without invalidating the declared exercise.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show authorization, scope, active test, targets, methods, safety state, findings, evidence, contacts, and impact. Provide authorized start, pause, stop, narrow, escalate, clean up, approve remediation, retest, and close controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Authorization and rules of engagement; target and exclusion register; test plan; evidence and data-handling plan; findings and severity rationale; cleanup, remediation, retest, exception, decision, evidence, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify authorization, tester or agent, target, version, environment, method, timestamp, account, observed result, evidence location, impact, reproduction boundary, and limitation. Do not retain unnecessary sensitive data or weaponized material.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify authorization and scope before start; exercise stop, rate, privacy, cleanup, and evidence controls; reproduce material findings safely; validate remediation and retest; confirm no unauthorized target or data was affected; and obtain owner acceptance of limitations.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Testing without authorization, scope drift, production impact without approval, uncontrolled exploit or data collection, unannounced testing that defeats safety, clean result treated as total security, and findings published without handling controls.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Stop immediately on unsafe, out-of-scope, or unexpected impact; preserve state and evidence; notify the owner and incident path; clean up or restore under authority; assess data and user impact; and reopen findings or scope when evidence changes.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Authorization, scope, methods, safety, findings, evidence, limitations, remediation, retest, owner decisions, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending independent review and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve rules, target identity, test timeline, findings, evidence references, cleanup, remediation, retest, exceptions, and open gaps. Hand off to Security Operations, Vulnerability, Threat Modeling, Red Team, Blue Team, Incident Response, Testing, and Governance.
