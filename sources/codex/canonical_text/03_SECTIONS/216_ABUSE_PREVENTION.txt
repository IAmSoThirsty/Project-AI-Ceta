SECTION 216 — Abuse Prevention

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Prevent, detect, investigate, and respond to misuse of systems, products, communities, resources, or capabilities while preserving legitimate access, due process, safety, and evidence.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

An abuse-prevention model with abuse taxonomy, affected parties, signals, reporting, triage, user protection, enforcement, appeal, escalation, privacy, evidence, recovery, and continuous review.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Abuse is contextual and often targets people with less power. Overbroad controls can silence legitimate use, expose reporters, or punish victims; weak controls can allow harassment, exploitation, fraud, or unsafe behavior to continue.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when users, operators, suppliers, agents, or external actors can misuse a product, platform, process, facility, data set, or organizational capability to harm people, property, systems, or trust.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability when no user, public, resource, or trust relationship can be abused within scope and an approved exact baseline documents the conclusion. Do not use absence of reports as proof of absence.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Use trust and safety, privacy, security, fraud, legal, ethics, accessibility, support, incident, and governance baselines. Define prohibited harm, reporting authority, vulnerable populations, response, appeal, and emergency escalation.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Abuse taxonomy, user journeys, reports, signals, content or behavior scope, identity and access, risk, policy, reviewer capacity, victim support, evidence, legal duties, and recovery resources.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Owners define protected outcomes, prohibited abuse, enforcement, evidence, reporter protection, appeal, emergency response, and residual risk. Trained humans review high-impact action, vulnerable-person cases, ambiguity, and appeals.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may classify authorized reports, surface patterns, draft queues and safety explanations, and recommend bounded review. It must preserve context, reporter privacy, uncertainty, cultural and accessibility limits, and appeal. It must not label a person an abuser, expose a reporter, remove content, close an account, or make an emergency determination without delegated human authority.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Trust and Safety Reviewer, User Experience Reviewer, Researcher, Analyst, Security Auditor, Privacy Reviewer, Ethics Reviewer, Incident Responder, Tester, Verifier, Governance Reviewer, Critic, and Documentation Writer.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Define abuse and harm; map affected parties; establish reporting and evidence handling; triage and protect; investigate with proportionality; enforce or remediate; communicate; support victims; provide appeal; monitor evasion and bias; test; review policy and outcomes.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show report context, source, confidence, policy, action, user impact, reviewer, and appeal. Protect reporters and accused people from unnecessary disclosure. Require human review for irreversible or high-impact action and preserve emergency escalation.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Separate reporting, evidence, classifier or rule, review, enforcement, appeal, support, and audit. Minimize personal data, protect reporter identity, support regional and accessibility needs, and prevent adversarial input from directly causing irreversible action.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show abuse category, risk, queue, affected parties, evidence freshness, action state, reviewer, policy version, appeals, and authorized protect, restrict, reverse, escalate, notify, and retire controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Abuse taxonomy and policy; reporting and triage design; reviewer and victim-support model; enforcement and appeal procedures; privacy and fairness assessment; case, test, decision, evidence, recovery, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify report, subject, context, source, time, policy, reviewer, action, confidence, impact, and limitation. Reports and model classifications are allegations or signals until reviewed and bounded.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Test reporting, privacy, triage, context, false positives, evasion, reviewer load, accessibility, emergency escalation, enforcement, reversal, appeal, victim support, and evidence retention. Verify legitimate use is not systematically blocked.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Punishing reporters, opaque enforcement, no appeal, context-free classification, proxy discrimination, public accusation, irreversible automation, reviewer burnout, and treating low report volume as low abuse.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Protect affected people, preserve evidence, pause harmful controls, reverse unjustified actions, provide support and appeal, notify owners, and reassess policy or classifier drift. Reopen on new harm, evasion, policy change, or systematic disparity.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Abuse scope, affected parties, reporting, review, enforcement, appeal, privacy, evidence, recovery, owners, tests, limitations, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending independent verification and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve policy and classifier versions, reports, cases, decisions, appeals, support, evidence, incidents, exceptions, and open gaps with appropriate privacy controls. Hand off to Trust and Safety, Privacy, Security, Legal, Support, Testing, and Governance.
