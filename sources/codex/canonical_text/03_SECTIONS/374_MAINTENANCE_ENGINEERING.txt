SECTION 374 — Maintenance Engineering

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Keep systems, products, facilities, services, data, and processes safe, supported, reliable, secure, compliant, and useful throughout their operating life.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A maintenance model with asset, condition, schedule, preventive and corrective work, dependencies, parts, skills, safety, evidence, backlog, change, and retirement.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Maintenance competes with delivery and can be deferred until failure. Hidden technical debt, unsupported components, unsafe work, and maintenance windows can accumulate operational risk.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when a system, product, infrastructure, device, service, data pipeline, or process requires planned or unplanned work to remain fit for use.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability when no ongoing support, repair, update, inspection, condition, or lifecycle obligation exists and an approved exact baseline covers it.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Use operational, asset, quality, security, reliability, change, supply, safety, records, and retirement baselines.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Asset, condition, work, schedule, failure, parts, skills, dependencies, safety, window, evidence, backlog, and owner.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Maintenance owners prioritize work, protect safety, approve windows, parts, change, risk, backlog, and retirement. Humans decide when deferral is unacceptable.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may analyze authorized condition and work records, identify trends, and draft plans. It must not infer safety, schedule critical work, change equipment, or close maintenance without authority.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Maintenance Engineer, Reliability Engineer, Operations Engineer, Hardware Engineer, Software Engineer, Safety Reviewer, Planner, Tester, Verifier, and Documentation Writer.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Inventory assets; assess condition; plan preventive, corrective, and adaptive work; secure window; execute; test; document; manage backlog and parts; review; and retire.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show asset, condition, work, safety, dependency, window, evidence, owner, and limitation. Require human approval for safety, production, destructive, access, and retirement action.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Use maintenance state, asset history, service mode, redundancy, backup, rollback, spare or substitute, condition telemetry, and evidence linked to change.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show asset health, maintenance due, backlog, safety, dependency, evidence freshness, owner, and authorized schedule, defer, execute, verify, escalate, and retire controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Maintenance charter; asset and condition; work plan; safety and window; execution and test; backlog, decision, evidence, exception, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify asset, condition, work, version, method, time, operator, result, safety, and limitation. Maintenance completion proves work occurred, not restored fitness.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify asset, condition, procedure, safety, parts, access, execution, test, rollback, evidence, backlog, and return to service. Test deferred and failed maintenance.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Maintenance deferred forever, work without safety, no asset history, completion by ticket, unsupported part, change untested, and maintenance burden hidden.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Protect asset and people, isolate, repair or substitute, preserve evidence, test, communicate, and reassess service and safety. Reopen on failure, condition, part, design, or lifecycle change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Assets, condition, work, schedules, owners, safety, evidence, backlog, verification, recovery, limitations, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending qualified acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve asset history, work, tests, parts, incidents, decisions, evidence, exceptions, and open gaps. Hand off to Corrective, Preventive, Adaptive, Security, Operations, Quality, and Retirement.
