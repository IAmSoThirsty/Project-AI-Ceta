SECTION 108 — Computer Science

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define the governed use of computational models, abstractions, theories, and methods to understand, design, analyze, and verify systems without confusing a mathematical result with an implemented or acceptable system.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

The result is a bounded computational question, model or proof basis, assumptions, complexity analysis, reproducible method, evidence, limitations, and handoff to engineering or architecture.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Computer science provides the abstractions used to reason about computation, information, algorithms, languages, systems, and limits. Unstated assumptions can make a formally valid result irrelevant to real users, data, resources, security, or lifecycle conditions.

APPLICABILITY

Applies when computational complexity, correctness, formal models, algorithms, data structures, languages, concurrency, distributed behavior, information representation, or theoretical limits affect a design or decision.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Not applicable to a purely descriptive or bounded editorial change with no computational claim, model, method, requirement, implementation, or evidence consequence. Record the exact scope and reopening trigger.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Requires an authorized question, domain and requirements context, declared assumptions, relevant data or formal definitions, qualified review, and a reproducible method. Related architecture, security, privacy, safety, and performance constraints must be available or marked unresolved.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Question, definitions, inputs, constraints, model, proof or experiment method, expected properties, complexity target, resource limits, threat assumptions, evidence standard, and acceptance authority.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Humans set the question, acceptable abstraction, values, constraints, interpretation, risk, and acceptance. They approve claims that affect architecture, policy, safety, rights, or investment and ensure qualified independent review.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may explain concepts, propose models, derive examples, check reasoning, generate experiments, and identify counterexamples. It must label conjecture, proof, simulation, and empirical result separately; it must not invent a proof, hide assumptions, or approve consequential conclusions.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Architect, Researcher, Analyst, Critic, Verifier, Tester, Security Auditor, Governance Reviewer, Documentation Writer, Memory Curator, Planner, and Teacher. Existing role contracts remain controlling.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Define the problem, abstraction, scope, assumptions, and non-goals.
2. Select notation, models, data structures, computational resources, and correctness properties.
3. Analyze feasibility, complexity, termination, determinism, concurrency, information loss, and adversarial conditions.
4. Prove, model-check, simulate, implement a reference, or experimentally evaluate as appropriate.
5. Seek counterexamples, boundary conditions, sensitivity, and independent review.
6. Record results, limitations, unresolved gaps, and implications for implementation and operations.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Human review is required for model selection, assumption acceptance, proof claims, security or safety conclusions, and decisions affecting people or resources. AI output remains advisory until independently checked.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Keep formal model, reference implementation, production implementation, data, experiment, and evidence separate. Record computational resource limits, nondeterminism, precision, reproducibility, and invalidation conditions. Preserve fallback and human interpretation for consequential results.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show question, model version, assumptions, method, proof or experiment status, evidence freshness, limitations, reviewer, and actions to reproduce or challenge the result. Provide controls to quarantine, supersede, or reopen claims.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Problem statement, formal definitions, model, proof or analysis, reference code, experiment record, complexity and resource analysis, counterexamples, review record, limitations, and engineering handoff.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Evidence identifies definitions, model version, inputs, method, environment, seed or nondeterminism, resource limits, reviewer, result, and excluded claims. Missing or inconclusive evidence is not success.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Reproduce the proof or experiment; vary assumptions; test boundary and adversarial inputs; compare model and implementation; measure resource use; independently challenge the conclusion; verify handoff requirements and invalidation triggers.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Unstated assumptions, asymptotic claims treated as production performance, simulation treated as proof, proof treated as user acceptance, ignored resource limits, and AI-generated reasoning accepted without checking.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Preserve the prior claim and evidence when a counterexample, assumption change, or reproducibility failure appears. Narrow use, rerun analysis, obtain qualified review, or supersede through an attributable decision; do not erase the failed result.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

The question, model, assumptions, method, result, limits, review, acceptance authority, and engineering implications are explicit, reproducible within declared conditions, and free of unrecorded material gaps.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Hand off model identity, definitions, assumptions, results, counterexamples, evidence, decisions, dissent, open work, and reopening conditions to Algorithm, Software, Data, AI, Security, Testing, and Architecture sections.
