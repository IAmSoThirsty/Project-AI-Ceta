SECTION 171 — Natural Language Processing

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define governed engineering of systems that process, classify, retrieve, translate, summarize, generate, or reason over human language while preserving meaning, provenance, privacy, accessibility, and human authority.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- A language task and contract with population, languages, context, source, output, error, and review boundaries.
- Evaluation for meaning, robustness, subgroup and language coverage, privacy, security, and usability.
- Traceable deployment, correction, refusal, fallback, and retirement paths.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Language varies by dialect, culture, domain, power, and context. A fluent or high-scoring output can still misrepresent intent, exclude speakers, disclose private content, or cause harm.

APPLICABILITY

Applies to classification, extraction, search, translation, question answering, dialogue, summarization, sentiment, generation, moderation, and language interfaces. Depth increases with rights, safety, employment, health, legal, or public impact.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

May be non-applicable to deterministic string handling with no semantic, user, or decision effect, or exploratory work with no operational claim. Record scope and reopening trigger.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Requires language definitions, source rights, data lineage, domain review, evaluation, privacy, security, accessibility, and human oversight. Depends on Sections 097, 170, 180, 182, 186, and 188.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Task; language and dialect coverage; user population; corpus; labels; context; tokenizer; model; output schema; error costs; bias plan; privacy rules; translation and accessibility needs; evaluation; and fallback.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Humans approve language scope, labels, prohibited uses, publication, moderation thresholds, appeals, and residual risk. Native speakers, domain experts, and affected communities must review material language decisions.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may annotate, transform, generate, and evaluate language within scope. It must preserve source and language context, disclose uncertainty, and must not invent translation, consent, meaning, or citations.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Researcher, Analyst, Domain Expert, Implementer, Prompt Engineer, UX Reviewer, Accessibility Reviewer, Privacy Reviewer, Security Auditor, Safety Auditor, Tester, Verifier, Critic, Red Team, Documentation Writer, and Governance Reviewer.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Define task, language, dialect, audience, context, and prohibited use.
2. Audit corpus rights, representation, labels, annotation quality, and leakage.
3. Define semantic, structural, safety, privacy, and accessibility contracts.
4. Evaluate per-language and subgroup behavior, ambiguity, drift, and adversarial input.
5. Validate review, correction, appeal, refusal, and fallback.
6. Monitor distribution, errors, user impact, and model or provider changes.
7. Baseline and hand off evidence and unresolved language gaps.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show source language, transformation, model, context, confidence limits, review state, and citation. Users must correct or contest language output and access a safe non-AI path. No fluency score substitutes for meaning review.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Version corpora, annotations, tokenizer, model, prompts, translation memory, retrieval, policy, output schema, and evaluator. Preserve character and locale handling, provenance, access, redaction, trace, and fallback across interfaces.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show language coverage, model, source, output, uncertainty, quality, subgroup results, incidents, reviewer, and freshness. Allow authorized restrict, correct, regenerate, withdraw, quarantine, fallback, revoke, and retire actions.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Language task charter; corpus and rights register; label guide; model and prompt record; per-language evaluation; bias and accessibility review; output and appeal contract; deployment record; incident plan; and handoff package.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Claims identify language, dialect, corpus, model, context, task, metric, subgroup, method, result, reviewer, and limits. Aggregate scores hide gaps unless disaggregated. Missing language or population evidence is UNKNOWN.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Test ambiguity, dialect, code-switching, translation, long context, malformed input, prompt injection, privacy, toxicity, accessibility, refusal, correction, appeal, provider loss, and per-language regression. Independently reproduce claims.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

English-only generalization; label leakage; dialect erasure; fluency mistaken for accuracy; translation hallucination; hidden moderation bias; unsupported language claims; and irreversible language decisions without appeal.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Restrict affected language or task on harmful errors, rights impact, drift, source loss, or evidence failure. Preserve inputs and outputs, notify owners, use human review or fallback, correct downstream records, and reopen after corpus or population change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Task, language scope, data, model, evaluation, controls, review, accessibility, privacy, recovery, evidence, and authority are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending reconciliation and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve corpus, annotations, model, prompts, evaluations, translations, traces, decisions, incidents, dissent, and gaps. Hand off to Generative AI, Content, Localization, Speech, Evaluation, Safety, Governance, and HITL Operations.
