SECTION 116 — Middleware Engineering

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define the engineering of shared middleware that coordinates applications, services, messaging, transactions, identity, serialization, and cross-cutting controls.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A versioned middleware contract with supported patterns, interfaces, compatibility, security, observability, failure, recovery, performance, and ownership.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Middleware concentrates hidden coupling. A default serializer, retry, transaction, or identity behavior can affect every consumer and make failures systemic.

APPLICABILITY

Applies to brokers, RPC layers, service meshes, transaction managers, serializers, workflow engines, caches, configuration services, and shared libraries.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

An application using an unchanged approved middleware component is not middleware engineering; record the consumed baseline and reopening trigger.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Requires consumer contracts, workload, data, trust model, delivery semantics, failure model, compatibility, capacity, observability, support, and acceptance authority.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Interfaces, schemas, identity, ordering, delivery, retries, idempotency, transactions, timeouts, backpressure, quotas, telemetry, upgrades, and recovery.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Humans approve shared defaults, security, compatibility, service commitments, risk, release, and deprecation. Consumer owners remain accountable for local consequences.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may draft adapters, schemas, tests, and analyses. It must not change shared semantics, retry or identity policy, or production configuration without delegated approval and evidence.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Architect, Analyst, Researcher, Implementer, Tester, Verifier, Critic, Security Auditor, Governance Reviewer, Optimizer, Documentation Writer, Memory Curator, and Red Team. Existing role contracts remain controlling.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Inventory consumers and define shared contracts and ownership.
2. Specify delivery, identity, schema, error, retry, transaction, ordering, and compatibility behavior.
3. Implement observability, backpressure, quotas, isolation, and safe degradation.
4. Test failures, duplicates, reordering, partial work, upgrades, and recovery.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Expose middleware and consumer identity, data, policy, authority, version, and outcome. Require human review for shared behavior and preserve direct or manual fallback.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Separate middleware definition, configuration, runtime state, consumer contract, message or transaction, and evidence. Design idempotency, dead-letter, replay, compatibility, and compensation.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show service, version, consumers, queues, dependency, capacity, failure, evidence, and recovery state with authorized pause, drain, replay, isolate, and rollback controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Middleware contracts, schemas, ownership matrix, configuration, compatibility, load and failure tests, security review, observability, migration, runbooks, and retirement records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify consumer, message or transaction, version, configuration, workload, environment, result, reviewer, and limitations. Nominal delivery does not prove end-to-end outcome.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Test duplicate, loss, delay, reorder, poison data, timeout, retry storm, partition, schema change, unauthorized access, overload, replay, drain, restore, and compensation.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Unbounded retries, hidden coupling, incompatible schema change, shared credentials, lost messages, unowned dead letters, and rollback that duplicates external effects.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Pause or isolate, preserve messages and evidence, drain or quarantine, use compatible prior behavior, replay or compensate idempotently, and reopen affected consumer contracts.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Contracts, semantics, ownership, capacity, security, observability, compatibility, recovery, evidence, support, and retirement are explicit and verified for consumers.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Hand off contracts, schemas, configuration, consumer inventory, evidence, incidents, decisions, migration, recovery, and open gaps to Integration, API, Event, Application, Platform, Security, Testing, and Operations sections.
