SECTION 131 - Distributed Systems Engineering

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define governed engineering of systems whose state, computation, authority, or outcome spans multiple processes, machines, regions, networks, or organizations.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A distributed-systems baseline with ownership, consistency, timing, partition, identity, coordination, observability, recovery, and evidence boundaries.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Networks fail, clocks disagree, messages duplicate, and nodes diverge. Designs that assume a single reliable state create data loss, split authority, and unreproducible incidents.

APPLICABILITY

Applies to distributed storage, services, actors, queues, clusters, replicated state, multi-region systems, and federated operations.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

May be non-applicable to a single-process, single-state artifact with no distributed execution or external coordination claim. Record scope, owner, evidence, and reopening trigger.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Requires consistency and availability objectives, failure model, topology, clocks, identity, data ownership, contracts, capacity, observability, recovery, security, and operational authority.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Provide nodes, regions, messages, state, ordering, quorum, latency, partitions, retries, duplication, tenancy, security, data, recovery, and user-impact requirements.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Humans approve consistency tradeoffs, quorum, data authority, risk, recovery objectives, regions, cost, and acceptance. Humans decide conflict resolution and external communication.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may model failures, inspect traces, propose tests, and compare designs. It must not invent guarantees, change consensus or data authority, or execute recovery without delegation.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Architect, Analyst, Observer, Security Auditor, Implementer, Tester, Verifier, Governance Reviewer, Coordinator, Documentation Writer, Memory Curator, Optimizer, Critic, and Red Team. Existing role contracts remain controlling.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Define topology, state, actors, trust, authority, consistency, timing, and failure model.
2. Design communication, coordination, partition behavior, idempotency, reconciliation, and recovery.
3. Implement with fault injection, tracing, staged rollout, and operational readiness.
4. Measure outcome, repair divergence, migrate, and retire safely.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Expose node, region, leader, quorum, message, state, authority, and evidence context. Human approval is required for failover and conflict decisions; status and safe halt remain available during automation failure.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Model partial failure, clock uncertainty, retries, deduplication, ordering, leader election, quorum, replication, isolation, backpressure, tracing, and compensation. Separate local health from global outcome.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show topology, role, state freshness, quorum, lag, partitions, conflicts, capacity, traces, incidents, and controls to isolate, fail over, reconcile, restore, or narrow operation.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Topology and failure model, consistency contract, data authority, protocol records, test and fault-injection evidence, observability plan, recovery runbook, migration, and retirement package.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify topology, revision, node, message, clock, workload, configuration, method, trace, result, and limitation. Preserve partition, duplicate, stale, divergent, and recovered evidence.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify partition, duplication, reordering, clock skew, node loss, leader loss, quorum loss, replay, failover, reconciliation, recovery, migration, security, and user outcome.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Assuming reliable delivery or clocks; shared mutable state without authority; unbounded retries; split-brain; opaque failover; and treating all replicas healthy as correct outcome.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Freeze risky writes, isolate divergent nodes, preserve logs and state, select approved authority, reconcile and compensate effects, communicate impact, and reopen guarantees before resuming.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Failure model, guarantees, ownership, controls, evidence, recovery, operations, and retirement are explicit. Keep CONTENT-COMPLETE until independent verification and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve topology, protocol, state, traces, incidents, decisions, evidence, and next safe action. Hand off to Distributed Architecture, Microservices, Runtime, Network, Data, Reliability, Resilience, Testing, and Operations.
