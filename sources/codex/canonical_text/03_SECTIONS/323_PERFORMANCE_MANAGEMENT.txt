SECTION 323 — Performance Management

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Manage response, throughput, resource, latency, cost, and user-experience performance over time through objectives, monitoring, capacity, change, and improvement.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A performance-management model with service and user outcomes, indicators, objectives, workload, baselines, budgets, ownership, action, and review.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Performance changes with workload, dependency, data, configuration, and time. A one-time test or average can miss tail behavior and can encourage optimization that harms correctness or cost.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when performance affects users, operators, service levels, cost, capacity, safety, or business outcome.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability when no performance, workload, cost, or user outcome changes and an approved exact baseline covers it.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Use performance tests, metrics, SRE, capacity, architecture, cost, service levels, and operations baselines.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Outcome, workload, indicators, objective, baseline, latency, throughput, resource, cost, dependency, incidents, and action.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Owners define meaningful performance and tradeoffs; engineers manage causes; humans decide scale, degrade, cost, and acceptance.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may analyze authorized performance data, identify bottlenecks, and propose bounded tuning. It must expose context and not change production or optimize a proxy without authority.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Performance Engineer, SRE, Capacity Engineer, Architect, Analyst, Operations Engineer, Tester, Verifier, Optimizer, and Documentation Writer.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Define outcomes and indicators; baseline; monitor; analyze workload and dependencies; tune; capacity-plan; test; communicate; review regressions; and recover.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show metric, workload, percentile, environment, dependency, cost, action, evidence, and limitation. Require human approval for tuning, scale, service reduction, and commitment.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Use business and technical indicators, tail latency, resource and cost signals, backpressure, caching, queueing, degradation, capacity, and rollback.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show performance, workload, SLO, resource, cost, bottleneck, trend, evidence freshness, owner, and authorized tune, scale, throttle, rollback, and reopen controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Performance charter; indicator and baseline; workload and capacity; tuning and change; incident, decision, evidence, exception, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify service, workload, metric, percentile, environment, period, source, method, result, cost, and limitation. Average latency or throughput proves only its measured slice.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify indicators, workload, latency distribution, resource, cost, dependency, user outcome, regression, degradation, recovery, and reproducibility.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Average-only, benchmark theater, optimization without user outcome, cost hidden, warm cache, tail latency ignored, and performance target treated as quality.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Reduce workload, scale, roll back tuning, preserve evidence, communicate, and reassess architecture and objectives. Reopen on workload, dependency, version, cost, or user change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Outcomes, workload, indicators, objectives, resources, cost, evidence, owners, actions, limits, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve baselines, workload, tuning, incidents, metrics, decisions, evidence, exceptions, and open gaps. Hand off to Capacity, SRE, Testing, Application, Platform, and Operations.
