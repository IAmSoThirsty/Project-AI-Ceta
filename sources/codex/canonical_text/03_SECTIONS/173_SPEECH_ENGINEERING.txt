SECTION 173 — Speech Engineering

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define governed engineering for speech capture, recognition, synthesis, diarization, translation, voice interaction, and audio classification with explicit consent, identity, context, quality, accessibility, and human-control boundaries.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- A speech task and audio contract covering speakers, languages, environment, consent, retention, output, and action.
- Evidence for intelligibility, word or intent accuracy, subgroup and acoustic coverage, privacy, security, and safety.
- Controlled capture, review, correction, fallback, and retirement.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Speech carries identity, emotion, private content, accent, disability, and environmental context. Errors can mishear intent, impersonate a person, exclude a speaker, or create an irreversible transaction.

APPLICABILITY

Applies to speech-to-text, text-to-speech, voice authentication, diarization, call analysis, spoken dialogue, audio event detection, and voice cloning. Depth increases with biometrics, transactions, surveillance, health, safety, or public communication.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

May be non-applicable to non-semantic audio formatting or clearly isolated research with no identity, content, user, or operational claim. Record scope, consent, owner, and reopening trigger.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Requires audio rights and consent, language and accessibility context, data lineage, evaluation, privacy, security, safety, and human review. Depends on Sections 088-089, 170-173, 180, 182, and 188.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Task; speakers; language and accent; audio conditions; consent; microphone; corpus; model; output; confidence; identity boundary; retention; disclosure; evaluation; fallback; and transaction authority.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Humans approve capture, biometric or voice use, retention, disclosure, synthesis, transaction, review, and appeal. Affected speakers and accessibility experts must inform material acceptance decisions.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may transcribe, synthesize, classify, translate, and identify bounded audio. It must preserve source and consent, disclose uncertainty and synthetic status, and must not infer identity, emotion, or authorization without evidence.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Architect, Researcher, Analyst, Implementer, UX Reviewer, Accessibility Reviewer, Privacy Reviewer, Security Auditor, Safety Auditor, Tester, Verifier, Critic, Red Team, Governance Reviewer, Documentation Writer, and HITL Operator.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Define speech task, population, languages, environment, consent, and prohibited use.
2. Audit corpus rights, speaker representation, labels, acoustic conditions, and leakage.
3. Specify input, output, identity, synthetic disclosure, retention, and action contracts.
4. Evaluate accents, noise, overlap, impairment, code-switching, spoofing, and drift.
5. Validate human correction, appeal, safe confirmation, and non-AI fallback.
6. Monitor errors, incidents, consent, provider changes, and retention.
7. Baseline and hand off evidence and unresolved coverage.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show recording state, consent, speaker or synthetic status, language, model, transcript, confidence, reviewer, and action. Require confirmation for consequential intent or identity. Preserve text, visual, manual, and recovery paths.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Version microphones, codecs, audio, transcripts, labels, models, voices, prompts, thresholds, policies, and retention. Protect recordings and voice artifacts, preserve timing and provenance, and enforce transaction controls outside recognition.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show capture, consent, speaker, model, output, confidence, retention, reviewer, incidents, and evidence. Allow authorized stop recording, correct, delete, restrict, revoke, fallback, quarantine, and retire actions.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Speech charter; consent and rights register; corpus and label guide; model and voice record; acoustic and subgroup evaluation; privacy and security review; output and confirmation contract; deployment and incident plan.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Claims identify audio source, speaker population, language, condition, model, output, metric, subgroup, method, result, consent, reviewer, and limitation. Word accuracy or naturalness does not prove intent or identity accuracy.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Test accents, noise, overlap, silence, impairment, language switching, replay, spoofing, privacy, synthetic disclosure, confirmation, correction, deletion, provider loss, and emergency fallback. Independently reproduce material results.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Silent recording; voice cloning without consent; accent exclusion; transcript treated as verbatim truth; confidence treated as authorization; biometric overreach; hidden audio retention; and speech-only access to essential service.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Stop capture or action on consent loss, unsafe misrecognition, spoofing, privacy event, drift, or evidence failure. Preserve required records, correct affected transactions, notify owners, use manual fallback, and reopen after population or provider change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Task, audio, consent, population, model, output, evaluation, privacy, accessibility, confirmation, recovery, evidence, and authority are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve audio references, consent, transcripts, voices, models, evaluations, traces, decisions, incidents, dissent, and gaps. Hand off to NLP, Generative AI, UX, Accessibility, Security, Privacy, Evaluation, Operations, and HITL.
