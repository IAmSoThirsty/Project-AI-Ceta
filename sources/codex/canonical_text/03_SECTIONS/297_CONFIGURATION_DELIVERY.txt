SECTION 297 — Configuration Delivery

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Deliver approved configuration changes to exact environments and consumers with version, validation, authorization, observability, rollback, and drift control.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A configuration-delivery model with source, target, value references, schema, gate, identity, sequence, dependency, health, rollback, audit, and recovery.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Configuration delivery can change behavior without a binary release. Wrong target, stale value, secret exposure, ordering, or incomplete rollback can create broad and invisible impact.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when configuration, policy, feature, environment, routing, threshold, or runtime setting is delivered or activated.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability when no configuration or runtime behavior changes and an approved exact baseline covers the scope.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Use configuration, source, environment, feature, deployment, identity, secrets, testing, operations, and rollback baselines.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Change, schema, source, target, value reference, identity, dependencies, gate, sequence, validation, health, rollback, and approval.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Owners approve values, target, sequence, security, production, emergency, rollback, and retirement. Humans review secret and safety impact.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may compare configs, draft delivery plans, validate schemas, and identify drift. It must not expose values, deliver, activate, change gates, or claim success without authority.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Configuration Manager, Deployment Engineer, Release Engineer, Platform Engineer, Security Auditor, Tester, Verifier, and Operations Engineer.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Validate source and schema; resolve target and authority; stage; deliver; verify; observe; pause or roll back; reconcile; record; and retire obsolete configuration.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show item, target, schema, value reference, identity, impact, evidence, and limitation without revealing secrets. Require human approval for protected and production change.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Use versioned source, schema validation, secret references, staged delivery, atomic or ordered change, drift detection, safe default, rollback, and audit.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show config version, target, drift, health, owner, evidence freshness, dependency, and authorized promote, pause, reconcile, rollback, restrict, and retire controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Configuration delivery plan; source and target; schema and validation; rollout and health; rollback and drift; decision, evidence, exception, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify config item, version, target, schema, identity, method, time, result, and limitation. Delivery event proves transmission, not correct activation or behavior.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify schema, target, permissions, sequence, dependency, secret boundary, activation, health, drift, rollback, and failure. Test invalid, stale, partial, and conflicting values.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Manual edit, secret in delivery log, wrong target, schema bypass, config drift, no rollback, activation without health, and config change invisible to release history.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Pause, restore prior valid configuration, preserve evidence, rotate secrets, communicate, and reassess behavior. Reopen on config, environment, dependency, policy, or feature change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Source, schema, target, identity, sequence, health, rollback, evidence, owners, limitations, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending review and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve config versions, delivery logs, targets, drift, incidents, decisions, evidence, exceptions, and open gaps. Hand off to Configuration, Feature, Deployment, Platform, Security, and Operations.
