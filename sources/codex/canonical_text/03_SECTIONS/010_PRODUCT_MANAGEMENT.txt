SECTION 010 — Product Management

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Continuously govern product decisions from discovery through delivery, operation, learning, change, deprecation, and retirement so the product remains aligned with mission, strategy, user outcomes, evidence, and real system capability.

Product management is an ongoing decision function, not backlog administration. It connects users, owners, stakeholders, business, domain, requirements, architecture, design, engineering, operations, support, analytics, risk, and lifecycle obligations while preserving who may decide what.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- A current product decision system with explicit owner, users, outcomes, principles, capabilities, constraints, and lifecycle state.
- A prioritized and traceable opportunity, problem, capability, and requirement flow rather than an unbounded request queue.
- A roadmap and release logic tied to dependencies, evidence, risk, readiness, and learning.
- Clear definitions of discovery, experiment, planned, committed, implemented, tested, verified, released, degraded, deprecated, and retired states.
- Closed feedback loops across research, usage, support, incidents, operations, market, and stakeholder evidence.
- Bounded human-AI product behavior, authority, correction, transparency, fallback, and recovery where applicable.
- Lifecycle decisions and user obligations preserved through change and retirement.

WHY THIS MATTERS

A product can drift when the backlog becomes the strategy, the loudest request becomes the priority, or shipping becomes the only definition of progress. AI can accelerate intake and synthesis while also amplifying noise, duplicating requests, inventing demand, and making weak priorities look quantitative.

Governed product management ensures that work earns its place through outcomes, evidence, constraints, and human judgment. It keeps product promises synchronized with actual runtime capability and preserves users' ability to understand and control consequential behavior.

APPLICABILITY

- A product is being discovered, built, operated, changed, supported, monetized, deprecated, or retired.
- Requests, problems, opportunities, defects, incidents, risks, or strategic bets compete for prioritization.
- Product decisions require coordination across research, design, engineering, security, operations, and business.
- AI or automation materially affects user experience, decisions, data, authority, or trust.
- Product commitments must remain current despite changing evidence, providers, markets, or constraints.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

- A bounded project or maintenance task creates no product decision and follows an approved product plan.
- The work is portfolio-level resource allocation rather than product-level outcome governance.
- No product owner or decision authority exists.
- The request is only to record a backlog without evaluating evidence, outcomes, dependencies, or lifecycle impact.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

- Approved product strategy and product decision owner.
- Research, market, stakeholder, user, domain, feasibility, risk, and business analysis appropriate to the decision.
- Current product, architecture, roadmap, requirement, release, support, incident, analytics, and operational state.
- A governed intake, prioritization, status, evidence, and change-control model.
- Defined user and owner journeys, capability states, and lifecycle obligations.

REQUIRED INPUTS

- Product strategy, mission, business constraints, product principles, and intended outcomes.
- User research, feedback, usability, accessibility, support, incident, and behavioral evidence.
- Market, competitive, stakeholder, regulatory, legal, security, privacy, safety, and ethical evidence.
- Capability, architecture, engineering, quality, test, release, operational, cost, and capacity state.
- Requests, problems, opportunities, defects, risks, dependencies, and commitments.
- Provider, model, data, platform, distribution, compatibility, migration, and retirement conditions.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

The product owner retains authority over priorities, promises, tradeoffs, releases, deprecation, and retirement within higher governance. AI may assist but may not create product legitimacy.

- Confirm product outcomes, principles, prioritization criteria, and mandatory constraints.
- Decide tradeoffs among users, stakeholders, mission, business, risk, accessibility, privacy, safety, and operations.
- Authorize commitments, roadmap changes, consequential automation, releases, and deprecations.
- Correct AI interpretations of user needs, urgency, value, and acceptance.
- Ensure minority, high-risk, low-frequency, accessibility, and recovery needs are not erased by aggregate usage.
- Retain final authority over whether product evidence is sufficient to proceed.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

The AI may classify, link, summarize, compare, forecast, and recommend from current evidence. It may not fabricate users, demand, priority, acceptance, or completed product state.

- Preserve source identity and distinguish requests, observations, hypotheses, commitments, and verified outcomes.
- Deduplicate without erasing materially different contexts, users, or consequences.
- Trace each proposed item to product outcome, evidence, dependencies, risks, and acceptance.
- Expose stale evidence, conflicting findings, hidden prerequisites, and lifecycle effects.
- Avoid ranking solely by volume, engagement, revenue, model confidence, or requester seniority.
- Maintain capability and roadmap status truthfully across external provider and runtime changes.
- Escalate priority and value conflicts to the authorized human.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Primary roles:

- Analyst — evaluates product evidence, options, prioritization, outcomes, and tradeoffs.
- User Experience Reviewer — evaluates user roles, complete journeys, comprehension, accessibility, agency, and recovery.
- Planner — sequences discovery, delivery, release, migration, and lifecycle work by dependencies and evidence gates.

Common supporting roles:

- Researcher — acquires and evaluates user, market, competitor, domain, and regulatory evidence.
- Coordinator — routes product work across roles and preserves status, evidence, and conflict.
- Architect — identifies capability, interface, data, trust, and technical-dependency implications.
- Observer — captures product behavior, incidents, and user outcomes without unsupported causation.
- Memory Curator — preserves product decisions, rationale, roadmap history, and continuity.

Challenge and assurance roles where consequence warrants:

- Critic — attacks framing, hidden assumptions, omitted alternatives, and unsupported confidence.
- Verifier — checks artifact identity, evidence integrity, acceptance boundaries, and the exact status of claims.
- Governance Reviewer — checks objective fidelity, authority, delegation, policy, evidence, and human decision boundaries.
- Tester — validates product behavior against requirements and complete journeys.
- Security Auditor — evaluates product threat, data, identity, abuse, and provider risks.
- Red Team — attacks prioritization, dark patterns, misuse, deceptive status, and failure recovery.

Role selection must use the smallest sufficient set, preserve chamber independence where review is required, and leave every source-defined role contract unchanged.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Product state maintenance
Maintain authoritative product identity, users, outcomes, principles, capabilities, releases, environments, dependencies, and lifecycle state.

2. Governed intake
Capture source, requester, affected users, problem, evidence, urgency, consequence, desired outcome, and authority for every material item.

3. Discovery and problem validation
Test whether the problem exists, whom it affects, current alternatives, frequency, severity, and conditions before committing to a solution.

4. Opportunity and capability analysis
Translate validated problems into outcome and capability hypotheses without prematurely locking a feature or architecture.

5. Prioritization
Apply explicit criteria, mandatory constraints, evidence strength, dependencies, risk, information gain, cost, and lifecycle effects.

6. Roadmap and commitment control
Separate exploratory options, planned work, approved commitments, and externally communicated promises.

7. Requirements and delivery coordination
Maintain traceability through business analysis, requirements, architecture, design, implementation, testing, verification, and release.

8. Release and readiness decisions
Require exact candidate, environment, evidence, limitations, rollback, support, and owner acceptance.

9. Outcome and feedback learning
Combine user research, product analytics, support, incidents, accessibility, operations, and market evidence without confusing correlation with causation.

10. Lifecycle management
Govern compatibility, migration, maintenance, deprecation, sunset, data, account, service, and retirement obligations.

11. Decision and continuity
Record product decisions, dissent, evidence, affected commitments, and reopening triggers.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

- The owner must see why an item is prioritized, what evidence supports it, and what would change the decision.
- AI-generated summaries must preserve minority, high-consequence, accessibility, and contradictory findings.
- A conversational request must not silently become a committed roadmap item or executed product change.
- Capability labels must reflect actual runtime, permissions, provider state, and verification.
- Users must be told when AI behavior is experimental, degraded, externally dependent, or not verified.
- Feedback collection and personalization must preserve consent, correction, data control, and appeal appropriate to consequence.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

The work must expose architectural obligations without treating a strategy, analysis, or plan as implemented architecture.

- Use a traceability graph from product outcomes and problems through capabilities, requirements, artifacts, releases, evidence, and user journeys.
- Maintain one authoritative capability state and invalidate dependent promises when provider or runtime evidence changes.
- Separate product-management workflow state from execution authority.
- Integrate feature flags, staged release, observability, rollback, feedback, support, and incident linkage.
- Support export, migration, compatibility, deprecation, and retirement from the product lifecycle.
- Protect research, feedback, analytics, and model data through minimization, consent, access, retention, and audit controls.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

The owner-facing operating surface or equivalent durable record should expose:

- Product outcomes, principles, users, capabilities, lifecycle state, and decision owner.
- Intake items with source, evidence, affected parties, consequence, dependencies, and status.
- Prioritization criteria, recommendations, dissent, decisions, and change history.
- Roadmap states separated into hypothesis, planned, approved, committed, delivered, verified, and released.
- Candidate readiness, tests, limitations, incidents, support, rollback, and user acceptance.
- Outcome, feedback, accessibility, trust, security, privacy, reliability, and operational signals.
- Deprecation, migration, notice, data, and retirement controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

- Product decision register and product-state record.
- Governed intake and opportunity backlog.
- Prioritization framework and decision rationale.
- Outcome-capability-requirement-roadmap traceability.
- Discovery, experiment, validation, and release plans.
- Product readiness and release decision records.
- Feedback, analytics, support, incident, and outcome synthesis.
- Lifecycle, deprecation, migration, and retirement records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

- Source and context for every material request, finding, commitment, and decision.
- User, market, domain, support, incident, analytics, operational, and technical evidence with dates and limitations.
- Human-approved criteria, priorities, commitments, and risk decisions.
- Exact requirement, artifact, candidate, test, environment, release, and capability evidence.
- Outcome evidence that distinguishes effect from correlation and activity from benefit.
- Notice, migration, data, support, and closure evidence for lifecycle changes.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

- Trace a roadmap item from source problem through evidence, capability, requirement, release, and measured outcome.
- Inject duplicate requests from one loud source and confirm volume alone does not dominate priority.
- Remove capability or provider evidence and verify dependent roadmap and UI claims degrade.
- Attempt to convert a conversational request into commitment without approval; it must fail.
- Run critical user journeys for a release through failure, denial, recovery, and accessibility paths.
- Verify a deprecation cannot close until notice, migration, data, support, and obligation criteria are satisfied.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

- Backlog as strategy and feature count as progress.
- Prioritization by requester volume, hierarchy, revenue, engagement, or model confidence alone.
- Invented user demand, acceptance, urgency, or impact.
- Roadmap promises that outrun requirements, capacity, dependencies, or evidence.
- Shipping treated as outcome, verification, or user value.
- Silent capability drift from provider or model changes.
- Ignoring low-frequency catastrophic, accessibility, privacy, safety, or recovery needs.
- Deprecation or retirement without user control and obligation closure.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Product-management recovery restores truthful product state and reopens decisions whose evidence, dependencies, or commitments became invalid.

- Pause affected commitments or rollout while preserving safe service and user obligations.
- Reconcile authoritative product, roadmap, capability, release, support, incident, and evidence state.
- Identify the first invalid problem, priority, requirement, provider, capability, or outcome assumption.
- Reprioritize or replan through human authorization and communicate material promise changes.
- Rollback, disable, narrow, redesign, migrate, deprecate, or retire affected capability as appropriate.
- Update users, stakeholders, operations, documentation, and continuity.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

- The product decision system has an authorized owner, current evidence, and explicit criteria.
- Intake, prioritization, roadmap, requirements, release, outcomes, and lifecycle are traceable.
- Product and AI capability states are truthful and version-bound.
- User agency, accessibility, privacy, safety, security, recovery, and operational obligations are integrated.
- Recommendations remain distinct from commitments and executed changes.
- The product can continue without hidden chat memory or undocumented decision rationale.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

- Provide each project or delivery team with approved outcome, capability, scope, criteria, dependencies, and evidence.
- Carry forward user, stakeholder, domain, accessibility, security, privacy, safety, and lifecycle constraints.
- Bind commitments to exact roadmap, requirement, release, and acceptance states.
- Preserve rejected, deferred, superseded, and reopened decisions with rationale.
- Transfer operational, support, analytics, feedback, migration, and retirement obligations.
- State next product decision and safe-to-continue status.
