SECTION 130 - Microservices Engineering

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define governed engineering of independently deployable services organized around bounded responsibilities, explicit contracts, data ownership, operational authority, resilience, security, observability, and lifecycle.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A service baseline with bounded contexts, ownership, APIs and events, data authority, deployment, failure, scaling, security, observability, compatibility, and retirement evidence.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Microservices distribute failure and decision-making. Poor boundaries create chatty coupling, inconsistent data, operational overload, and unclear responsibility disguised as flexibility.

APPLICABILITY

Applies when a solution uses multiple independently deployed services, service teams, containers, queues, or provider components with separate lifecycles.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

May be non-applicable to a bounded monolith or single process with no service boundary, independent deployment, or distributed contract. Record scope, owner, evidence, and reopening trigger.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Requires domain and capability boundaries, requirements, service ownership, APIs and events, data authority, identity, threat model, deployment, observability, reliability, support, and retirement policy.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Provide service goals, boundaries, dependencies, data, commands, events, SLOs, failure domains, scaling, security, tenancy, migration, test, operational, and compensation requirements.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Humans approve service boundaries, ownership, data authority, risk, operational burden, release, exceptions, and retirement. Humans retain authority over cross-service business decisions and external commitments.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may propose boundaries, contracts, tests, capacity analysis, and diagnostics. It must not create services for fashion, alter data authority, deploy changes, or hide distributed failure without authorization.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Architect, Analyst, Researcher, Security Auditor, Governance Reviewer, Implementer, Tester, Verifier, Observer, Coordinator, Documentation Writer, Memory Curator, Optimizer, Critic, and Red Team. Existing role contracts remain controlling.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Define bounded contexts, outcomes, owners, data authority, and contracts.
2. Model calls, events, dependencies, consistency, failure, retries, tracing, deployment, and recovery.
3. Build and release with independent evidence, operational readiness, and migration control.
4. Monitor service and outcome health, manage change, and retire consumers and services safely.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show service identity, owner, version, dependencies, authority, data, health, evidence, and consequence. Human review is required for boundary, data, and production decisions; stop and recovery controls must survive model failure.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Use explicit bounded contexts, contract versions, data ownership, synchronous and event paths, timeouts, circuit breaking, idempotency, tracing, tenancy, secrets, deployment, and compensation. Avoid distributed transactions unless justified.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show service map, versions, owners, dependencies, SLOs, capacity, incidents, data freshness, deployment state, contract drift, and controls to throttle, isolate, roll back, restore, or retire.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Service catalog, boundary and dependency model, contracts, data ownership, threat assessment, deployment and observability design, resilience tests, runbooks, migration, and retirement package.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify service, revision, dependency, request or event, data, environment, trace, workload, method, result, and limits. Distinguish service health from end-to-end outcome and preserve partial-failure evidence.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify contracts, authorization, tenancy, timeout, retry, idempotency, ordering, consistency, dependency loss, scaling, deployment, rollback, tracing, recovery, migration, and retirement.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Service per table; shared database authority; chatty synchronous chains; unbounded retries; missing timeouts; independent deploy without compatibility; and green service health masking failed outcomes.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Isolate failing services, stop unsafe writes, preserve queues and traces, use approved fallback or prior version, reconcile data, compensate external effects, and reopen boundaries and readiness evidence.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Boundaries, ownership, contracts, data, controls, operations, evidence, recovery, change, and retirement are explicit. Keep CONTENT-COMPLETE until independent verification and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve service and revision identity, maps, contracts, traces, incidents, evidence, decisions, open gaps, and next safe action. Hand off to Distributed Systems, API, Integration, Data, Platform, Reliability, Testing, and Operations sections.
