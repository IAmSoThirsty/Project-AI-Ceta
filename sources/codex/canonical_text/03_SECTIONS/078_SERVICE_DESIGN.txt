SECTION 078 — Service Design

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define the coordinated design of services, people, processes, technology, policy, suppliers, channels, and evidence that produce an outcome for users and the organization.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

 - A bounded service ecosystem, actors, value exchange, frontstage and backstage, dependencies, outcomes, and lifecycle.
 - Service blueprint, ownership, measures, controls, accessibility, failure, continuity, and acceptance records.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Services fail at boundaries: policy to practice, channel to channel, provider to owner, and user to support. Service Design exposes those dependencies and assigns accountability for the whole outcome.

APPLICABILITY

Applies to public, commercial, internal, digital, physical, assisted, subscription, support, and multi-party services.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

No material service outcome, actor, process, channel, dependency, commitment, accessibility, or recovery effect; or an unchanged approved service baseline with impact recorded.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Service strategy, stakeholders, user research, journeys, processes, capabilities, applications, suppliers, policies, contracts, accessibility, privacy, security, operations, and acceptance authority.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Service strategy, stakeholders, user research, journeys, processes, capabilities, applications, suppliers, policies, contracts, accessibility, privacy, security, operations, and acceptance authority.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Humans own service purpose, commitments, eligibility, fairness, staffing, supplier choices, risk, exceptions, and acceptance. They decide conflicts and retain stop, escalation, redress, and retirement authority.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may map service evidence, identify friction, simulate demand, and propose blueprint changes. It must preserve source and affected-party limits and must not assign obligations, deny service, or change commitments without authority.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Service Designer, Architect, Researcher, Analyst, User Experience Reviewer, Process and Operations owners, Governance Reviewer, Security Auditor, Accessibility specialist, Tester, Verifier, Critic, Coordinator, Documentation Writer, and Memory Curator.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Define service outcome and eligibility; map actors, channels, frontstage, backstage, processes, systems, suppliers, evidence, controls, failure, support, and recovery; prototype and test; baseline ownership and handoff.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show service state, actor, eligibility, authority, channel, dependency, automation, consequence, wait, explanation, and recovery. Keep human assistance and accessible alternatives available when AI or a provider fails.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Represent service ecosystem, contracts, queues, handoffs, data, policy, identity, suppliers, support, observability, continuity, and lifecycle. Distinguish service definition from actual delivery and outcome.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show service owner, current mode, journey, demand, capacity, dependencies, incidents, commitments, evidence, open findings, and controls to pause, narrow, escalate, compensate, restore, or retire.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Service blueprint, ecosystem map, journey and channel models, service standards, ownership matrix, supplier and support contracts, evidence plan, measures, risk register, recovery model, and handoff package.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify population, service, channel, actor, period, contract or requirement, method, result, limitation, and owner. A blueprint, SLA, queue metric, or survey does not prove equitable or effective service.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify end-to-end outcome, eligibility, accessibility, channel continuity, handoffs, supplier failure, demand peaks, privacy, security, support, complaint, compensation, and recovery.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Optimizing internal throughput; hiding eligibility or denial; outsourcing accountability; fragmented channels; inaccessible support; and treating an SLA or dashboard as the user outcome.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Contain affected service, preserve records, communicate limits, use approved alternate channel or manual support, escalate to the owner, and compensate where authorized. Reconsider after supplier, policy, capacity, accessibility, and evidence review.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Service outcome, ecosystem, actors, channels, dependencies, commitments, controls, evidence, recovery, open risks, and acceptance authority are explicit. Remain CONTENT-COMPLETE pending reconciliation, hostile review, and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve blueprint, contracts, service owners, supplier and support contacts, measures, decisions, incidents, evidence, dissent, and next safe action. Hand off to Process, Operations, UX, Content, Architecture, Support, Testing, and Governance.
