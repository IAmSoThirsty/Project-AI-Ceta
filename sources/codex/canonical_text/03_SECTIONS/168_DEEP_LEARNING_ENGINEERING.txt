SECTION 168 — Deep Learning Engineering

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define governed engineering for deep neural systems, including architecture, training computation, data pipelines, optimization, evaluation, serving, resource use, safety controls, and lifecycle.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- An attributable neural-system design with architecture, data, objective, compute, artifacts, and operational contract.
- Reproducible training and evaluation with robustness, calibration, subgroup, privacy, security, and resource evidence.
- Controlled deployment, monitoring, fallback, and retirement.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Deep models can fail outside benchmarks, encode data defects, consume extraordinary resources, and be difficult to interpret or reproduce. Explicit engineering records make those risks visible and actionable.

APPLICABILITY

Applies to neural networks, representation learners, multimodal models, sequence models, foundation models, and neural components used in consequential systems. It applies to training, fine-tuning, distillation, compression, and serving.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

May be non-applicable to non-neural deterministic components or conceptual research with no model artifact or operational claim. Record exact scope, owner, date, and reopening condition.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Requires data and model contracts, compute and supply-chain controls, evaluation, safety, privacy, security, and deployment authority. Depends on Sections 167, 179-184, and 253.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Task; architecture; data; objective; tokenizer or input representation; compute budget; optimizer; checkpoints; reproducibility settings; metrics; threat model; intended and prohibited uses; serving constraints; and human-control plan.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Humans approve architecture class, data use, compute, evaluation gates, release, acceptable residual risk, and public claims. Engineers cannot infer safety or authorization from scale, loss, or benchmark improvement.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may suggest architectures, code, hyperparameters, diagnostics, and tests. It must label generated work, preserve exact configuration, and must not fabricate runs, claim interpretability, or authorize release.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Architect, Implementer, Researcher, Analyst, Optimizer, Tester, Verifier, Security Auditor, Privacy Reviewer, Safety Auditor, Critic, Red Team, Documentation Writer, Memory Curator, and Governance Reviewer.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Define task, contract, threat, protected properties, and prohibited uses.
2. Audit data, preprocessing, splits, contamination, and representativeness.
3. Establish architecture, objective, resource, checkpoint, and reproducibility controls.
4. Train, validate, stress-test, and compare baselines and alternatives.
5. Evaluate robustness, calibration, subgroup, privacy, security, latency, and cost.
6. Verify serving, fallback, monitoring, rollback, and retirement.
7. Record failed runs, uncertainty, dissent, and owner decisions.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Expose architecture, checkpoint, data, configuration, output limits, review, and uncertainty. Generated changes remain proposed. Preserve manual review, stop, fallback, and appeal paths for model outputs and deployment actions.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Version code, data, preprocessing, architecture, weights, tokenizer, hardware, libraries, checkpoints, evaluation, container, endpoint, and policy. Protect artifacts and secrets, enforce input/output contracts, and record resource and failure telemetry.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show checkpoint lineage, training state, compute, evaluations, deployment, drift, incidents, and authority. Provide authorized controls to pause training, revoke artifacts, reduce traffic, fallback, rollback, quarantine, retrain, and retire.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Neural-system charter; architecture and data record; training configuration; checkpoint register; reproducible environment; evaluation and robustness report; security and safety review; serving contract; deployment and retirement plan; decision record.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Claims identify architecture, weights, data, compute, configuration, evaluation set, metric, subgroup, method, result, uncertainty, and limitation. Loss and benchmark scores do not prove real-world safety, fairness, or usefulness.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Reproduce checkpoints; test preprocessing and serving parity; exercise corruption, shift, adversarial input, resource exhaustion, access, fallback, rollback, and authority revocation; and verify evidence invalidation after configuration changes.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Checkpoint confusion; undocumented preprocessing; contamination; unstable training; benchmark fixation; hidden unsafe capability; unbounded resource use; artifact tampering; and silent post-release retraining.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Freeze affected checkpoints and deployments on unsafe behavior, mismatch, breach, drift, or evidence loss. Preserve artifacts, use approved prior or manual mode, investigate, re-evaluate, and reopen before resuming.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Architecture, data, training, evaluation, serving, controls, resources, recovery, evidence, and authority are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending reconciliation and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve checkpoints, data, code, hardware, environment, metrics, decisions, incidents, dissent, and gaps. Hand off to ML Engineering, Training, Evaluation, MLOps, Model Operations, Safety, Security, and Governance.
