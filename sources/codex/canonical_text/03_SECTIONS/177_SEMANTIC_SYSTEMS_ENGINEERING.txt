SECTION 177 — Semantic Systems Engineering

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define engineering of systems that interpret, map, reason over, validate, or exchange meaning across data, language, domains, and applications with explicit provenance and authority.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- A semantic contract connecting ontology, data, information, queries, inference, users, and decisions.
- Verified mappings, reasoning boundaries, provenance, access, contradiction handling, and lifecycle controls.
- A clear distinction between semantic consistency and domain correctness.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Semantic systems can be internally consistent while consistently representing the wrong meaning. Hidden mappings and inference can affect access, ranking, decisions, and generated answers without visible accountability.

APPLICABILITY

Applies to semantic APIs, knowledge representations, reasoning engines, federated meaning, ontology services, semantic search, validation, and AI grounding. Depth increases with automated inference, cross-domain exchange, or external consequence.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

May be non-applicable to local syntax or direct lookup with no meaning mapping, inference, or cross-boundary interpretation. Record the exact boundary and reopening condition.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Requires Information, Data, Knowledge, Ontology, API, Identity, Privacy, Security, and Governance authorities. Depends on Sections 030, 031, 042, 055, 175, and 176.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Semantic purpose; ontologies; vocabularies; mappings; data; context; inference rules; query contract; provenance; identity; permissions; contradiction policy; performance; and owner acceptance.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Humans approve meaning, mappings, inference limits, contradictions, access, publication, and downstream use. Semantic engineers cannot resolve domain disputes or turn logical entailment into policy authority.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may propose mappings, embeddings, queries, and explanations. It must preserve source and uncertainty and must not silently infer meaning, permission, identity, or obligation.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Architect, Ontology Engineer, Information Architect, Data Engineer, Researcher, Analyst, Governance Reviewer, Privacy Reviewer, Security Auditor, Critic, Tester, Verifier, Documentation Writer, and Memory Curator.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Define semantic boundary, users, domains, contexts, and prohibited inference.
2. Inventory concepts, mappings, data, sources, and authority.
3. Specify semantic contracts, query behavior, provenance, access, and contradiction policy.
4. Implement and test mapping, validation, reasoning, retrieval, and explanation.
5. Evaluate ambiguity, drift, identity collision, leakage, and unintended entailment.
6. Validate with domain owners and affected users.
7. Version, monitor, deprecate, migrate, and hand off.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show source, semantic path, mapping, inference, confidence, context, permissions, and reviewer. Users must inspect and challenge reasoning. Generated semantic links remain proposals and cannot alter records or policy without authority.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Version semantic contracts, ontologies, mappings, reasoners, embeddings, indexes, queries, provenance, policies, and outputs. Enforce context and access at query and inference boundaries. Preserve contradictions and temporal validity.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show semantic baseline, source, concept, mapping, inference, query, conflict, access, freshness, and downstream uses. Provide authorized correct, restrict, quarantine, rerun, supersede, withdraw, and retire controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Semantic charter; ontology and mapping register; contract; reasoning and contradiction policy; provenance and access design; query and explanation examples; validation report; decision record; and continuity handoff.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Claims identify semantic version, source, mapping, inference rule, context, query, result, method, reviewer, and limitation. Logical consistency, embedding similarity, or query success does not prove domain validity.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Test mappings, contradictions, ambiguity, temporal changes, unauthorized inference, stale indexes, identity collisions, version compatibility, query reproducibility, deletion, and explanation. Independently reproduce representative results.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Semantic drift; hidden mapping; inference treated as source; closed-world assumption unnoticed; embedding similarity as truth; contradiction suppression; unauthorized traversal; and schema compatibility mistaken for meaning compatibility.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Restrict affected reasoning on semantic conflict, mapping failure, access loss, or provenance gap. Preserve prior version, identify downstream decisions, correct or rebuild, obtain domain review, and reopen after ontology or data change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Meaning, mappings, inference, provenance, access, contradictions, tests, lifecycle, evidence, and authority are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending source reconciliation and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve contracts, ontologies, mappings, reasoners, queries, results, decisions, dissent, evidence, and gaps. Hand off to Knowledge Graph, Ontology, Information, Data, API, Search, AI, Evaluation, and Governance.
