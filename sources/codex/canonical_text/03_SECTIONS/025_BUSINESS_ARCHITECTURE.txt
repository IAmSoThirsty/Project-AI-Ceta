SECTION 025 — Business Architecture

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define the governed structure of how an enterprise or bounded organization creates, delivers, sustains, governs, changes, and retires value. Business Architecture connects mission and strategy to stakeholders, value propositions, outcomes, value streams, capabilities, organizations, roles, decisions, policies, information, products, services, resources, measures, risks, controls, investments, and transition work.

This section translates strategy and enterprise intent into an operating structure that can be understood before it is embedded in technology, staffing, contracts, procedures, or AI automation. It exposes who is affected, who owns which decisions, what the organization must be able to do, how value and evidence move, where accountability resides, and which conditions must remain true during change.

Business Architecture is not an organization chart, process inventory, software map, or consulting diagram. It does not treat an aspiration as an operating reality, an AI-generated model as authority, or a capability label as proof that the capability exists. It preserves the difference between current, proposed, approved, implemented, adopted, measured, effective, degraded, and retired states.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- A declared enterprise or business boundary with included and excluded organizations, products, services, populations, jurisdictions, environments, partners, suppliers, channels, and lifecycle horizon.
- A source-grounded current-state business architecture that distinguishes observed operation from policy, planned work, informal workaround, and inference.
- A target-state business architecture traceable to mission, strategy, stakeholder outcomes, user evidence, domain meaning, requirements, risk, and authorized decisions.
- Explicit value propositions and outcome statements identifying intended beneficiaries, affected parties, obligations, harms to avoid, evidence, assumptions, and success boundaries.
- A value-stream architecture showing triggering need, participating stakeholders, capabilities, decisions, information, products, services, controls, evidence, support, recovery, and terminal outcome.
- A business capability map with stable definitions, ownership, outcomes, dependencies, required competencies, resources, information, controls, measures, evidence, lifecycle state, and realization mechanisms.
- An operating-model view defining how capabilities, organizations, roles, decision rights, processes, information, technology, partners, locations, and governance combine to deliver outcomes.
- A decision-rights and accountability model that distinguishes authority, responsibility, consultation, execution, verification, approval, risk acceptance, and final human control.
- A business information concept model identifying authoritative concepts, records, evidence, ownership, provenance, privacy, retention, and cross-domain translation needs.
- A product, service, channel, customer, user, partner, supplier, and ecosystem view linked to capabilities and value streams.
- A policy, rule, obligation, control, and exception view showing source, jurisdiction, owner, enforcement location, evidence, expiry, and escalation path.
- Current, target, and transition states with dependencies, sequencing, investments, benefits, risks, adoption, training, migration, rollback, continuity, and retirement obligations.
- A measurement architecture that distinguishes activity, output, outcome, benefit, harm, control effectiveness, resilience, owner operability, and evidence quality.
- A durable handoff to Capability Architecture, Process Architecture, Functional Architecture, Operational Architecture, Information Architecture, organization design, product and service design, technology architecture, delivery, operations, and lifecycle governance.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Strategy fails when it cannot be translated into an operating structure. Organizations may fund projects without knowing which enduring capability they improve. Teams may optimize local output while damaging an end-to-end value stream. Technology may automate an undocumented workaround or conceal a broken decision boundary. Metrics may reward volume while degrading user outcomes, safety, dignity, trust, or long-term viability.

Business Architecture gives the owner a coherent view of how intent becomes value and obligation becomes controlled action. It makes visible the relationships among mission, stakeholders, capabilities, decisions, processes, information, products, services, resources, controls, and evidence. It also exposes gaps between what the enterprise claims to do and what current evidence shows it can actually sustain.

For human-AI systems, Business Architecture prevents the substitution of model capability for institutional authority. AI may help discover patterns, model options, identify contradictions, and maintain traceability. It may not decide whose interests matter, redefine the mission, create binding policy, allocate undelegated authority, accept risk, or represent an unproven operating capability as real.

APPLICABILITY

- Mission, business, product, portfolio, program, project, or transformation strategy must be translated into an operating structure.
- The enterprise needs to understand how value is created across organizational, product, service, process, information, technology, partner, or jurisdiction boundaries.
- Capabilities, value streams, roles, decision rights, policies, controls, products, services, or investments are unclear, duplicated, fragmented, or changing.
- A merger, acquisition, divestiture, partnership, outsourcing, insourcing, reorganization, platform change, modernization, or retirement affects business operation.
- A digital, automation, AI, or agent initiative could change authority, accountability, work allocation, user experience, evidence, or risk.
- Business and technology teams disagree about what outcome is required, who owns it, or which operating constraint is binding.
- Regulatory, contractual, privacy, safety, security, accessibility, records, or public-interest obligations must be embedded in operating design.
- Portfolio and investment decisions require stable capability and value-stream context rather than project labels alone.
- Current-state operations include manual workarounds, shadow systems, undocumented decisions, or hidden dependencies.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

- A narrowly bounded change has no effect on value propositions, value streams, capabilities, operating model, decision rights, policy, organization, information, products, services, partners, measures, risk, controls, or lifecycle obligations, and current architecture evidence proves that conclusion.
- The work is a temporary exploratory exercise that makes no claim of organizational approval, implementation, adoption, benefit, or operating readiness. The exercise must still state its boundary and limitations.
- An active safety, security, privacy, integrity, labor, legal, or authority incident requires containment before business redesign can proceed safely.
- The requested modeling would require unauthorized access to protected strategy, personnel, customer, user, partner, supplier, contract, financial, security, or operational information.
- The legitimate business owner, affected-party authority, or decision boundary cannot be established. Work may continue only as a labeled research or option package.

A non-applicability decision must identify the exact current architecture, scope, version, evidence date, decision owner, assumptions, and reopening triggers. Convenience or small apparent size is not sufficient.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

The authorized human owner remains responsible for:

- Defining and approving mission, values, strategic outcomes, enterprise boundary, affected-party obligations, and acceptable tradeoffs.
- Establishing legitimate decision owners and confirming the authority of participating executives, managers, domain experts, operators, employees, contractors, partners, and reviewers.
- Approving value propositions, target operating model, capability priorities, organization changes, policies, investments, external commitments, risk acceptance, and transition authority.
- Determining when workforce, labor, accessibility, privacy, safety, security, legal, ethical, public-interest, or contractual consultation is required.
- Resolving value conflicts, policy conflicts, priority conflicts, and disputed interpretations that were not validly delegated.
- Preventing AI recommendations, scoring, simulations, or generated diagrams from becoming binding through presentation alone.
- Ensuring affected people have appropriate notice, participation, consent, grievance, appeal, accommodation, or recourse where required.
- Accepting, rejecting, conditioning, deferring, superseding, or reopening business architecture decisions.
- Ratifying claims of implementation, adoption, operating effectiveness, benefit, or readiness only when current evidence supports them.

AI may support but cannot inherit these responsibilities through tool access, system capability, role title, or prior conversational context.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may:

- Retrieve, classify, compare, and summarize authorized source material.
- Build current-state inventories, capability maps, value-stream models, decision maps, operating-model views, dependency graphs, traceability matrices, and option analyses.
- Detect contradictions, duplicated capabilities, fragmented ownership, missing decisions, unsupported claims, stale evidence, metric conflicts, and transition risks.
- Generate candidate target states, transition options, scenarios, questions, experiments, acceptance conditions, and evidence requests.
- Maintain provenance, assumptions, unknowns, dissent, dependencies, versions, and reopening triggers.
- Simulate bounded scenarios when the model, assumptions, data, and limitations are explicit.

AI must not:

- Redefine mission, values, stakeholders, outcomes, obligations, or enterprise scope.
- Invent organizational authority, policy, law, contract, budget, capacity, market evidence, user needs, consent, or operating state.
- Treat access to information or systems as authority to decide, approve, commit, reorganize, hire, terminate, contract, disclose, or deploy.
- Rank people, teams, partners, or populations using undisclosed or unauthorized criteria.
- Recommend harmful surveillance, coercion, manipulation, discrimination, dark patterns, or removal of meaningful human control.
- Convert a capability hypothesis into an implemented capability claim.
- Hide dissent, exceptions, weak evidence, affected-party harm, or uncertainty to create a clean target model.
- approve its own architecture, implementation, or verification where independent review is required.

When authority, evidence, or governing obligations are unclear, AI must return an explicit unknown, hold, request for owner decision, or bounded option set rather than fabricate closure.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

- Architect: structures the business architecture, views, boundaries, interfaces, dependencies, authority, failure, recovery, and verification path.
- Researcher: acquires and assesses authoritative business, market, operational, regulatory, contractual, and stakeholder evidence.
- Analyst: compares current and target states, options, outcomes, costs, dependencies, risks, and sensitivity to assumptions.
- Critic: challenges unsupported claims, hidden dependencies, incomplete stakeholder coverage, metric distortion, and operating-model contradictions.
- Security Auditor: evaluates security, identity, authority, supply-chain, information, partner, and control implications.
- Tester: converts business architecture claims into testable journeys, scenarios, controls, and acceptance conditions.
- Verifier: independently checks claim-to-evidence alignment, artifact identity, version, scope, and completion boundaries.
- Documentation Writer: maintains architecture descriptions, catalogs, decisions, glossary, traceability, and operating guidance.
- Planner: sequences discovery, modeling, decisions, experiments, transition, adoption, verification, and recovery work.
- Coordinator: assigns bounded work, preserves provenance, reconciles compatible outputs, and surfaces unresolved conflict.
- Observer: captures actual operating behavior, decisions, events, dependencies, workarounds, and evidence without unsupported interpretation.
- Memory Curator: preserves governed architecture state, decisions, source links, supersession, open questions, and continuity.
- Governance Reviewer: evaluates authority, rule hierarchy, policy, exception, approval, and safe-halt conditions.
- Red Team: pressure-tests incentives, bypasses, hidden authority, concentration, transition, resilience, and false-readiness claims.
- User Experience Reviewer: evaluates whether owner, employee, operator, customer, and user journeys remain understandable, accessible, controllable, and recoverable.
- Decision Arbiter Advisory: compares options against authorized criteria and provides advisory recommendations unless explicit decision authority exists.

Roles must be selected for the required function and given explicit assignments, inputs, authority, prohibited actions, outputs, evidence requirements, review gates, and completion conditions. The role corpus remains unchanged.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

- AI-generated business models are proposals until approved by the legitimate human authority.
- Every material AI claim must identify source, method, evidence date, scope, limitations, and whether it is observation, inference, assumption, recommendation, or unknown.
- The owner must be able to inspect current-state evidence, alternative models, dissent, tradeoffs, affected-party consequences, and unresolved questions before approval.
- The AI must disclose when a target model reflects a design choice rather than discovered fact.
- AI must not compress different stakeholder interests into one synthetic objective without authorization.
- Capability, maturity, performance, risk, or prioritization scores must expose criteria, weights, data quality, uncertainty, and decision authority.
- Consequential recommendations require evidence, alternatives, reversibility, conditions, and what would change the recommendation.
- Human approval must be bound to the reviewed architecture version, scope, assumptions, and evidence. Material change invalidates prior approval.
- The owner must be able to stop, narrow, revoke, supersede, or defer AI work and inspect the resulting state.
- Critical continuity must survive chat loss through versioned architecture artifacts, decisions, traceability, evidence, and next-safe-action records.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Business Architecture establishes requirements for all downstream architecture and system work.

- Value streams and capabilities must map to domain, information, application, technology, solution, systems, process, functional, operational, security, privacy, safety, accessibility, data, integration, and lifecycle architecture as applicable.
- Each consequential business decision must have an authority path, policy basis, state model, evidence requirement, and owner-visible control.
- Each critical value stream must identify system and non-system dependencies, manual fallbacks, degradation, recovery, support, and continuity needs.
- Business information concepts must align with Domain Architecture and later Information and Data Architecture. Translation across contexts must be explicit.
- Technology may realize a capability but must not define the capability's enduring meaning or business ownership.
- AI and automation must be modeled as realization mechanisms with bounded role, authority, data, evidence, failure, fallback, monitoring, and revocation requirements.
- Current-state architecture must expose shadow systems, spreadsheets, manual decisions, tacit knowledge, external services, and human work rather than omitting them because they are not formal technology.
- Target-state architecture must preserve owner operability and human final authority where values, rights, obligations, risk, or irreversible consequences remain human decisions.
- Architecture changes must invalidate affected requirements, designs, tests, evidence, approvals, training, procedures, contracts, and measures.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

- Inspect the active business architecture version, scope, environment, jurisdiction, evidence date, decision owner, and approval state.
- Navigate from mission and strategy to outcomes, value streams, capabilities, decisions, processes, information, products, services, systems, initiatives, measures, risks, controls, and evidence.
- Distinguish current, proposed, approved, implemented, adopted, measured, effective, degraded, superseded, and retired states.
- See who owns each value stream, capability, decision, policy, measure, risk, exception, and transition obligation.
- Trace a capability claim to actual people, process, information, technology, partner, control, support, recovery, and evidence.

- Compare architecture options and view consequences, dependencies, costs, reversibility, and residual risks.
- Stop, defer, revoke, quarantine, supersede, or reopen architecture and transition decisions within defined authority.
- Export architecture models, decisions, evidence, traceability, and continuity records without relying on AI chat history.
- Continue essential status, stop, decision, and recovery functions when AI or external providers are unavailable.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Evidence may include mission and strategy records, owner decisions, governance documents, organization records, role definitions, policies, contracts, laws, regulations, budgets, portfolio data, product and service records, customer and user research, stakeholder evidence, operational observation, process records, transaction records, system telemetry, incident history, support cases, audits, risk assessments, control tests, workforce evidence, training records, supplier evidence, financial records, and benefit measurement.

Each material claim must identify source identity, version, environment, jurisdiction, effective date, method, scope, owner, limitations, and relationship to the claim. A policy does not prove operational behavior. A software feature does not prove business capability. A project completion record does not prove adoption or benefit. A metric does not prove value unless its definition, data lineage, incentives, and validity are known. An AI-generated map is not evidence of the organization.

Current-state evidence should combine documented intent with direct operational evidence where practical. Target-state evidence must identify assumptions and design authority. Transition claims must bind to exact dependencies, resources, approvals, training, migration, support, rollback, and acceptance conditions.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

- Select a mission outcome and trace it through strategy, value proposition, value stream, capabilities, decisions, processes, information, products or services, systems, measures, controls, and evidence.
- Select a capability and prove that its outcome, owner, dependencies, competencies, resources, information, systems, controls, support, recovery, and current state are explicit.
- Select a critical value stream and run a representative end-to-end scenario, including exception, degradation, support, recovery, and evidence.
- Modify one current-state assumption and verify that affected architecture, requirements, initiatives, measures, risks, and decisions are identified.
- Introduce conflicting decision ownership and verify the architecture surfaces rather than silently resolves the conflict.
- Attempt to infer authority from an organization title, system permission, or AI capability and confirm that explicit delegation is still required.
- Remove or invalidate evidence supporting a capability or operating claim and confirm the claim degrades to unknown, unverified, or not effective.
- Compare current and target states and verify that every material gap has an owner, dependency, investment, transition action, evidence gate, and reopening trigger.
- Change a policy, contract, regulation, partner, supplier, or critical system and confirm impact propagation.
- Ask the owner to reconstruct the architecture and next safe decision from durable artifacts without chat memory.
- Verify that front-end, dashboard, or conversational claims cannot override authoritative business, system, authority, or evidence state.
- Hostile-review incentives and measures for gaming, displaced harm, hidden work, automation bias, and anti-metric failure.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

- Organization chart presented as Business Architecture.
- Process inventory, application map, or project portfolio presented as a complete operating model.
- Capability names derived from current systems, departments, vendors, or initiatives without stable outcome definitions.
- Strategy slogans with no value stream, capability, decision, investment, measure, or evidence path.
- Current-state model built only from policy, leadership narrative, or documentation while actual work differs.
- Target state represented as current or inevitable.
- Value stream ending at internal output rather than stakeholder outcome.
- Metrics that reward throughput, engagement, or cost reduction while hiding harm, rework, exclusion, risk, or loss of control.
- Decision rights inferred from access, seniority, system role, or AI capability.
- Accountability assigned to a role that lacks authority, resources, information, or control.
- Technology chosen before business need, capability, domain, authority, and evidence are understood.
- AI automation used to conceal unresolved policy, staffing, labor, accessibility, privacy, safety, or governance choices.
- Capability duplication hidden by different labels or capability differences hidden by one broad label.
- Partner or supplier dependency omitted from capability and resilience models.
- Informal work, tacit knowledge, manual exceptions, shadow systems, or support burden omitted.
- Architecture approval treated as implementation, adoption, benefit, or operating proof.
- Transition plan without coexistence, migration, training, support, rollback, continuity, or retirement.
- Dissent, exceptions, and evidence gaps deleted to produce apparent alignment.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

When Business Architecture is blocked, contradicted, or invalidated:

1. Identify the exact claim, model element, decision, source, or dependency affected.
2. Classify the issue as authority, source identity, scope, objective, stakeholder, domain, capability, value stream, decision right, information, policy, measure, evidence, feasibility, risk, implementation, adoption, or transition failure.
3. Freeze affected approvals, target-state claims, investment recommendations, or transition actions when continued use would be misleading or harmful.
4. Preserve prior versions, evidence, dissent, decisions, and change history.
5. Identify safe work that can continue without compromising truth, authority, or continuity.
6. Define the smallest admissible evidence request, experiment, owner decision, correction, or remapping action.
7. Reassess affected requirements, architecture, initiatives, contracts, controls, training, measures, tests, and evidence.
8. Reissue the architecture with explicit supersession, conditions, residual risk, and reopening triggers.
9. Escalate value conflicts, missing authority, affected-party harm, or unresolved governing obligations to the authorized human.

A denial or safe halt must identify the blocker, consequence, safe remaining work, minimum resolution, evidence required for reconsideration, and human decision point.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Business Architecture is complete for its declared scope only when:

- Scope, authority, sources, current state, target state, transition state, assumptions, unknowns, and exclusions are explicit.
- Mission, strategy, stakeholder outcomes, value propositions, value streams, capabilities, decisions, processes, information, products, services, systems, resources, controls, measures, investments, and lifecycle obligations are traceable.
- Current-state claims are supported by current evidence and remain distinct from planned or inferred state.
- Capability and value-stream definitions are stable enough for downstream use and have owners, dependencies, evidence, and lifecycle states.
- Decision rights, accountability, delegation, approvals, exceptions, risk acceptance, and final human authority are explicit.
- Business information, records, evidence, privacy, retention, and translation responsibilities are defined.
- Target and transition decisions include alternatives, tradeoffs, dependencies, costs, risks, affected parties, reversibility, adoption, support, recovery, and retirement.
- Measures distinguish activity, output, outcome, benefit, harm, control effectiveness, resilience, and evidence quality.
- Required review, verification, hostile review, and owner acceptance have occurred or remain truthfully open.
- Dissent, exceptions, unresolved decisions, missing evidence, and reopening triggers remain visible.
- Downstream workers can proceed without inventing material business meaning, authority, ownership, or acceptance criteria.
