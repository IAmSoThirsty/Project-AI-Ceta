SECTION 205 — Secrets Management

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Control non-public credentials, tokens, passwords, connection material, certificates, signing material, configuration secrets, and other sensitive values across creation, use, storage, rotation, exposure, recovery, and retirement.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A secrets lifecycle with inventory, classification, ownership, approved storage, retrieval, least privilege, injection, rotation, detection, redaction, incident response, recovery, and destruction for every declared environment and consumer.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Secrets are frequently copied into code, logs, images, tickets, prompts, backups, and developer machines. Exposure can create immediate access while rotation can silently break services. A secret store does not prove that consumers, replicas, caches, or historical copies are controlled.

APPLICABILITY

Apply whenever a non-public value can authenticate, authorize, decrypt, sign, connect, identify, configure, or otherwise grant access to a system, service, device, data store, provider, or person.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability only when no sensitive value, credential, token, certificate, connection, signing material, or secret lifecycle is affected and an approved exact baseline applies.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Use IAM, authentication, authorization, cryptography, key, application, platform, source-control, build, deployment, logging, privacy, incident, retention, and operations baselines. Identify owners, consumers, environments, providers, and emergency access authority.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Secret inventory, type, owner, purpose, consumers, environments, source, storage, injection, permissions, lifetime, rotation, detection, redaction, backups, logs, exposure history, recovery, and destruction requirements.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Owners approve secret purpose, storage, access, rotation, emergency retrieval, exceptions, exposure response, and destruction. Humans authorize recovery and decide whether a suspected exposure requires service isolation, user notification, or legal response.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may inspect authorized metadata, configuration references, redacted logs, dependency records, and secret lifecycle evidence; identify likely exposure paths; draft remediation and tests; and explain bounded impact. AI must not request, print, store, copy, infer, or place secret values into prompts, code, logs, reports, or memory. It must not rotate, revoke, retrieve, or distribute secrets without explicit authorized tooling and human control.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Security Auditor, Identity Engineer, Cryptography Engineer, Key Management Engineer, Architect, Implementer, Tester, Verifier, Governance Reviewer, Researcher, Critic, Documentation Writer, and Memory Curator.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Discover and classify secrets; assign owners and consumers; remove hard-coded values; select storage and injection; minimize exposure; enforce access and redaction; rotate and revoke; scan code, artifacts, logs, and backups; handle incidents; test failure and recovery; retire and destroy; record exceptions and evidence.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Use metadata, fingerprints, redaction, or placeholders rather than secret values. Show owner, purpose, consumer, environment, age, state, evidence, and impact. Require human review for exposure response, emergency retrieval, rotation, revocation, notification, and destruction.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Separate secret authority, storage, retrieval, injection, application use, audit, rotation, detection, backup, and destruction. Prefer short-lived, scoped values; avoid secrets in source, images, command lines, telemetry, prompts, and crash artifacts; define behavior when the store is unavailable.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show secret identifier, class, owner, purpose, consumers, environment, age, rotation, exposure status, evidence freshness, and authorized rotate, revoke, quarantine, recover, and destroy actions without rendering the value.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Secret taxonomy and inventory; storage and injection design; access and redaction policy; rotation and revocation procedures; exposure response; scan and test results; exception, decision, evidence, recovery, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify secret class and non-sensitive identifier, owner, purpose, consumer, environment, storage, policy version, method, time, result, and limitation. Evidence must never reproduce the value; a clean scan proves only its coverage and detection rules.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify source, image, log, prompt, backup, runtime, and developer-workstation exposure controls; access restriction; injection; rotation; revocation; redaction; audit; outage behavior; and recovery. Use synthetic test values where testing requires a secret.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Hard-coded credentials, shared long-lived tokens, secrets in logs or tickets, broad read access, untracked copies, rotation without consumer coordination, false-positive scans that train teams to ignore findings, and secret values included in AI context.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Treat suspected exposure as potentially real until bounded evidence shows otherwise. Preserve forensic records without copying the value, revoke or rotate, isolate consumers, remove replicas, notify owners, and validate replacement. Reassess service continuity, user impact, legal duties, backups, and retention before closure.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Secrets, owners, consumers, environments, storage, access, injection, rotation, detection, recovery, destruction, evidence, tests, exceptions, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending independent verification and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve non-sensitive identifiers, lifecycle records, consumer dependencies, exposure history, rotations, evidence, decisions, exceptions, recovery, and open gaps. Never place secret values in continuity artifacts. Hand off to IAM, Cryptography, Keys, Application, Platform, Operations, Security Operations, and Incident Response.
