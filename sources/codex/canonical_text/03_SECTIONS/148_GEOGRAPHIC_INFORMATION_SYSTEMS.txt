SECTION 148 - Geographic Information Systems

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define governed engineering of systems that capture, represent, analyze, visualize, query, exchange, and act on geographic or spatial information with explicit coordinate, temporal, privacy, accuracy, and authority controls.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A GIS baseline with spatial reference, data lineage, accuracy, access, analysis, visualization, update, interoperability, evidence, and recovery rules.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Spatial error, stale boundaries, misleading maps, and exposed locations can cause physical harm, exclusion, privacy loss, or invalid decisions.

APPLICABILITY

Applies to mapping, geocoding, remote sensing, routing, spatial analytics, cadastral, environmental, logistics, asset, and location-aware systems.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

May be non-applicable to systems with no location, geometry, coordinate, map, spatial query, or geography-dependent outcome. Record scope, owner, evidence, and reopening trigger.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Requires spatial purpose, coordinate reference, data ownership, accuracy, temporal validity, privacy, security, interoperability, accessibility, visualization, and decision authority.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Provide geometries, coordinates, projections, units, sources, resolution, time, uncertainty, privacy zones, analysis, routing, users, and failure requirements.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Humans approve data collection, location privacy, boundaries, accuracy, public use, safety, and acceptance. Domain authorities retain control over official geographic claims.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may geocode, classify imagery, suggest routes, and detect spatial patterns. It must expose uncertainty, preserve source and coordinates, and never infer sensitive location or direct action without authority.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

GIS Architect, Domain Expert, Researcher, Analyst, Privacy Reviewer, Security Auditor, Accessibility specialist, Implementer, Tester, Verifier, Governance Reviewer, Documentation Writer, Memory Curator, and Red Team. Existing role contracts remain controlling.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Define spatial scope, reference systems, sources, accuracy, time, users, and authority.
2. Design capture, transform, store, index, analyze, visualize, route, share, and delete paths.
3. Verify coordinates, topology, accuracy, privacy, accessibility, performance, interoperability, and recovery.
4. Monitor source and boundary drift, correct, publish, restrict, and retire layers safely.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show layer, source, projection, time, accuracy, sensitivity, analysis, uncertainty, and consequence. Human approval is required for official boundaries, sensitive location use, and physical action.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Separate sensor, source, coordinate, projection, geometry, topology, index, analysis, map, route, cache, and physical effect. Preserve lineage, precision, temporal validity, and privacy masking.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show map and layer versions, source freshness, projection, accuracy, access, analysis, incidents, and controls to restrict, correct, mask, roll back, or retire data.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

GIS charter, layer and source catalog, spatial reference and accuracy record, privacy assessment, data lineage, analysis and map design, interoperability tests, runbook, and retirement package.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify layer, source, coordinate system, timestamp, resolution, method, result, uncertainty, and limits. Separate positional accuracy, semantic validity, visualization, privacy, and decision evidence.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify coordinate and unit handling, projections, topology, geocoding, routing, stale layers, missing data, privacy masking, accessibility, interoperability, source loss, and recovery.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Mixing coordinate systems; false precision; stale boundaries; exposing sensitive locations; map visual bias; routing without validation; and treating a map as the territory.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Restrict affected layers, preserve source and lineage, use an authoritative prior layer, correct or withdraw maps, compensate decisions, and reopen accuracy, privacy, and authority review.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Spatial reference, source, accuracy, privacy, authority, evidence, recovery, and lifecycle are explicit. Keep CONTENT-COMPLETE until independent verification and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve layer identity, coordinates, projections, sources, analyses, decisions, incidents, evidence, and next safe action. Hand off to Data, Search, Privacy, Security, Accessibility, Routing, Testing, and Operations.
