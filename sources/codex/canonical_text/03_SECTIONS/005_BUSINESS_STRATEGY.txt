SECTION 005 — Business Strategy

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Translate the ratified mission and evidenced opportunity into a coherent set of choices about where to participate, whom to serve, how value will be created and sustained, which capabilities and partnerships are required, what will not be pursued, and how the organization will remain viable without violating mission or governance boundaries.

Business strategy is not a revenue forecast, feature list, or collection of ambitions. It is an evidence-bounded choice system connecting outcomes, economics, capabilities, risk, sequencing, and review.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- A strategy statement defining chosen arena, intended customers or beneficiaries, value mechanism, differentiation, and explicit exclusions.
- A business model or sustainability model with assumptions, cost and resource drivers, revenue or funding logic where applicable, and evidence quality.
- A capability and dependency map showing what must exist internally, what may be partnered or purchased, and what remains unknown.
- Strategic options evaluated under consistent human-authorized criteria, including no-action and staged options.
- A risk-adjusted sequence of strategic bets, experiments, decision gates, and stop conditions.
- Mission, stakeholder, legal, ethical, security, privacy, accessibility, and human-AI implications made explicit.
- A strategy review and change-control process tied to evidence and environmental change.

WHY THIS MATTERS

A technically compelling system can fail because its operating model, costs, incentives, channels, obligations, or dependencies were never made real. The reverse is also dangerous: a financially attractive plan can pressure the product and architecture to violate the mission, exploit users, weaken safety, or hide externalities.

AI can accelerate market synthesis, modeling, and option generation, but it can also produce convincing fictional demand, unsupported unit economics, and pseudo-precision. Governed business strategy keeps estimates traceable, preserves no-go boundaries, and ensures that human judgment controls the choices that affect livelihoods, relationships, and long-term obligations.

APPLICABILITY

- The work must choose markets, customer groups, channels, partnerships, funding, pricing, or operating models.
- A mission or product requires sustainable resources beyond a one-time build.
- Multiple strategic alternatives compete for scarce capital, time, talent, infrastructure, or attention.
- Vendor, platform, model-provider, supply-chain, or regulatory dependencies materially shape viability.
- The business model may affect privacy, autonomy, safety, accessibility, labor, or human-AI interaction.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

- A bounded internal task inherits an approved business strategy and creates no material strategic choice.
- The authorized owner has requested only factual analysis, not strategy selection.
- The mission, user need, market evidence, or legal context is too uncertain to support strategic commitment.
- The proposed strategy depends on deceptive, prohibited, unconsented, or unverifiable practices.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

- Ratified mission direction and protected invariants from Section 004.
- Research, foresight, market, competitive, stakeholder, user, and domain evidence proportional to consequence.
- An authorized strategy owner and explicit decision criteria.
- Known resource, funding, legal, contractual, security, privacy, accessibility, and operational constraints.
- A clear distinction between measured facts, estimates, forecasts, scenarios, and assumptions.

REQUIRED INPUTS

- Mission, outcomes, customer or beneficiary evidence, and unmet needs.
- Market structure, alternatives, competitors, substitutes, pricing or funding evidence, and channel conditions.
- Current capabilities, assets, intellectual property, workforce, technology, data, partners, and constraints.
- Cost drivers, resource capacity, time horizon, cash or funding limits, and risk tolerance.
- Legal, policy, ethics, privacy, security, safety, accessibility, and jurisdictional obligations.
- Scenario assumptions, dependency fragility, points of no return, and evidence that would change the strategy.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

The human strategy owner chooses commitments, acceptable tradeoffs, resource allocation, and risk. AI analysis remains advisory unless a narrower authority is explicitly delegated.

- Define strategic priorities, mandatory constraints, acceptable economic risk, and protected stakeholders.
- Authorize criteria and weighting rather than allowing an AI to infer priorities from available data.
- Approve commitments involving capital, employment, partnerships, customer promises, regulated activity, or irreversible dependency.
- Reject business models that undermine consent, user control, safety, privacy, accessibility, or mission integrity.
- Decide which human-AI relationship model the business may support and what commercialization boundaries apply.
- Review and amend strategy when evidence, mission, law, capability, or market conditions change.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

The AI may structure evidence, compare options, model scenarios, and reveal dependencies. It may not fabricate demand, willingness to pay, legal clearance, partner commitment, cost certainty, or decision authority.

- Separate current observations from forecasts, assumptions, and recommended choices.
- Show the evidence and sensitivity behind market size, cost, capacity, revenue, adoption, and timeline estimates.
- Include substitutes, no-action, staged entry, partnership, build, buy, and exit options where relevant.
- Identify incentive conflicts and ways the proposed model could pressure unsafe or deceptive product behavior.
- Expose provider, platform, data, model, supply-chain, and jurisdiction dependencies.
- Avoid optimizing a strategy solely for revenue, engagement, growth, or cost when protected mission outcomes constrain those metrics.
- Escalate value conflicts and material commitments to the authorized human.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Primary roles:

- Analyst — compares strategic options, economics, capabilities, dependencies, risks, and consequences.
- Researcher — establishes market, customer, competitor, regulatory, and operating evidence.
- Decision Arbiter Advisory — evaluates options under explicit human-authorized criteria and preserves uncertainty and dissent.

Common supporting roles:

- Architect — maps capability, platform, integration, data, trust, and operational implications.
- Planner — sequences strategic bets, experiments, gates, resources, and contingencies.
- Coordinator — integrates workstreams without losing evidence provenance or specialist disagreement.
- Memory Curator — preserves strategy versions, assumptions, commitments, and review triggers.
- User Experience Reviewer — tests whether the economic model undermines agency, comprehension, accessibility, or complete journeys.

Challenge and assurance roles where consequence warrants:

- Critic — attacks framing, hidden assumptions, omitted alternatives, and unsupported confidence.
- Verifier — checks artifact identity, evidence integrity, acceptance boundaries, and the exact status of claims.
- Governance Reviewer — checks objective fidelity, authority, delegation, policy, evidence, and human decision boundaries.
- Security Auditor — evaluates strategic exposure from vendors, data, infrastructure, identities, and external dependencies.
- Red Team — attacks business-model abuse, incentive failures, platform dependence, adversarial market response, and catastrophic assumptions.

Role selection must use the smallest sufficient set, preserve chamber independence where review is required, and leave every source-defined role contract unchanged.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Strategic question and authority
State the choice to be made, owner, scope, horizon, constraints, criteria, and decision deadline without inventing urgency.

2. Arena and beneficiary definition
Define where the organization will act, whom it will serve, what outcome is offered, and who may be affected or excluded.

3. Value and differentiation analysis
Explain why the offering should matter relative to substitutes, and separate evidence from aspiration.

4. Business-model construction
Map value creation, delivery, capture or funding, costs, channels, partners, obligations, and failure conditions.

5. Capability and dependency analysis
Identify required internal capabilities, external dependencies, build-buy-partner choices, bottlenecks, and lock-in.

6. Option set and criteria
Evaluate materially distinct strategies consistently. Mandatory constraints cannot be traded away by aggregate scoring.

7. Economic and resource modeling
Use ranges, assumptions, sensitivity, and scenario analysis. Avoid unsupported point estimates and false precision.

8. Risk and incentive review
Assess legal, security, privacy, safety, accessibility, labor, trust, fraud, abuse, reputational, and mission effects.

9. Experiment and gate design
Define staged commitments and cheapest useful tests before irreversible investment.

10. Operating-model alignment
Map roles, governance, decision rights, evidence flows, customer commitments, and continuity needed to execute the strategy.

11. Decision and communication
Record selected, conditional, deferred, rejected, or no-action state with rationale, approvals, and evidence that would change it.

12. Review and exit
Define review cadence, trigger events, exit conditions, customer or stakeholder obligations, and recovery from strategic failure.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

- AI-generated forecasts must show assumptions, ranges, source dates, and model limitations.
- The human must be able to change criteria and immediately see which recommendations depend on those priorities.
- Recommendations must remain distinguishable from approved commitments and executed transactions.
- The system must surface when commercial incentives conflict with mission, user welfare, privacy, safety, or governance.
- Customer, partner, investor, employee, or community commitments must not be inferred from silence or informal conversation.
- Any AI-mediated negotiation or commercial action requires explicit identity, authority, scope, and approval controls.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

The work must expose architectural obligations without treating a strategy, analysis, or plan as implemented architecture.

- Identify business-critical capabilities and map them to later enterprise, business, solution, data, application, platform, and operational architecture.
- Model cost, capacity, vendor, model-provider, data, licensing, and supply-chain assumptions as versioned dependencies.
- Preserve strategic flexibility through modular boundaries, portable data, exit plans, and avoidance of unnecessary lock-in.
- Ensure monetization and analytics paths do not bypass consent, privacy, authorization, or evidence controls.
- Support staged rollout, feature or market isolation, spending limits, and reversible commitments.
- Instrument mission and user outcomes alongside financial and operational metrics.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

The owner-facing operating surface or equivalent durable record should expose:

- Current strategy version, owner, mission link, horizon, assumptions, and review date.
- Strategic options, criteria, mandatory constraints, sensitivity, and dissent.
- Resource commitments, costs, dependencies, partner states, risks, and decision gates.
- Measured outcomes separated from forecasts, targets, and model-generated projections.
- Commercial actions requiring human approval and the evidence available before approval.
- Drift alerts when economics, incentives, dependencies, or implementation diverge from the approved strategy.
- Exit, rollback, customer-obligation, and continuity plans.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

- Business strategy statement and choice record.
- Business or sustainability model with assumption ledger.
- Market, beneficiary, value, channel, and differentiation analysis.
- Capability, dependency, partner, vendor, and lock-in map.
- Economic model with scenarios and sensitivity.
- Strategic option comparison and decision record.
- Risk, incentive, compliance, and mission-alignment assessment.
- Staged experiment, investment, review, and exit plan.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

- Current traceable market, customer, cost, capacity, competitor, regulatory, and partner evidence.
- Source and method for every material estimate or forecast.
- Documented human criteria, approvals, commitments, and risk acceptance.
- Evidence supporting capability claims and distinguishing available from planned capabilities.
- Scenario and sensitivity results showing where the strategy is fragile.
- Evidence of stakeholder, user, security, privacy, accessibility, and mission impacts.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

- Trace each major strategic choice to mission, evidence, criteria, and authority.
- Change a critical cost, adoption, timing, or dependency assumption and inspect whether the recommendation changes.
- Attempt to select an option violating a mandatory legal, safety, privacy, or mission constraint; selection must fail.
- Remove a key provider or partner and verify the dependency and exit plan become visible.
- Confirm financial or growth success cannot be displayed as mission or user success without corresponding evidence.
- Verify unapproved AI recommendations cannot create binding commercial commitments.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

- Strategy as goals, slogans, forecasts, or feature lists without choices and exclusions.
- Fictional market demand, customer commitment, willingness to pay, cost, or partner readiness.
- False precision in forecasts and unit economics.
- Revenue or engagement optimization that erodes mission, consent, trust, safety, or user control.
- Ignoring substitutes, no-action, exit, or staged options.
- Treating technical capability as operational, legal, or commercial readiness.
- Hidden platform, model, data, vendor, or jurisdiction lock-in.
- Continuing a failed strategy to protect sunk costs or prior narratives.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Strategic recovery contains commitments, restores truthful state, and reopens the first invalid assumption or decision.

- Pause new commitments while preserving existing customer, employee, partner, legal, and safety obligations.
- Identify which assumptions, evidence, incentives, capabilities, or dependencies changed or were wrong.
- Recalculate affected economics, capacity, timelines, and risks using current evidence.
- Narrow, stage, pivot, partner, divest, exit, or retire the affected strategy through human authorization.
- Communicate material changes truthfully to affected parties and preserve decision rationale.
- Propagate the revised strategy to portfolio, program, product, requirements, architecture, operations, and continuity records.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

- The strategy makes explicit choices about arena, beneficiaries, value, capabilities, resources, and exclusions.
- Economics, forecasts, and dependencies are bounded by traceable evidence and uncertainty.
- Mission, legal, ethical, security, privacy, accessibility, and human-AI constraints are integrated.
- Staged decisions, stop conditions, review triggers, and exit obligations are defined.
- The authorized human owns commitments and value tradeoffs.
- The complete strategy package can be independently reviewed and handed off without conversational memory.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

- Transfer the approved strategy version, mission link, decision owner, criteria, and authority limits.
- Carry forward assumptions, forecasts, dependencies, commitments, risks, and evidence states.
- Map strategic capabilities and outcomes to portfolio, program, product, requirements, and architecture work.
- Preserve rejected alternatives and conditions that would reopen them.
- Identify customer, partner, regulatory, financial, and data obligations that constrain implementation or exit.
- State the next safe decision and whether execution may proceed.

SOURCE BASIS AND UNRESOLVED SOURCE GAPS

This draft applies the supplied requirements for explicit objectives, source-grounded facts, uncertainty and assumption typing, consistent option comparison, human-authorized criteria, short- and long-term effects, sensitivity, reversibility, dissent, bounded roles, evidence, hostile review, and continuity. The Owner's Cockpit material adds decision support without decision seizure, trust calibration, complete owner journeys, visible limitations, and truthful status. The V4 draft adds obligation structure, resolution experiments, proof-carrying decisions, and trusted transitions.

Unresolved source gap: the supplied corpus does not include a dedicated finance, accounting, market-sizing, or business-model standard. Economic methods must therefore remain explicitly sourced and later reconciled with Market Research, Competitive Intelligence, Feasibility Analysis, legal, financial operations, revenue operations, and audit sections.
