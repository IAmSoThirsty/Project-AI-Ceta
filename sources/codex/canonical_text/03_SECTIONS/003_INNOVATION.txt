SECTION 003 — Innovation

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Convert a meaningful unmet need, newly available capability, or changed condition into a bounded, testable opportunity without confusing novelty, idea volume, or technical possibility with legitimate value.

Innovation in Thirsty's Codex is governed exploration. It creates room for imagination while preserving objective fidelity, human authority, evidence, reversibility, safety, and continuity. An innovation may be technical, operational, organizational, experiential, commercial, scientific, or relational. It becomes actionable only after its problem, beneficiaries, constraints, hypotheses, risks, and proof burden are explicit.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- An innovation brief stating the opportunity, affected parties, desired outcome, exclusions, and decision owner.
- A current-state and prior-art baseline sufficient to distinguish a real gap from rediscovery or misunderstanding.
- Multiple materially different solution hypotheses, including the no-change option and at least one reversible experiment where practical.
- Explicit assumptions, unknowns, failure hypotheses, dependencies, ethical constraints, and evidence thresholds.
- Selection criteria authorized by the human rather than inferred from model preference.
- A smallest-safe experiment plan with expected information gain, resource limits, stop conditions, and recovery.
- A decision record to continue, revise, hold, reject, transfer, or escalate the opportunity.
- A continuity package allowing another human or AI role to reproduce what was considered and why.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Generative systems can produce an enormous number of plausible ideas faster than those ideas can be evaluated. That abundance creates a new failure mode: novelty theater, where fluent possibility is mistaken for usefulness, originality, feasibility, or permission.

Governed innovation protects creativity from both premature rejection and premature implementation. It allows humans and AI to explore beyond current practice while ensuring that experiments do not quietly become production, prototypes do not become claims, and a compelling demonstration does not erase affected people, operational reality, or downstream consequences.

APPLICABILITY

- A problem remains materially unsolved or existing solutions fail important users, environments, or constraints.
- A new technology, model, material, method, dataset, interface, or organizational capability may change what is feasible.
- A mission or product requires a step-change rather than routine optimization.
- Existing assumptions have failed and alternative approaches must be explored.
- A proposed capability may alter human-AI roles, authority, trust, work, or responsibility.
- The work includes experimentation whose results may affect later strategy, architecture, or investment.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

- The required change is already understood and belongs to maintenance, implementation, or remediation.
- The objective is merely to imitate a known solution without a distinct problem or constraint.
- The activity is unbounded brainstorming with no decision, experiment, or handoff purpose.
- Required authority, consent, safety controls, or protected environments for experimentation do not exist.
- The claimed innovation depends on facts or access that have not been established and cannot yet be safely tested.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

- Section 001 Research sufficient to establish the actual problem and current state.
- Section 002 Foresight / Futures where long-horizon consequences, path dependence, or emerging capabilities matter.
- An authorized human objective, experiment boundary, protected assets, prohibited actions, and final decision owner.
- A source and prior-art search proportional to any novelty or originality claim.
- A risk, consent, privacy, security, and safety precheck appropriate to the experiment.
- A place to preserve assumptions, evidence, dissent, decisions, and next safe action.

REQUIRED INPUTS

- Problem statements, user or owner evidence, current workflows, failure reports, and unmet outcomes.
- Relevant technical, scientific, market, operational, legal, ethical, accessibility, and environmental constraints.
- Existing alternatives, prior experiments, patents or licenses where applicable, and reasons prior approaches failed.
- Available people, agents, tools, data, environments, budget, time, and authority.
- Success criteria, unacceptable harms, reversibility requirements, and evidence needed for adoption.
- Known uncertainties, stakeholder disagreement, and conditions that would invalidate the opportunity.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

The authorized human defines why exploration is legitimate and retains authority over value, consequence, adoption, and risk.

- Confirm the need, intended beneficiaries, values, non-negotiable constraints, and acceptable experiment exposure.
- Authorize selection criteria and decide material tradeoffs that depend on values or mission.
- Approve access to people, protected data, controlled systems, physical resources, or external parties.
- Distinguish curiosity from commitment and prototype permission from deployment permission.
- Decide whether residual uncertainty is acceptable for the next bounded step.
- Pause or revoke exploration when consequences, cost, scope, or evidence no longer match the authorization.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

The AI may expand the option space and reduce uncertainty, but it may not manufacture novelty, authority, demand, consent, or success.

- Preserve the human's problem and constraints before proposing solutions.
- Generate materially different options rather than superficial variations of one favored design.
- Identify which ideas are derived from supplied sources, common patterns, inference, or speculative synthesis.
- Avoid claiming originality, patentability, market demand, feasibility, safety, or user acceptance without the corresponding evidence.
- Expose hidden prerequisites and the cheapest safe experiments capable of resolving them.
- Separate simulation, prototype behavior, and measured real-world evidence.
- Report negative and inconclusive results without reframing them as progress.
- Do not autonomously widen experiments, enroll participants, access protected systems, or convert a prototype into consequential operation.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Primary roles:

- Researcher — establishes the problem, current state, prior art, source quality, and unresolved evidence.
- Analyst — compares alternatives, assumptions, effects, costs, risks, and decision implications.
- Architect — identifies structural feasibility, boundaries, interfaces, invariants, and implementation consequences without treating the concept as built.

Common supporting roles:

- Planner — defines staged experiments, dependencies, gates, rollback, and completion evidence.
- User Experience Reviewer — examines whether the opportunity improves actual journeys, agency, comprehension, accessibility, and recovery.
- Observer — captures experiment state and outcomes without unsupported interpretation.
- Coordinator — assigns bounded workstreams and preserves provenance across parallel exploration.
- Decision Arbiter Advisory — compares options under human-authorized criteria and keeps recommendations advisory unless authority is explicit.

Challenge and assurance roles where consequence warrants:

- Critic — attacks framing, hidden assumptions, omitted alternatives, and unsupported confidence.
- Verifier — checks artifact identity, evidence integrity, acceptance boundaries, and the exact status of claims.
- Governance Reviewer — checks objective fidelity, authority, delegation, policy, evidence, and human decision boundaries.
- Security Auditor — identifies experimentation threats, data exposure, trust-boundary changes, and controls required before testing.
- Red Team — attacks misuse paths, seductive demonstrations, bypasses, hidden externalities, and failure under adversarial conditions.

Role selection must use the smallest sufficient set, preserve chamber independence where review is required, and leave every source-defined role contract unchanged.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Opportunity framing
Record the actual unmet outcome, who experiences it, present evidence, scope, exclusions, and why existing approaches are insufficient.

2. Baseline and prior-art examination
Inspect current systems, workflows, alternatives, previous experiments, relevant sources, and known constraints before claiming a new category or capability.

3. Claim and assumption graph
Separate observations, stored facts, inferences, predictions, disputed claims, assumptions, and unknowns. Link each material claim to prerequisites and reversal conditions.

4. Divergent option creation
Create genuinely different approaches, including process, policy, interface, architecture, non-technical, and no-action options where relevant.

5. Constraint and harm screening
Reject or narrow concepts that violate authority, law, privacy, consent, security, safety, accessibility, mission, or irreversible consequence limits.

6. Selection design
Use human-authorized criteria. Mandatory constraints remain pass/fail boundaries and cannot be outweighed by aggregate novelty or benefit scores.

7. Failure-hypothesis design
State the most likely reasons each leading concept may fail, what evidence would distinguish those failures, and which upstream failure would invalidate later testing.

8. Minimum-resolution experiment
Select the lowest-cost, lowest-consequence experiment that can materially change the decision. Define environment, inputs, expected observations, stop conditions, and cleanup.

9. Prototype or simulation
Build only to the fidelity necessary for the question. Label synthetic data, simulated behavior, mocked integrations, and unimplemented controls explicitly.

10. Evaluation
Compare observed outcomes to criteria, include negative evidence and dissent, and assess human, operational, security, governance, cost, and lifecycle implications.

11. Adoption decision
Return a bounded state: continue, revise, limited pilot, seek evidence, hold, reject, or escalate. Do not return universal success.

12. Continuity preservation
Record source identities, versions, experiment conditions, results, rejected options, unresolved risks, and next safe action.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

- The AI must label ideation, recommendation, simulation, requested action, executed experiment, and verified result as different states.
- The human must be able to correct the problem framing before ideas are ranked or implemented.
- AI-generated enthusiasm, confidence, or volume must not substitute for human priority or evidence.
- Where people participate, consent, withdrawal, privacy, compensation, accessibility, and data use must be explicit before the experiment.
- Where an AI system is an actor or affected component, its technical capability, granted authority, provenance, constraints, and fallback must be visible.
- Consequential experiments require reviewable action contracts and must remain pausable and revocable.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

The work must expose architectural obligations without treating a strategy, analysis, or plan as implemented architecture.

- Use isolated sandboxes, synthetic or minimized data, feature flags, staged exposure, and bounded credentials where practical.
- Keep experiment paths separate from production authority and source-of-truth state.
- Design instrumentation around the hypothesis and failure conditions, not vanity activity metrics.
- Preserve modularity and reversibility so a failed concept can be removed without orphaning data or authority.
- Record external dependencies, provider assumptions, intellectual-property or licensing obligations, and lock-in risks.
- Ensure prototypes cannot silently bypass authentication, authorization, audit, evidence, privacy, or safety controls.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

The owner-facing operating surface or equivalent durable record should expose:

- The opportunity, hypothesis, scope, current state, and decision owner.
- Candidate options, mandatory constraints, human-authorized criteria, and rejected alternatives.
- Experiment identity, environment, participants, data, cost, authority, stop conditions, and current status.
- Observed evidence separated from simulation, inference, and recommendation.
- Risks, incidents, dissent, invalidated assumptions, and points of no return.
- The explicit action available to pause, narrow, revoke, repeat, or advance the experiment.
- The boundary between prototype evidence and any later product, production, safety, or market claim.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

- Innovation brief and opportunity statement.
- Current-state and prior-art record.
- Option set with constraints and comparison criteria.
- Claim, assumption, unknown, and dissent register.
- Experiment charter and action contract.
- Prototype or simulation record with declared fidelity and limitations.
- Evidence package and evaluation report.
- Adoption or disposition decision record.
- Continuity entry and next-safe-action statement.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

- Traceable evidence that the problem or opportunity exists within the declared population and context.
- Source identity, version, date, and limitations for prior-art and technical claims.
- Exact experiment artifact, configuration, environment, inputs, procedures, timestamps, observations, and deviations.
- Participant and data-governance records where human research or protected data is involved.
- Evidence for both expected benefits and material failure or harm conditions.
- Clear classification of untested, simulated, stale, contradictory, or wrong-environment evidence.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

- Trace every leading concept to the accepted opportunity rather than to novelty alone.
- Attempt to invalidate the core assumption with a cheaper prerequisite probe before a high-cost experiment.
- Confirm mandatory constraints cannot be outweighed by scoring.
- Verify that prototype, simulation, pilot, and production states are visibly distinct.
- Attempt an unauthorized scope expansion, protected-data access, and production action; each must fail closed.
- Reproduce the experiment from the retained record or state why reproduction is not possible.
- Confirm a negative or inconclusive result remains negative or inconclusive in the owner surface and final report.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

- Solutionism: selecting a technology before establishing the problem.
- Innovation theater: rewarding activity, novelty language, demos, or idea counts without decision-relevant evidence.
- Prototype laundering: presenting mocked or simulated behavior as implemented capability.
- Novelty inflation: claiming originality or patentability without adequate prior-art work.
- Metric capture: optimizing an easy proxy while degrading the intended human outcome.
- Unbounded experimentation, silent participant enrollment, unauthorized data use, or irreversible live exposure.
- Consensus manufacture: suppressing dissent or unfavorable evidence to preserve momentum.
- Sunk-cost continuation after assumptions fail or stop conditions are reached.
- Capability-authority confusion in AI-assisted or autonomous experiments.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Innovation must be recoverable from failed hypotheses, unsafe experiments, invalid evidence, and premature adoption.

- Stop the affected experiment while preserving logs, artifacts, state, participant protections, and evidence.
- Return systems and data to the declared baseline or an explicitly approved safe state.
- Reclassify claims invalidated by failed assumptions, wrong environments, stale sources, or implementation drift.
- Record harms, incidents, unexpected effects, and dissent without sanitizing them.
- Choose whether to revise the hypothesis, reduce scope, seek missing evidence, transfer the problem, or reject the opportunity.
- Reopen earlier research, foresight, strategy, risk, or requirements work when the failure reveals an upstream defect.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

- The opportunity and intended outcome are explicit and evidence-grounded.
- Multiple materially different options and the no-action option were considered where applicable.
- Assumptions, unknowns, constraints, risks, and affected parties remain visible.
- Any experiment was authorized, bounded, observed, recoverable, and truthfully classified.
- The decision state is supported by current evidence and does not exceed it.
- Human value judgments and adoption authority remain with the authorized human.
- Artifacts, dissent, rejected options, and next safe action are durable and handoff-ready.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

- Provide the accepted opportunity, scope, authority, criteria, and disposition.
- Bind every transferred finding to source identity, experiment version, environment, and evidence state.
- Preserve rejected alternatives and the reason for rejection so they are not repeatedly rediscovered without new evidence.
- Carry forward unresolved assumptions, risks, participant obligations, IP or licensing issues, and review triggers.
- Identify the receiving section or role: strategy, feasibility, risk, requirements, architecture, product, or research.
- State whether it is safe to continue and the exact next authorized action.

SOURCE BASIS AND UNRESOLVED SOURCE GAPS

This draft applies the supplied requirements for objective fidelity, source identity, unknown-means-unknown, evidence before claims, bounded roles, human authority, adaptive reasoning depth, minimum-resolution experiments, failure hypotheses, hostile review, dissent preservation, rollback, continuity, and trusted transition. The existing role corpus supplies the Researcher, Analyst, Architect, Planner, Observer, User Experience Reviewer, challenge, governance, and advisory decision functions. The Owner's Cockpit material supplies owner jobs, complete journeys, trust calibration, visible limitations, bounded delegation, grounded action, recovery, and evidence-backed status. The V4 draft supplies claim graphs, obligation lattices, expected information gain, proof-carrying work products, and reversal conditions.

Unresolved source gap: the supplied corpus does not contain a dedicated universal innovation-management standard or domain-specific experiment taxonomy. This section therefore integrates the governing source obligations with general innovation practice and must later be reconciled with Feasibility Analysis, Risk Management, Requirements Engineering, Experimentation, intellectual-property, safety, and lifecycle-governance sections.
