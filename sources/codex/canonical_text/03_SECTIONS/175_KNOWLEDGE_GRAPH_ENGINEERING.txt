SECTION 175 — Knowledge Graph Engineering

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define governed engineering of graph-based knowledge representations that connect entities, concepts, events, claims, sources, provenance, permissions, and temporal context for authorized retrieval and reasoning.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- A graph scope, vocabulary, identity policy, relationship model, provenance model, and query contract.
- Traceable ingestion, reconciliation, inference, access, quality, update, and retirement behavior.
- Explicit distinction between sourced facts, derived relationships, hypotheses, and generated answers.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Graphs can make an inferred or stale relationship appear canonical. Identity collisions, provenance loss, and unrestricted traversal can create privacy, security, and decision harm while appearing semantically coherent.

APPLICABILITY

Applies to entity graphs, semantic search, lineage graphs, recommendation, dependency maps, retrieval systems, reasoning systems, and graph-based agents. Depth increases with identity, sensitive data, automated inference, or external action.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

May be non-applicable to isolated relational lookup with no graph semantics, inference, or cross-source identity. Record exact scope, owner, evidence date, and reopening trigger.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Requires ontology or vocabulary authority, data lineage, identity rules, access policy, quality rules, query purpose, and human acceptance. Depends on Sections 030, 031, 155, 156, 176, 177, 180, and 183.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Graph purpose; entity types; identifiers; relationship semantics; source data; provenance; temporal rules; inference rules; access; sensitivity; quality thresholds; query users; and update and deletion obligations.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Humans approve canonical concepts, identity resolution, inference classes, sensitive links, publication, access, correction, retention, and deletion. Domain owners decide whether a graph claim is authoritative or merely useful.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may extract entities, propose links, reconcile records, and generate graph queries. It must preserve evidence and uncertainty and must not create canonical identity or relationship, infer permission, or publish claims without authority.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Architect, Researcher, Analyst, Data Engineer, Ontology Engineer, Privacy Reviewer, Security Auditor, Governance Reviewer, Critic, Tester, Verifier, Red Team, Documentation Writer, and Memory Curator.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Define graph purpose, scope, users, subjects, and prohibited traversal.
2. Establish vocabulary, identity, relationship, temporal, provenance, and access rules.
3. Ingest and map sources with lineage, quality, conflict, and deletion handling.
4. Separate asserted, derived, inferred, uncertain, and generated edges.
5. Evaluate identity collision, link quality, leakage, stale data, and query behavior.
6. Validate graph answers with domain owners and affected parties.
7. Monitor updates, inference drift, access, correction, and retirement.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show nodes, edges, source, timestamp, provenance, confidence, inference path, permissions, and reviewer. Users must challenge or correct links and see source evidence. Graph traversal cannot bypass access or disclosure controls.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Version ontology, identifiers, nodes, edges, assertions, inferences, source mappings, policies, indexes, queries, and snapshots. Enforce provenance and permissions at traversal and export boundaries. Preserve temporal and deletion semantics.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show graph version, source, lineage, identity decisions, inference, conflicts, access, freshness, and queries. Allow authorized approve, correct, merge, split, restrict, quarantine, withdraw, rebuild, and retire actions.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Graph charter; entity and relationship model; vocabulary and identity policy; source and lineage map; inference register; query and access contract; quality report; privacy and security review; correction and deletion plan; and handoff.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Each graph claim identifies node and edge versions, source, assertion or inference type, method, timestamp, confidence, permission, reviewer, and limitation. A connected path is not proof that its inferred meaning is true.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Test identity collision, duplicate, stale, conflicting, unauthorized, deleted, inferred, cyclic, and poisoned relationships; verify provenance, query permissions, temporal behavior, correction, export, and independent reproduction.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Identity collapse; inferred edge treated as fact; source laundering; unrestricted traversal; stale relationships; hidden conflict; graph completeness claims from one source; and deletion that leaves derived links active.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Quarantine affected nodes or edges on provenance, identity, access, or quality failure. Preserve prior snapshot, notify owners, correct or rebuild, review downstream answers, and reopen after vocabulary, source, identity, or policy change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Purpose, graph model, source, identity, provenance, inference, access, quality, lifecycle, verification, recovery, and authority are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve graph IDs, snapshots, mappings, ontology versions, queries, evidence, decisions, corrections, incidents, dissent, and gaps. Hand off to Ontology, Semantic Systems, Data, Search, AI, Evaluation, Privacy, Security, and Operations.
