SECTION 152 — Storage Engineering

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Engineer storage systems and media so data is placed, protected, retrieved, replicated, retained, restored, migrated, and retired within declared authority and service boundaries.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A storage design covering media, namespaces, capacity, performance, durability, availability, access, encryption, lifecycle, backup, restore, portability, and disposal with owners and evidence.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Storage loss, leakage, corruption, or exhaustion can invalidate every downstream function. Capacity and redundancy diagrams do not prove recoverability or lawful deletion.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when files, objects, blocks, volumes, caches, archives, backups, or physical media carry material data or service state.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

No material storage location, data class, access, lifecycle, capacity, recovery, or disposal behavior changes and an exact approved baseline covers the work. Record the determination and reopening trigger.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Data architecture, classifications, workload, access, durability and recovery objectives, regions, suppliers, media constraints, retention, deletion, cost, environmental, security, and operational requirements.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Authorized scope; data volumes and growth; read/write patterns; latency; durability; availability; replication; backup; restore; encryption; users; regions; retention; disposal; assumptions and unknowns.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

The human owner approves storage classes, locations, risk, cost, residency, retention, deletion, recovery objectives, exceptions, and acceptance, and assigns data, security, operations, supplier, and disposal authorities.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may inspect authorized storage inventories, classifications, configurations, capacity and performance telemetry, replication state, backup and restore evidence, provider terms, and disposal records. It may compare storage options, identify capacity, durability, exposure, residency, portability, cost, lifecycle, and recovery risks, and propose designs, tests, controls, runbooks, and evidence requirements.

AI must distinguish observed media or provider behavior from inference and recommendation. It must not select a region or provider as an authority decision, expose data or keys, change storage configuration, claim durability, residency, deletion, isolation, or restoration from incomplete evidence, or approve cost, legal, security, or recovery risk. A mutation-capable proposal must identify the authorized actor, exact scope, environment, copies and dependencies affected, validation, append-only audit evidence, and forward recovery or compensation path; missing authority, evidence, or disposal coverage requires stop and owner review.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Architect, Analyst, Researcher, Implementer, Tester, Verifier, Security Auditor, Governance Reviewer, Observer, Critic, Optimizer, Documentation Writer, Memory Curator, and Red Team. Existing role contracts remain controlling.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Classify data and define storage, access, lifecycle, durability, recovery, and disposal objectives.
2. Compare media and provider options against workload, portability, residency, security, capacity, and cost.
3. Design namespaces, quotas, replication, encryption, backup, restore, monitoring, migration, and secure disposal.
4. Exercise loss, corruption, provider outage, quota exhaustion, stale replica, restore, and deletion paths.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

AI may compare authorized options and analyze telemetry, but must not choose regions, expose data, change storage, or claim durability. Recommendations must state workload, assumptions, provider, evidence, and owner decision.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Model storage classes, locations, copies, replicas, caches, backups, keys, quotas, lifecycle states, and evidence separately. Enforce identity, access, retention, deletion, integrity, portability, and recovery at storage boundaries.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show storage class, location, owner, capacity, utilization, copies, freshness, encryption, retention, incidents, cost, evidence, and authorized quota, isolate, restore, migrate, delete, and retire controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Storage landscape and classification; capacity and performance model; access and protection design; replication, backup, restore, migration, retention, disposal, test, risk, decision, evidence, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify media, provider, region, configuration, dataset, time, workload, test method, result, and limitation. A redundant copy or vendor statement is not proof of restoration, isolation, or deletion.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify authorized access, encryption, integrity, capacity thresholds, performance, replication lag, backup restoration, provider loss, portability, retention expiry, deletion, and residual-copy behavior.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Unbounded growth, hidden copies, public buckets, shared keys, untested backups, region assumptions, cache leakage, quota blindness, provider lock-in without decision, and deletion claims that omit replicas or archives.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

On loss, corruption, exposure, exhaustion, or provider failure, restrict affected access, preserve evidence, and use approved restore, failover, substitution, quota, migration, or compensation paths. Reassess data and service impact before resuming.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Storage scope, ownership, classification, capacity, access, durability, recovery, lifecycle, disposal, tests, evidence, and gaps are recorded for the exact environment. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending later gates.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Hand off storage contracts, locations, quotas, keys, monitoring, backup, restore, migration, retention, disposal, and provider obligations to Data, Database, Infrastructure, Cloud, Security, Operations, Testing, and Retirement work.
