SECTION 321 — Capacity Management

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Plan, measure, allocate, forecast, protect, and improve resources needed to meet service, user, safety, reliability, cost, and growth outcomes.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A capacity model with resource, workload, demand, headroom, forecast, threshold, cost, scaling, dependency, owner, action, and failure plan.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Capacity failure often appears as latency, errors, queue growth, data loss, or unsafe overload before a simple utilization number turns red. Forecasts are assumptions and need validation.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when resources, demand, growth, concurrency, data, staff, provider quota, or budget can limit a service or outcome.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability when no capacity, demand, resource, cost, or service objective changes and an approved exact baseline covers the scope.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Use architecture, performance, scalability, SRE, cost, provider, staffing, data, operations, and service-level baselines.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Resources, demand, workload, growth, limits, headroom, service objective, cost, dependencies, forecast, scale, and recovery.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Owners set service and cost tradeoffs, headroom, staffing, scaling, throttling, and investment. Humans accept forecast uncertainty and user impact.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may analyze authorized capacity and workload data, forecast bounded trends, and propose scale or throttle. It must expose assumptions and must not scale, shed users, or make commitments without authority.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Capacity Engineer, SRE, Performance Engineer, Architect, FinOps Analyst, Data Analyst, Operations Engineer, Tester, Verifier, and Planner.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Define service and resources; measure demand and utilization; establish headroom; forecast; test scale; plan procurement or automation; monitor; throttle or expand; review forecast error; and recover.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show workload, resource, forecast, assumption, threshold, cost, service impact, evidence, and limitation. Require human approval for scale, throttle, budget, and commitment decisions.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Use quotas, autoscaling guardrails, queues, backpressure, partitioning, caching, capacity signals, resource isolation, cost controls, and graceful degradation.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show demand, resource, headroom, forecast, cost, SLO, incidents, evidence freshness, owner, and authorized scale, throttle, defer, budget, and reopen controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Capacity charter; resource and workload model; forecast and headroom; scaling and budget plan; test, incident, decision, evidence, and continuity records.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify resource, workload, period, forecast, source, method, threshold, result, cost, uncertainty, and limitation. A utilization average or forecast proves only its conditions.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify demand model, measurement, headroom, threshold, scale, throttle, cost, dependency, queue, overload, forecast error, and recovery. Test beyond expected scale safely.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Utilization-only, no headroom, linear forecast, autoscaling cost surprise, capacity hidden in vendor quota, throttle without fairness, and commitment without evidence.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Protect critical users, throttle or scale, preserve evidence, restore service, communicate, and reassess architecture and forecast. Reopen on workload, growth, provider, cost, or service change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Resources, demand, workload, forecast, headroom, cost, service, owners, evidence, actions, limitations, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve capacity history, forecasts, incidents, scaling, budgets, decisions, evidence, exceptions, and open gaps. Hand off to Performance, SRE, Cloud, Platform, Finance, and Operations.
