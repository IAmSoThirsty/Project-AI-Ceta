SECTION 006 — Product Strategy

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define what product should exist, for whom, to produce which outcomes, under which principles and boundaries, and how evidence will guide its evolution across the lifecycle.

Product strategy connects mission and business strategy to user and owner value without collapsing into a feature backlog. It establishes product identity, target users and contexts, problems and jobs, value proposition, differentiated capabilities, experience principles, lifecycle posture, and the conditions for investment, change, or retirement.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- A product strategy statement identifying intended users, owners, operators, affected parties, contexts, problems, and outcomes.
- An evidence-backed value proposition and explicit product non-goals.
- A prioritized capability model rather than an ungoverned feature list.
- Product principles covering agency, trust, accessibility, privacy, safety, security, reliability, recovery, and human-AI interaction where applicable.
- A product hypothesis and outcome framework with anti-metrics, risks, and evidence thresholds.
- A staged product lifecycle and roadmap logic tied to learning, not arbitrary dates.
- Build, buy, integrate, partner, hold, and retire decision boundaries.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Without product strategy, feature requests, competitor imitation, model capabilities, and internal convenience become the de facto product direction. The result may function technically while failing the user's actual job, the owner's control needs, or the mission.

AI products create additional ambiguity: conversational fluency may be mistaken for capability, recommendation for authority, personalization for consent, and automation for value. Product strategy must define the intended human-AI relationship and preserve the human's ability to understand, correct, limit, recover, and stop the system.

APPLICABILITY

- A product or product family must choose users, problems, capabilities, positioning, or lifecycle direction.
- Multiple feature, platform, AI, design, or operational options compete for investment.
- The product mediates consequential decisions, data, automation, or human-AI interaction.
- Existing product behavior has drifted from mission, user evidence, or owner control.
- A new market, platform, user group, or capability requires a product-level decision.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

- A bounded implementation task inherits a current approved product strategy and changes no product outcome or principle.
- Evidence of users, problems, or context is inadequate; user and market research must precede strategic commitment.
- The requested work is a one-off internal utility with no product lifecycle, user population, or material external consequence.
- The proposed direction cannot satisfy mandatory safety, legal, privacy, accessibility, or authority constraints.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

- Mission and business strategy sufficient to define product purpose and constraints.
- Research, market, competitor, stakeholder, user, domain, feasibility, and risk evidence proportional to the decision.
- An authorized product decision owner and explicit prioritization criteria.
- Current product state, architecture, analytics, support, incident, and lifecycle evidence where a product already exists.
- A clear distinction between documented, prototyped, tested, verified, and production capabilities.

REQUIRED INPUTS

- Mission outcomes, business choices, user jobs, pain, context, accessibility needs, and recovery expectations.
- Market structure, alternatives, differentiation evidence, and changing conditions.
- Current product behavior, usage, support findings, incidents, reliability, security, privacy, and operational cost.
- Technology and AI capability evidence, limitations, provider dependencies, and governance boundaries.
- Legal, ethical, regulatory, content, localization, and distribution constraints.
- Available resources, time horizon, portfolio dependencies, and retirement obligations.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

The human product owner decides whom the product serves, what outcomes matter, which tradeoffs are acceptable, and how much authority may be delegated to product mechanisms or AI.

- Confirm intended users, owner roles, excluded contexts, product outcomes, and non-goals.
- Authorize product principles and prioritization criteria.
- Decide value conflicts among user groups, business needs, mission, safety, privacy, accessibility, and operational burden.
- Approve consequential automation, data use, pricing, release, and retirement decisions.
- Define the intended human-AI relationship, including correction, review, appeal, fallback, and stop rights.
- Reject capability claims or roadmap commitments not supported by current evidence.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

The AI may synthesize evidence, identify patterns, generate alternatives, and maintain traceability. It may not invent users, needs, consent, demand, priorities, or product acceptance.

- Separate user evidence from internal requests, model suggestions, market assumptions, and competitor imitation.
- Translate outcomes into capability hypotheses without prematurely specifying features or architecture.
- Expose which roadmap proposals depend on unverified assumptions or provider capabilities.
- Identify failure, misuse, accessibility, trust, safety, privacy, security, and recovery implications.
- Avoid treating engagement, completion rate, generated output, or model confidence as sufficient proof of user value.
- Keep recommendations advisory and route material tradeoffs to the authorized human.
- Preserve product history and the reason for accepted, rejected, deferred, and retired capabilities.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Primary roles:

- Researcher — establishes users, contexts, alternatives, product evidence, and source quality.
- Analyst — connects evidence to product outcomes, capabilities, tradeoffs, and decision implications.
- User Experience Reviewer — evaluates jobs, journeys, agency, comprehension, accessibility, failure, and recovery.

Common supporting roles:

- Architect — maps product capabilities to structural obligations and system boundaries.
- Planner — sequences discovery, experiments, roadmap gates, and product lifecycle work.
- Observer — captures actual product and user behavior without unsupported interpretation.
- Documentation Writer — preserves product principles, capability boundaries, and user-facing truth.
- Decision Arbiter Advisory — compares product options under authorized criteria.

Challenge and assurance roles where consequence warrants:

- Critic — attacks framing, hidden assumptions, omitted alternatives, and unsupported confidence.
- Verifier — checks artifact identity, evidence integrity, acceptance boundaries, and the exact status of claims.
- Governance Reviewer — checks objective fidelity, authority, delegation, policy, evidence, and human decision boundaries.
- Security Auditor — examines product threat, identity, data, abuse, and dependency implications.
- Red Team — attacks misuse, deceptive affordances, automation bias, capability overclaim, and recovery failure.

Role selection must use the smallest sufficient set, preserve chamber independence where review is required, and leave every source-defined role contract unchanged.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Product identity and boundary
State the product, its owner, users, contexts, outcomes, exclusions, and relationship to mission and business strategy.

2. Problem and job evidence
Trace each strategic product problem to user, owner, market, domain, operational, or mission evidence.

3. Value proposition
Define the change users should experience and why available alternatives are insufficient. Keep aspiration separate from demonstrated value.

4. User and actor model
Identify users, operators, owners, administrators, affected non-users, agents, external services, and authority boundaries without inventing personas.

5. Capability model
Describe durable capabilities and dependencies before decomposing into features. Mark current, planned, experimental, external, and prohibited capabilities.

6. Product principles
Establish rules for trust, agency, privacy, security, safety, accessibility, content, reliability, recovery, and human-AI interaction.

7. Outcome and anti-metric design
Define user and mission outcomes, evidence, anti-metrics, and conditions where apparent usage success may mask harm or failure.

8. Option and tradeoff analysis
Compare build, buy, integrate, partner, manual, staged, and no-action approaches under human-authorized criteria.

9. Experiment and validation path
Define the smallest prototypes, pilots, and user validation needed before commitment, with participant protection and truthful fidelity labels.

10. Roadmap logic
Sequence product bets by dependencies, information gain, risk, and readiness rather than arbitrary feature volume.

11. Lifecycle and retirement
Define support, maintenance, compatibility, migration, data retention, deprecation, sunset, and retirement obligations from the beginning.

12. Strategy decision
Record approved, conditional, deferred, rejected, or retired product direction with evidence and reopening triggers.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

- Product conversations must distinguish explanation, proposal, prioritization, authorization, execution, and verified result.
- Users and owners must be able to correct misunderstood intent and see whether AI output is generated, retrieved, inferred, simulated, or verified.
- The product must not imply AI authority, certainty, identity, memory, or capability beyond the actual runtime and granted permissions.
- Automation must remain bounded by review, correction, revocation, fallback, and recovery appropriate to consequence.
- Research and analytics must honor consent, privacy, accessibility, and data-minimization boundaries.
- The system must make unavailable, degraded, experimental, or externally blocked capabilities visible before asking the user to rely on them.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

The work must expose architectural obligations without treating a strategy, analysis, or plan as implemented architecture.

- Maintain a product-capability model traceable to requirements, services, interfaces, data, policies, evidence, and owner journeys.
- Separate product promises from provider or model capabilities that may change independently.
- Use governed action boundaries for consequential commands regardless of UI, API, conversation, agent, or scheduled path.
- Preserve authoritative state, data lineage, freshness, identity, permissions, and completion evidence.
- Support graceful degradation, manual or non-AI fallback, export, portability, rollback, and retirement where required.
- Instrument complete journeys and user outcomes rather than isolated interface events.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

The owner-facing operating surface or equivalent durable record should expose:

- Current product strategy, intended users, outcomes, non-goals, principles, and owner.
- Capability inventory with current, experimental, planned, external, degraded, and unavailable states.
- Product hypotheses, supporting evidence, assumptions, risks, and validation status.
- Roadmap options, dependencies, decisions, and evidence gates.
- User, owner, security, privacy, accessibility, support, incident, and reliability signals.
- Consequential AI or automation permissions, active delegation, fallback, and revocation.
- Lifecycle obligations, compatibility, deprecation, data, and retirement state.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

- Product strategy and product-boundary statement.
- User, owner, actor, problem, and job map.
- Value proposition and alternatives analysis.
- Capability model and state inventory.
- Product principles and human-AI interaction contract.
- Outcome, anti-metric, assumption, and risk register.
- Validation and roadmap decision framework.
- Lifecycle, support, migration, and retirement posture.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

- Traceable user, owner, market, domain, support, incident, and operational evidence.
- Current capability evidence bound to artifact, version, environment, provider, and limitations.
- Research and validation records showing inclusion, method, participant protection, and observed behavior.
- Rationale and human approval for product principles, priorities, and consequential automation.
- Outcome evidence that distinguishes user benefit from engagement or activity proxies.
- Evidence of accessibility, security, privacy, reliability, recovery, and lifecycle feasibility proportional to claims.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

- Trace each strategic capability to a user or owner outcome and source evidence.
- Remove or invalidate provider capability evidence and confirm the product claim degrades to unknown, unavailable, or experimental.
- Attempt a consequential action through UI, API, conversation, and agent paths; governance must remain equivalent.
- Run critical journeys from stopped, degraded, denied, and recovery states.
- Test whether users can distinguish recommendation from decision and generated output from executed action.
- Verify roadmap items cannot be marked complete without defined acceptance and evidence.
- Confirm deprecation or retirement preserves user data, notice, migration, and recovery obligations.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

- Feature factory behavior disconnected from user outcomes or mission.
- Invented personas, demand, user needs, acceptance, or accessibility claims.
- Copying competitors without context or problem evidence.
- Treating model fluency, demo quality, or engagement as product value.
- Capability-authority confusion and conversational text presented as executed work.
- Dark patterns, coerced consent, hidden automation, or inaccessible control paths.
- Roadmaps treated as commitments despite unresolved dependencies or evidence.
- Ignoring support, recovery, migration, compatibility, and retirement obligations.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Product-strategy recovery returns the product to evidence, user outcomes, mission, and legitimate authority after drift or invalid assumptions.

- Pause affected roadmap or release commitments while preserving safe service and user obligations.
- Identify which problem, capability, user, provider, outcome, or business assumption failed.
- Reclassify affected claims and capabilities visibly.
- Reopen user research, domain analysis, feasibility, risk, requirements, architecture, or validation as needed.
- Narrow, redesign, stage, deprecate, or retire the affected direction through human approval.
- Communicate changes and migration or recovery paths truthfully to users and operators.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

- Users, owners, contexts, problems, outcomes, non-goals, and product authority are explicit.
- Capabilities and roadmap logic are evidence-grounded and distinct from features or promises.
- Product principles cover agency, trust, accessibility, privacy, security, safety, reliability, recovery, and lifecycle.
- The intended human-AI relationship and authority boundaries are explicit where applicable.
- Validation, review, change, and retirement conditions are defined.
- The strategy can be handed to product management, requirements, architecture, design, and engineering without inventing missing intent.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

- Transfer the approved product strategy version, decision owner, users, outcomes, non-goals, and principles.
- Provide capability states, assumptions, risks, evidence, dependencies, and unresolved decisions.
- Map capabilities to product management, requirements, architecture, UX, security, operations, and lifecycle sections.
- Preserve rejected options and evidence that would reopen them.
- Carry forward provider, model, data, consent, accessibility, migration, and retirement constraints.
- State the next safe product decision and current authority to proceed.

SOURCE BASIS AND UNRESOLVED SOURCE GAPS

This draft applies the supplied requirements for owner jobs and complete journeys, objective fidelity, evidence-before-claims, current-state inspection, capability-versus-authority separation, bounded delegation, grounded dialogue, visible uncertainty, accessibility, security, privacy, recovery, continuity, and no fake success. The role corpus supplies user-experience review, research, analysis, architecture, planning, testing, verification, governance, and hostile review. V4 adds claim and obligation structure, minimum-resolution experiments, proof-carrying results, dissent, and trusted transition.

Unresolved source gap: the supplied corpus does not contain a universal product-strategy or product-discovery standard, nor a final cross-domain policy for every form of human-AI relationship. This section must later be reconciled with Product Management, UX Strategy, User Research, Requirements Engineering, AI Safety, AI Governance, Trust and Safety, and lifecycle sections.
