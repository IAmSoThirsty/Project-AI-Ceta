SECTION 008 — Program Management

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Coordinate multiple related projects, products, workstreams, and organizational changes so they collectively produce an authorized outcome that no single project can deliver alone.

Program management governs shared outcomes, benefits, dependencies, interfaces, resources, risks, decisions, and transitions across workstreams. It does not replace project ownership, specialist judgment, product authority, or human governance with a central narrative of progress.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- A program charter defining outcome, scope, exclusions, sponsors, decision rights, participating workstreams, and completion conditions.
- An integrated outcome and benefits map connected to mission, strategy, users, owners, and evidence.
- A dependency-aware program roadmap with entry, exit, validation, and transition gates.
- A shared architecture, data, environment, configuration, release, and governance alignment plan where applicable.
- Resource, capacity, risk, issue, decision, change, and stakeholder-management systems.
- Durable inter-project handoffs and one authoritative state per program object.
- An operational transition and benefits-realization plan extending beyond project delivery.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Projects can each satisfy their local scope while the program fails because interfaces, timing, adoption, operations, data, authority, or benefits were never integrated. Conversely, a program office can create fictional progress by aggregating activities and suppressing project-level failure or dissent.

Multi-agent execution magnifies this risk. Parallel agents can produce more output while fragmenting truth. Program management must preserve exact versions, assignment boundaries, dependencies, evidence, conflicts, and human decision points across the whole outcome.

APPLICABILITY

- The desired outcome requires multiple interdependent projects, products, teams, vendors, agents, or organizational changes.
- Shared architecture, data, platforms, environments, releases, customers, or operations create cross-project risk.
- Benefits or mission outcomes appear only after integration and operational adoption.
- Program-level capacity, sequencing, governance, or transition decisions are required.
- A major migration, transformation, compliance effort, platform build, or system-of-systems initiative is underway.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

- A single project can deliver and validate the complete outcome without material external dependencies.
- The work is a loose portfolio of unrelated initiatives with no shared program outcome.
- No authority exists to coordinate participating workstreams or resolve program-level dependencies.
- The proposed program merely adds reporting overhead without changing integration, decisions, risk, or outcomes.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

- An approved mission, strategy, portfolio decision, and defined program outcome.
- Authenticated sponsor, program owner, workstream owners, decision rights, and escalation paths.
- Known projects or workstreams, dependencies, commitments, constraints, and current states.
- Shared status, evidence, configuration, identity, and continuity conventions.
- A program-level risk, change, architecture, and operational-transition posture.

REQUIRED INPUTS

- Program objective, outcomes, benefits, scope, exclusions, constraints, and success evidence.
- Project and product charters, roadmaps, plans, architecture, requirements, risks, and current status.
- Stakeholder, user, organizational, vendor, regulatory, security, privacy, safety, and operational obligations.
- Shared resources, budgets, people, agents, tools, environments, platforms, data, and release windows.
- Dependency, interface, version, configuration, and integration information.
- Prior decisions, incidents, unresolved conflicts, assumptions, and conditions requiring human authority.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

The sponsor and authorized program owner retain authority over program outcomes, resource commitments, scope changes, cross-workstream tradeoffs, and acceptance of residual risk.

- Ratify the program charter, governance, workstream boundaries, decision rights, and escalation thresholds.
- Approve program-level scope, funding, capacity, vendor, release, migration, and operational commitments.
- Resolve conflicts among mission, product, project, safety, security, legal, stakeholder, and operational priorities.
- Ensure central coordination does not erase specialist findings or project truth.
- Authorize AI or agent delegation by workstream, tool, data, consequence, and duration.
- Decide program continuation, replanning, partial transition, hold, or termination.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

The AI may coordinate, analyze dependencies, maintain state, and surface conflicts only within recorded authority. It may not invent program scope, owners, resources, approvals, or completion.

- Preserve the actual program outcome and each workstream's bounded objective.
- Maintain stable identities and exact versions for deliverables, dependencies, decisions, and evidence.
- Detect conflicts, duplication, blocked dependencies, schedule assumptions, and inconsistent states.
- Continue safe independent work while isolating affected blockers.
- Avoid averaging contradictory specialist conclusions or rolling failed projects into a green program status.
- Distinguish plan, forecast, reported status, observed state, tested integration, verified outcome, and accepted benefit.
- Escalate cross-workstream value or authority conflicts to the authorized human.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Primary roles:

- Planner — creates the dependency-aware program plan, phases, gates, risks, resources, and completion evidence.
- Coordinator — assigns, synchronizes, tracks, reconciles, and hands off workstreams while preserving provenance and conflict.
- Analyst — evaluates program outcomes, benefits, dependencies, scenarios, and decision implications.

Common supporting roles:

- Architect — governs shared structure, interfaces, standards, integration, trust, and technical decision dependencies.
- Observer — captures program and workstream state without interpreting activity as success.
- Memory Curator — preserves decisions, versions, continuity, and retrievable program history.
- Documentation Writer — maintains durable charters, interface records, operating guides, and transition documents.
- Decision Arbiter Advisory — structures cross-workstream decisions under authorized criteria.

Challenge and assurance roles where consequence warrants:

- Critic — attacks framing, hidden assumptions, omitted alternatives, and unsupported confidence.
- Verifier — checks artifact identity, evidence integrity, acceptance boundaries, and the exact status of claims.
- Governance Reviewer — checks objective fidelity, authority, delegation, policy, evidence, and human decision boundaries.
- Tester — validates integrated normal, boundary, failure, and recovery behavior across workstreams.
- Security Auditor — evaluates cross-project trust, identity, data, supply-chain, and shared-infrastructure risk.
- Red Team — attacks program assumptions, dependency failure, status laundering, and integration gaps.

Role selection must use the smallest sufficient set, preserve chamber independence where review is required, and leave every source-defined role contract unchanged.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Program charter
Define outcome, scope, exclusions, sponsor, owner, decision rights, workstreams, benefits, constraints, and completion state.

2. Workstream architecture
Define stable workstream identities, owners, objectives, inputs, outputs, interfaces, authority, evidence, and acceptance.

3. Dependency graph
Classify hard, soft, approval, evidence, resource, environmental, review, and external dependencies.

4. Integrated planning
Sequence phases, parallel work, milestones, integration points, validation gates, transition events, and contingency paths.

5. Shared governance
Establish architecture, security, privacy, safety, quality, change, configuration, release, evidence, and decision processes.

6. Resource and capacity management
Use actual availability and account for review, rework, integration, operations, and external delay.

7. Risk and issue control
Maintain program-level and inherited risks, triggers, owners, responses, residual risk, and decision thresholds.

8. State and evidence management
Use one authoritative status source per object and bind status to exact artifacts, environments, tests, and approvals.

9. Integration and validation
Verify interfaces and complete journeys across workstreams, including failure, recovery, migration, and operational scenarios.

10. Stakeholder and change coordination
Manage commitments, communications, adoption, training, organizational effects, and grievance or escalation paths.

11. Operational transition
Transfer capabilities, controls, documentation, support, monitoring, ownership, and recovery into real operation.

12. Benefits and closure
Measure outcomes after transition, close or transfer residual work, preserve lessons, and avoid declaring benefit at delivery alone.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

- Every agent or human workstream assignment must include objective, scope, authority, inputs, outputs, dependencies, evidence, review, and escalation.
- Program dashboards must distinguish reported status from direct observation and verification.
- The owner must be able to trace an integrated result to exact upstream artifacts and workstream owners.
- Conflicts must remain visible until reconciled by evidence or authorized decision.
- Conversational coordination must create durable action and handoff records; chat memory alone is insufficient.
- Program-level AI may not override specialist roles, create fictional consensus, or approve its own prior work where independence is required.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

The work must expose architectural obligations without treating a strategy, analysis, or plan as implemented architecture.

- Represent the program as a dependency and evidence graph linked to exact artifacts, versions, environments, interfaces, and decisions.
- Define shared architecture and standards without collapsing legitimate bounded contexts or project ownership.
- Centralize only controls requiring consistent enforcement, such as identity, policy, evidence, configuration, and release gates.
- Provide isolated environments and integration stages to prevent one workstream from corrupting another.
- Support cross-workstream observability, incident correlation, rollback, migration, and continuity.
- Keep benefits and operational outcomes separate from build completion and deployment events.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

The owner-facing operating surface or equivalent durable record should expose:

- Program outcome, scope, sponsor, owner, decision rights, and current state.
- Workstreams, owners, authority, dependencies, deliverables, versions, and evidence.
- Integrated roadmap, capacity, risks, issues, decisions, changes, and validation gates.
- Conflicts, blocked paths, safe parallel work, and human decisions required.
- Shared architecture, security, privacy, quality, release, and operational readiness state.
- Transition, adoption, support, benefits, and residual obligation status.
- Pause, narrow, replan, revoke delegation, and safe-halt controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

- Program charter and governance model.
- Outcome, benefit, workstream, and responsibility map.
- Integrated roadmap, dependency graph, and capacity plan.
- Architecture, interface, configuration, environment, and release alignment records.
- Program risk, issue, decision, change, and dissent registers.
- Integration, validation, transition, adoption, and benefits plans.
- Program evidence package, closure report, and continuity map.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

- Current workstream state and artifact identity from authoritative records.
- Evidence for dependencies, resource availability, interfaces, and shared constraints.
- Human approvals for program-level scope, allocation, risk, release, and transition decisions.
- Integration and end-to-end results bound to exact versions and environments.
- Operational and user outcome evidence after transition.
- Visible limitations, failed workstreams, unresolved risks, and evidence gaps.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

- Restart a receiving workstream and continue from its durable handoff alone.
- Change an upstream artifact and verify dependent work and evidence are invalidated or reopened.
- Inject contradictory specialist findings and confirm they remain visible rather than averaged.
- Block one dependency and confirm unaffected safe work can continue without false program completion.
- Run integrated critical journeys through normal, denied, degraded, rollback, and recovery states.
- Verify program benefit cannot be claimed from project delivery without current outcome evidence.
- Revoke an agent delegation and confirm new consequential work stops across all paths.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

- Program as reporting layer with no authority, integration, or outcome responsibility.
- Green program status built from incomplete, blocked, failed, or unverified project reports.
- Hidden dependencies, version mismatch, duplicated work, and ambiguous ownership.
- Central coordination overriding specialist evidence or project truth.
- Invented resources, deadlines, approvals, benefits, or operational readiness.
- Integration deferred until the end, creating unrecoverable coupling and schedule collapse.
- Delivery declared complete before adoption, operations, recovery, and benefits are proven.
- Chat-dependent handoffs and untraceable agent delegation.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Program recovery isolates affected workstreams, restores authoritative state, and reestablishes a safe integrated path.

- Enter a bounded hold or safe halt for affected integrations while preserving unaffected safe work.
- Reconcile artifact versions, workstream states, dependencies, decisions, resources, and evidence.
- Identify the first invalid assumption, interface, authority, or transition.
- Replan dependencies, resources, gates, and transition scope through human approval.
- Rollback, isolate, substitute, migrate, or terminate affected workstreams as authorized.
- Update stakeholders, operations, project plans, architecture, risk, and continuity records.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

- The program outcome, workstreams, owners, authority, dependencies, and completion evidence are explicit.
- Shared governance and architecture are defined without erasing bounded responsibilities.
- Integrated behavior, operational transition, and complete journeys have current evidence.
- Benefits are measured after transition and remain distinct from delivery activity.
- Conflicts, residual risks, and unresolved decisions are visible.
- Program closure or transfer leaves a durable, reproducible continuity package.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

- Provide every workstream with exact scope, authority, dependencies, inputs, outputs, evidence, and review requirements.
- Bind downstream work to exact upstream artifact and decision versions.
- Transfer shared risks, controls, resource limits, and integration obligations.
- Preserve conflicts, failed assumptions, residual work, and reopening conditions.
- At transition, transfer operational ownership, support, monitoring, recovery, documentation, and benefits measurement.
- State current safety, readiness, and next authorized program action.

SOURCE BASIS AND UNRESOLVED SOURCE GAPS

This draft applies the supplied Planner and Coordinator contracts for bounded work, dependency classification, workstream assignment, parallelization, conflict handling, evidence, validation gates, human decisions, and continuity. It applies the Owner's Cockpit requirements for durable multi-agent handoffs, exact upstream artifact identity, one authoritative status source, visible conflicts, governed action, and owner control. V3 and V4 add objective fidelity, no fake success, obligation structure, independent review, proof-carrying results, and trusted transition.

Unresolved source gap: the supplied corpus does not define a universal benefits-realization, earned-value, or organizational-change methodology. Such methods must be selected explicitly and later reconciled with Project Management, Change Management, Operations, Product Analytics, Post-Implementation Review, and Audit.
