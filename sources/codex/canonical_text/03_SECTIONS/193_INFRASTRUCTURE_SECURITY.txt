SECTION 193 — Infrastructure Security

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Protect physical and virtual infrastructure, hosts, storage, operating environments, provisioning paths, administrative planes, and supporting facilities throughout their lifecycle.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

Infrastructure assets, trust zones, owners, hardening baselines, privileged paths, patching, monitoring, isolation, backup, recovery, and evidence are explicit and tested.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Infrastructure is a common blast-radius boundary. Weak host, image, storage, firmware, or provisioning controls can defeat application security and erase recovery evidence.

APPLICABILITY

Applies to servers, hosts, hypervisors, storage, images, operating systems, facilities, provisioning, administrative tooling, and physical or virtual infrastructure.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Only an approved baseline covering exact assets, versions, configurations, locations, privileges, and changes may justify non-applicability; labs remain limited-scope evidence.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Sections 020, 032-040, 045, 051-052, 258-286, 301-330, and 398-400 provide system, platform, network, security, resilience, infrastructure, operations, and disposal context.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Asset inventory, ownership, topology, images, firmware, configurations, identities, access, data, dependencies, patch history, vulnerabilities, telemetry, backup, restore, and facility constraints.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Infrastructure owners approve hardening, privileged access, maintenance, exceptions, outage risk, recovery, and retirement; qualified security and operations authorities must review material changes.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may inventory, compare configurations, detect drift, and propose tests. It cannot grant access, change hosts, suppress evidence, or claim security from configuration similarity or successful provisioning.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Architect, Infrastructure Engineer, Systems Administrator, Security Auditor, Network Engineer, Implementer, Tester, Verifier, Observer, Red Team, Governance Reviewer, Operations Engineer, and Documentation Writer.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Inventory hosts, storage, images, firmware, control planes, facilities, and trust boundaries.
2. Define secure baselines, least privilege, segmentation, patching, provenance, monitoring, and recovery controls.
3. Review configuration and supply-chain drift; test isolation, tampering, denial, restore, and evidence capture.
4. Record residual risk, exceptions, remediation, and lifecycle handoff.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Separate observed state from AI analysis. Human approval is required for privileged changes, isolation, outage, recovery, and exceptions; manual status and recovery remain available when automation fails.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Use immutable or attestable images, controlled provisioning, segmented management planes, secure boot where applicable, least privilege, encrypted storage, tamper-evident logs, independent backups, and recovery not dependent on the failed host.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show asset, version, location, owner, hardening, privileged sessions, drift, findings, evidence freshness, outage impact, and authorized isolate, patch, restore, or retire actions.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Infrastructure inventory, threat model, hardening baseline, access matrix, patch and image records, configuration evidence, vulnerability findings, recovery tests, exceptions, and handoff package.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify asset, version, configuration, environment, method, coverage, date, result, owner, and limits. A template, scan, or uptime signal proves only its bounded condition.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Exercise unauthorized administration, image tampering, storage disclosure, host compromise, patch failure, management-plane loss, capacity exhaustion, restore, and evidence replay.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Shared administrators, unmanaged images, unpatched hosts, public management ports, unencrypted backups, single recovery paths, ignored physical access, and treating provisioning success as security.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Isolate affected assets, revoke credentials, preserve evidence, restore known-good infrastructure, validate dependent workloads and data, and reopen when topology, firmware, provider, or threat assumptions change.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Assets, boundaries, owners, controls, evidence, tests, recovery, exceptions, residual risk, and retirement are explicit; status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending qualified review and acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve asset identity, configuration, versions, incidents, decisions, evidence, dissent, and next action. Hand off to Platform, Cloud, Network, Security Operations, Incident Response, Reliability, Backup, Testing, and Disposal.
