SECTION 028 — Functional Architecture

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define the governed structure of what a system, service, product, organization, human, AI agent, device, or enabling element must do to produce authorized outcomes under declared conditions.

Functional Architecture decomposes approved requirements, operational concepts, process obligations, capabilities, domain rules, and system behavior into coherent functions and subfunctions. It identifies inputs, outputs, transformations, triggers, states, controls, dependencies, interfaces, allocations, failure behavior, recovery, evidence, and verification without prematurely selecting implementation technology or treating a functional model as proof of runtime behavior.

Functional Architecture specializes the whole-system functional analysis established by Systems Engineering. It preserves the difference between an enduring capability, a process sequence, a required function, a design component, an implementation artifact, and an observed outcome.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- A declared functional-architecture scope identifying the system or organizational boundary, objectives, environments, users, operators, affected parties, lifecycle horizon, and authority.
- A stable functional taxonomy and hierarchy with clear parent-child meaning, function identity, purpose, inputs, outputs, conditions, constraints, owner, allocation, dependencies, evidence, and lifecycle state.
- A functional context model showing external actors, systems, services, people, devices, data, events, resources, commands, controls, authorities, trust boundaries, and evidence flows.
- A functional decomposition that covers normal, boundary, denied, degraded, failure, recovery, maintenance, migration, deprecation, and retirement behavior where applicable.
- Functional flows and behavior models connecting triggers, preconditions, transformations, decisions, outputs, states, timing, sequencing, concurrency, exceptions, and postconditions.
- Explicit allocation of functions and interfaces to human roles, AI roles, system elements, components, services, devices, organizations, external systems, processes, or enabling systems.
- Functional contracts defining inputs, outputs, units, schemas, identity, authority, timing, ordering, quality, error behavior, retry, idempotency, audit, evidence, and compatibility as applicable.
- Traceability from objectives, domain meaning, requirements, capabilities, processes, risks, controls, functions, allocations, implementations, tests, evidence, acceptance, operations, maintenance, and retirement.
- A functional-state model that distinguishes desired, defined, proposed, allocated, implemented, integrated, verified, validated, operational, degraded, blocked, superseded, and retired states.
- A functional verification and validation basis that makes claims reproducible and distinguishes analysis or model evidence from implementation and observed-behavior evidence.
- A durable handoff to Operational, Information, Data, Application, Software, Platform, Infrastructure, Integration, API, Event, Security, Privacy, Safety, Reliability, Resilience, Accessibility, Engineering, Testing, Operations, Maintenance, and Retirement work.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Requirements state what is authorized and required; process models state how work is coordinated in context. Functional Architecture makes the required behavior explicit enough to allocate, design, implement, test, verify, operate, recover, and retire. Without it, teams may implement features without a coherent behavior model, allocate a requirement to nobody, duplicate responsibility, or hide an essential control inside an assumed component.

Functional decomposition is also a powerful place for false certainty. A clean function tree can omit human judgment, authority, error handling, data provenance, accessibility, latency, recovery, or evidence. A function can be mathematically defined and still be unimplemented, untestable, unsafe, or unusable in context.

For human-AI systems, function boundaries determine whether an AI is classifying, recommending, deciding, executing, monitoring, or merely assisting. Those are materially different authorities and failure modes. Functional Architecture makes the boundary inspectable and prevents a model's ability to perform a function from becoming permission to perform it consequentially.

APPLICABILITY

- A system, service, product, organization, device, process, model, agent, or capability contains interacting behaviors, transformations, controls, decisions, or interfaces.
- Requirements must be decomposed and allocated across people, functions, systems, components, services, devices, external providers, or lifecycle activities.
- The system crosses software, hardware, data, human, organizational, domain, supplier, physical, or jurisdictional boundaries.
- Functional behavior has material security, privacy, safety, accessibility, reliability, resilience, performance, support, maintenance, recovery, or retirement consequences.
- Multiple implementations or architectural alternatives must satisfy the same outcome-level obligations.
- AI, automation, orchestration, or external services may perform, recommend, constrain, or supervise a function.
- Verification requires functional-level analysis, inspection, demonstration, simulation, testing, measurement, or traceability independent of implementation detail.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

- The work is a bounded observation, documentation correction, or reversible change with no material effect on required behavior, functional allocation, interface, control, state, evidence, user journey, or lifecycle obligation.
- A current approved functional architecture covers the exact objective, requirements baseline, version, environment, operating mode, interfaces, and change, and impact analysis confirms no material functional effect.
- The work is exploratory and makes no claim that functions are approved, allocated, implemented, integrated, verified, validated, or operational. The model must remain labeled as proposed.
- An active safety, security, privacy, integrity, legal, or authority incident requires containment before normal functional analysis can safely proceed.
- The necessary requirements, domain meaning, system boundary, or acceptance authority is unavailable. Work may continue as an evidence request or proposed analysis, but not as an accepted functional baseline.

A non-applicability decision must identify the controlling functional baseline, exact scope, version, environment, evidence date, authority, assumptions, and reopening condition. A component diagram, feature list, familiar implementation, or AI-generated decomposition is not sufficient reason to omit material functional analysis.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

The authorized human owner retains authority over objectives, required outcomes, values, mandatory constraints, system boundary, functional criticality, acceptance, risk, safety, privacy, accessibility, security, resource commitments, autonomy, and irreversible allocation or deployment decisions.

- Confirm the functional scope, system of interest, operating modes, environments, affected parties, lifecycle stages, non-goals, and acceptance authority.
- Approve or reject derived functions, decomposition boundaries, criticality, allocations, variants, constraints, exceptions, and functional baselines.
- Decide value conflicts involving human judgment, rights, safety, privacy, accessibility, dignity, employment, public impact, cost, schedule, performance, and residual risk.
- Ensure qualified specialists and affected users, operators, maintainers, and support personnel review functions where consequence requires expertise or lived experience.
- Approve AI or automation roles, autonomy levels, tool permissions, human checkpoints, fallback, revocation, and appeal paths.
- Ratify functional claims only for the exact version, configuration, environment, scope, evidence, and unresolved conditions reviewed.
- Retain the ability to stop, narrow, quarantine, roll back, restore, supersede, or retire functional work when authority, evidence, safety, or mission conditions fail.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may inspect requirements and architecture, identify candidate functions, normalize terminology, compare decompositions, generate behavior models, propose allocations, analyze dependencies, draft functional contracts, produce test ideas, simulate bounded scenarios, and identify evidence gaps within its assignment.

AI must:

- restate the authorized objective, requirements baseline, system boundary, process context, assumptions, unknowns, and consequence limits before material decomposition;
- distinguish source requirement, derived function, inferred behavior, proposed allocation, implementation fact, simulation, recommendation, execution result, and verified behavior;
- preserve source identity, version, scope, environment, method, and limitations for each material functional claim;
- identify missing inputs, outputs, actors, authority, states, error behavior, timing, resources, controls, evidence, accessibility, and recovery;
- keep function definitions at the appropriate abstraction level and avoid narrowing them to a preferred implementation without authority;
- model AI participation with explicit identity, function, authority, tool access, data, human review, monitoring, fallback, revocation, and evidence;
- expose alternatives, tradeoffs, dissent, sensitivity, reversibility, and evidence that would change an allocation or recommendation;
- stop or escalate when requirements, domain meaning, authority, mandatory constraints, or acceptance boundaries are materially unresolved.

AI must not:

- invent requirements, functions, actors, interfaces, performance, resources, integrations, supplier capability, certification, or current behavior;
- convert a feature, API, prompt, component, model benchmark, prototype, simulation, or diagram into proof of functional satisfaction;
- assign authority, approve requirements, accept risk, decide values, or bind a functional baseline without valid delegation;
- optimize local function metrics while silently degrading mission outcome, user control, security, privacy, safety, accessibility, reliability, resilience, maintainability, evidence, or retirement;
- hide human judgment, exception handling, denial, error, degraded behavior, or recovery to make the functional model appear complete;
- allow conversational, administrative, retry, import, or recovery paths to bypass functional controls or authority boundaries.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

- Architect: defines functional boundaries, decomposition, contracts, allocations, dependencies, states, interfaces, failure, recovery, and lifecycle implications.
- Analyst: examines function relationships, alternatives, completeness, feasibility, quality, dependencies, tradeoffs, and consequences.
- Researcher: gathers requirements, domain, technical, human, operational, supplier, standard, and current-behavior evidence.
- Observer: records actual behavior, inputs, outputs, states, transitions, timings, failures, and system signals without unsupported causation.
- Planner: sequences functional analysis, allocation, prototyping, integration, verification, validation, transition, and review work.
- Coordinator: synchronizes functional workstreams and resolves routing or provenance issues without seizing authority.
- Critic: challenges decomposition gaps, hidden implementation bias, circular dependencies, false completeness, and unsupported behavior.
- Security Auditor: assesses identity, authorization, trust, data, control, abuse, evidence, and security behavior at functional boundaries.
- User Experience Reviewer: evaluates user, owner, operator, maintainer, support, accessibility, explanation, correction, denial, and recovery functions.
- Tester: designs functional tests across normal, boundary, failure, degraded, recovery, compatibility, security, accessibility, and governance conditions.
- Verifier: independently checks functional claims, allocations, contracts, evidence, reproducibility, and exact scope.
- Documentation Writer: maintains functional catalogs, models, contracts, decisions, assumptions, limitations, and handoffs.
- Memory Curator: preserves functional baseline identity, traceability, versions, evidence, dissent, decisions, and continuity.
- Governance Reviewer: checks authority, requirements precedence, mandatory constraints, delegation, exceptions, acceptance, and status transitions.
- Implementer: realizes approved functions within the allocated architecture and reports deviations rather than silently changing the functional baseline.
- Refactorer: improves internal structure while preserving approved functional behavior unless change is authorized.
- Optimizer: improves measured function performance while preserving protected properties and acceptance boundaries.
- Red Team: attempts authorized bypass, misuse, malformed input, privilege escalation, model manipulation, failure, recovery, and evidence attacks.
- Decision Arbiter Advisory: compares functional alternatives under authorized criteria and preserves owner decisions.

Role selection must preserve independence between implementation and verification where a false functional claim could create consequence. Existing role contracts remain controlling.

1. Establish the functional context: objective, outcome, system boundary, environments, operating modes, users, operators, affected parties, lifecycle, non-goals, and authority.
2. Accept and inspect the exact requirements, process, capability, domain, systems, and architecture handoffs, including open findings and evidence limits.
3. Define terminology and distinguish capability, process, function, subfunction, activity, task, behavior, service, component, interface, control, requirement, and implementation.
4. Identify functional actors and subjects, including humans, AI agents, automation, rule engines, external systems, devices, services, facilities, and enabling organizations.
5. Establish the functional context and identify incoming and outgoing information, data, events, commands, resources, authority, identity, evidence, physical effects, and human communication.
6. Derive candidate functions from approved requirements, operational concepts, process obligations, capability outcomes, domain rules, risk treatments, and lifecycle needs.
7. Build a functional taxonomy and catalog with stable identities, purpose, scope, inputs, outputs, conditions, owner, criticality, status, dependencies, evidence, and lifecycle state.
8. Decompose functions using black-box and white-box views only as far as needed for responsibility, interface, risk, allocation, verification, implementation, and lifecycle decisions.
9. Define functional flows, behavior, sequences, timing, concurrency, state transitions, preconditions, guards, postconditions, exceptions, and compensation.
10. Define functional contracts for inputs and outputs, schema, units, identity, authorization, timing, ordering, quality, errors, retry, idempotency, audit, evidence, compatibility, and ownership.
11. Allocate functions to people, roles, processes, systems, components, services, devices, external providers, facilities, or enabling systems with rationale and authority.
12. Identify duplicated, missing, circular, conflicting, unowned, unreachable, unsafe, inaccessible, or unverifiable functions and allocations.
13. Allocate cross-cutting obligations for security, privacy, safety, accessibility, reliability, resilience, performance, maintainability, observability, support, migration, deprecation, and retirement.
14. Define functional variants by environment, jurisdiction, population, role, data class, risk, operating mode, product, feature state, and lifecycle stage.
15. Define function measures, thresholds, margins, evidence methods, test conditions, and invalidation rules for material quality or performance claims.
16. Develop and compare alternatives while preserving mandatory constraints outside weighted scoring and exposing sensitivity, reversibility, and residual uncertainty.
17. Define integration, transition, fallback, recovery, rollback, migration, coexistence, maintenance, deprecation, and retirement behavior at functional boundaries.
18. Validate functional meaning and allocation with owners, users, operators, maintainers, support, domain experts, and qualified specialists as applicable.
19. Hostile-review the functional architecture for omissions, unsafe assumptions, bypasses, inaccessible paths, unowned state, unsupported evidence, and implementation drift.
20. Baseline and hand off the functional architecture with exact identity, traceability, evidence boundary, unresolved decisions, change authority, and next safe action.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

- Every consequential function must disclose whether it is performed by a human, AI model, agent, automation, rule engine, external service, or hybrid arrangement.
- The owner must be able to inspect each function's purpose, source, authority, inputs, outputs, state, allocation, criticality, controls, evidence, failure, recovery, and lifecycle status.
- AI-generated functions, decompositions, allocations, contracts, simulations, and optimizations remain proposals until authorized adoption.
- The human must be able to correct terminology, scope, function boundaries, affected parties, allocation, criticality, and acceptance without losing history or dissent.
- AI recommendations that affect rights, safety, privacy, accessibility, cost, commitments, autonomy, or external state must expose alternatives, assumptions, uncertainty, reversibility, affected parties, and evidence that would change the recommendation.
- The system must distinguish defined, proposed, allocated, implemented, integrated, verified, validated, operational, degraded, blocked, superseded, and retired function states.
- Critical stop, correction, appeal, evidence inspection, fallback, and recovery functions must remain available when an AI model, provider, network, or nonessential dependency fails.
- Automated function selection, routing, prioritization, or optimization must preserve mandatory constraints and cannot suppress minority findings or human review conditions.
- No conversational path may bypass the identity, authority, policy, state, evidence, audit, accessibility, or recovery controls required by other paths.
- Long-running or autonomous functions must expose assignment, authority expiry, current state, resource use, evidence progress, stop conditions, and safe interruption behavior.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

- Represent functions, subfunctions, actors, inputs, outputs, flows, states, contracts, allocations, controls, dependencies, evidence, tests, and lifecycle states as versioned attributable records or equivalent controlled artifacts.
- Preserve traceability among objective, requirement, capability, process, domain rule, function, allocation, interface, implementation artifact, test, evidence, acceptance, operation, incident, change, and retirement.
- Maintain separate functional context, functional hierarchy, functional flow, functional behavior, functional state, functional allocation, and functional contract views with explicit consistency rules.
- Use black-box functions to describe externally required behavior and white-box decomposition to expose internal structure only when that structure is needed for allocation, risk, interface, verification, or implementation decisions.
- Keep function identity separate from implementation component identity. Multiple implementations may realize one function; one component may realize multiple functions; neither relationship creates authority automatically.
- Define functional state transitions with actor, authority, trigger, precondition, guard, evidence, effect, error, timeout, retry, compensation, cancellation, and resulting state where applicable.
- Treat every material functional interface as a contract with producer, consumer, purpose, data or resource, identity, authority, timing, ordering, quality, error, compatibility, observability, evidence, and ownership.
- Allocate nonfunctional and cross-cutting obligations to functions, interfaces, actors, elements, environments, and lifecycle activities rather than leaving them as unowned global prose.
- Preserve human judgment, manual fallback, accessibility support, control, appeal, explanation, and recovery as explicit functions when they are required outcomes.
- Model AI and external providers as bounded functional realizations with version, data, context, tools, permissions, evaluation, monitoring, fallback, substitution, revocation, incident, and retirement behavior.
- Instrument functional execution and outcomes sufficiently to distinguish invocation, completion, correct result, accepted outcome, control effectiveness, and recovery.
- Ensure changes to requirements, domain rules, processes, data, interfaces, authorities, models, providers, environments, or implementations trigger functional impact analysis and evidence invalidation.
- Preserve compatibility, migration, deprecation, support, maintenance, and retirement semantics for functional contracts and historical evidence.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

- Show the functional baseline identity, version, scope, environment, operating mode, owner, authority state, lifecycle state, and evidence freshness.
- Let the owner navigate from objective, requirement, capability, process, or domain rule to function, allocation, interface, implementation, test, evidence, acceptance, operation, and retirement.
- Identify available, unavailable, prohibited, consequential, awaiting-approval, degraded, failed, unverified, and recovery actions.
- Expose the acting identity, role, capability, authority, tools, data boundary, approval point, and consequence boundary for each consequential function.
- Show functional inputs, outputs, states, dependencies, controls, timing, quality, errors, retry, idempotency, observability, evidence, and owner.
- Provide controls to approve, reject, defer, narrow, allocate, revoke, pause, stop, roll back, restore, quarantine, supersede, and retire within authority.
- Explain what is defined versus implemented, what has been tested versus verified, what is simulated versus live, and what remains unknown.
- Preserve critical status, stop, correction, evidence, fallback, and recovery functions when nonessential dependencies fail.
- Export the functional baseline, contracts, allocations, traceability, evidence, decisions, dissent, and continuity record without hidden conversational state.

The cockpit must not equate a function catalog, component existence, benchmark, demo, or green dashboard with functional correctness or accepted outcome.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

- Every functional claim must identify subject, function identity, version, scope, environment, operating mode, conditions, method, evidence source, timeframe, actor, and limitations.
- A requirement proves an obligation exists; a functional model proves how the obligation is represented; neither alone proves implementation or runtime behavior.
- Every function must identify its source requirement, capability, process, domain rule, risk treatment, or lifecycle obligation, or be explicitly marked proposed or unresolved.
- Every allocation must identify allocated function, receiving actor or element, responsibility, authority, rationale, interface effects, verification level, and status.
- Every functional contract must identify endpoints, exact versions, inputs, outputs, units, identity, authority, timing, ordering, quality, errors, retries, compatibility, observability, evidence, and ownership as applicable.
- Analysis, simulation, inspection, demonstration, prototype, test, measurement, runtime record, user observation, and owner acceptance must remain distinct evidence types.
- Performance and quality evidence must identify workload, data, configuration, environment, thresholds, uncertainty, method, result, and excluded claims.
- AI evidence must identify model or provider, version, configuration, context, data, tools, permissions, evaluation, human review, fallback, and limitations proportionate to consequence.
- Verification evidence must identify exact artifact or baseline, expected result, actual result, procedure, inputs, environment, actor, date, logs, deviations, reproducibility, and independence.
- Failed, blocked, inconclusive, stale, superseded, contradictory, or partial evidence must remain visible and must constrain the related functional state.
- Missing evidence must result in `UNKNOWN`, `NOT VERIFIED`, `BLOCKED`, `PROPOSED`, `CONDITIONAL`, or another bounded state; it must not be treated as functional success.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

- Trace representative objectives, requirements, capabilities, processes, and domain rules into functions, allocations, interfaces, implementations, tests, evidence, acceptance, operations, maintenance, and retirement.
- Trace representative implementations, components, interfaces, and tests backward to authorized functions and their source obligations.
- Introduce an orphan function, duplicate function, unallocated function, unreachable function, unowned state, incomplete contract, and orphan test and confirm each is detected or explicitly recorded.
- Change a requirement, process, domain rule, interface, data schema, authority rule, model, provider, environment, or implementation and confirm impacted functions, allocations, evidence, tests, approvals, and readiness are flagged.
- Exercise normal, boundary, invalid, unauthorized, malformed, duplicate, replayed, out-of-order, timed-out, partial, degraded, offline, failure, recovery, rollback, migration, maintenance, deprecation, and retirement behavior.
- Verify functional contracts reject or safely handle missing, stale, malformed, incompatible, unauthorized, duplicate, replayed, or partially processed inputs as applicable.
- Disable an AI model, external provider, network, data source, credential, or evidence store and confirm critical control, stop, status, fallback, and recovery functions remain available or fail safely.
- Attempt to let an AI-generated function, allocation, optimization, risk acceptance, or readiness claim become binding without authority and verify rejection.
- Confirm mandatory security, privacy, safety, accessibility, governance, reliability, resilience, and human-control obligations cannot be traded away through aggregate scoring or implementation convenience.
- Validate complete owner, user, operator, maintainer, support, incident, and recovery journeys in realistic environments with appropriate accessibility coverage.
- Independently reproduce representative functional claims from the exact handoff package and state what remains unverified.
- Restore a previous functional baseline and confirm identity, allocations, interfaces, evidence, compatibility, and owner controls remain coherent.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

- Treating a function list, component diagram, API list, prompt, feature list, benchmark, prototype, or simulation as proof of functional behavior.
- Deriving functions from the current implementation and then calling them requirements or architecture without authorized analysis.
- Decomposing too little to expose ownership, interfaces, controls, states, failure, evidence, or verification.
- Decomposing too far into an ungoverned task inventory that obscures outcomes, responsibility, or change impact.
- Duplicating functions under different names or assigning one function to conflicting owners without an explicit allocation rule.
- Leaving human judgment, manual fallback, accessibility, explanation, appeal, recovery, evidence, or lifecycle work implicit.
- Allocating a function to a team, document, or component without defining authority, inputs, outputs, contract, resources, evidence, and verification.
- Treating a model's technical ability, provider claim, training result, or agent confidence as permission or operational competence.
- Local optimization that improves latency, cost, or throughput while weakening correctness, safety, privacy, security, accessibility, resilience, maintainability, support, or retirement.
- Hiding denied, malformed, adversarial, concurrent, stale, partial, degraded, or recovery behavior.
- Treating a test of one implementation, environment, input, or path as proof of the general functional claim.
- Allowing changes in requirements, process, domain, data, authority, provider, or environment to leave functional evidence and approvals falsely green.
- Making recovery depend on the failed function, component, credential, network, provider, model, data, or evidence store.
- Retiring a function or implementation without preserving required capability, user transition, compatibility, records, evidence, support, and accountability.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

1. Quarantine the affected function, allocation, contract, baseline, evidence, approval, or readiness claim while preserving the last trusted state.
2. Classify the issue as objective, requirement, domain meaning, process, decomposition, actor, authority, input, output, state, contract, allocation, control, quality, evidence, implementation, interface, failure, recovery, or lifecycle defect.
3. Downgrade the affected functional state to `UNKNOWN`, `PROPOSED`, `BLOCKED`, `NOT VERIFIED`, `FAILED`, or another truthful state.
4. Preserve the prior functional model, implementation, evidence, dissent, failed attempt, incident, and supersession links.
5. Identify safe unaffected work and enter safe halt for actions that could create consequence from the invalid function.
6. Request the smallest missing requirement clarification, domain decision, owner approval, specialist review, observation, contract correction, implementation change, test, or recovery exercise needed.
7. Re-run impact analysis across processes, capabilities, requirements, architecture, allocations, interfaces, data, controls, tests, evidence, training, operations, support, maintenance, migration, deprecation, and retirement.
8. Narrow scope, split the function, create a variant, use a reversible realization, restore a prior baseline, or defer advancement as authorized.
9. Record the new state, conditions, residual uncertainty, owner decision, review date, and evidence that would change the conclusion.

A blocked functional record must name the exact blocker, consequence, safe work, minimum resolution, authority required, and evidence needed for reconsideration.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Functional Architecture is complete for its declared scope only when:

- Objective, outcomes, requirements baseline, scope, system boundary, environments, operating modes, affected parties, authority, non-goals, and lifecycle stages are explicit.
- Function meaning is distinguished from capability, process, activity, task, procedure, policy, component, service, interface, implementation, and observed behavior.
- Every material function has stable identity, purpose, inputs, outputs, conditions, owner, criticality, dependencies, allocation, evidence, verification method, and lifecycle state.
- Functional context, hierarchy, flows, behavior, states, contracts, allocations, controls, and dependencies are coherent or disagreements are visible.
- Functions trace to authorized requirements, capabilities, processes, domain rules, risk treatments, and lifecycle obligations, or remain visibly proposed or unresolved.
- Material cross-cutting obligations for security, privacy, safety, accessibility, reliability, resilience, performance, maintainability, observability, support, migration, deprecation, and retirement are allocated.
- Normal, boundary, denied, malformed, concurrent, degraded, failure, recovery, rollback, migration, maintenance, deprecation, and retirement behavior is addressed where applicable.
- Human-AI roles, authority, autonomy, human checkpoints, fallback, revocation, appeal, monitoring, evidence, and incident behavior are bounded and testable.
- Functional contracts and allocations are versioned, compatible, traceable, and supported by appropriate evidence and independent verification.
- Applicable owners, users, operators, maintainers, support personnel, and qualified specialists have reviewed the exact scope or their absence is recorded as a limitation or blocker.
- Hostile review, verification, acceptance, change, invalidation, recovery, and reopening methods are defined; unresolved mandatory conditions block affected advancement.
- Downstream roles can reconstruct the functional baseline, assumptions, evidence, decisions, open findings, and next safe action without chat history.

Completion does not mean functions are implemented, integrated, verified, validated, accepted, operational, effective, or retired. It means the declared functional architecture is sufficiently coherent and governed to control the next authorized realization step truthfully.
