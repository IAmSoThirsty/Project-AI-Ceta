SECTION 145 - Streaming Engineering

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define governed engineering of time-based delivery of audio, video, data, or interactive content across variable networks and clients with explicit latency, quality, access, continuity, and recovery behavior.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

A streaming baseline with source, segments, manifests, timing, authorization, adaptation, buffering, observability, accessibility, evidence, and retirement.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Streaming fails through latency, stalls, drift, stale manifests, unauthorized access, or quality adaptation. Availability metrics alone do not describe user or operational outcome.

APPLICABILITY

Applies to live, on-demand, interactive, event, media, telemetry, and data streaming systems.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

May be non-applicable where content is delivered only as bounded nonstreaming transfers with no timing or continuity claim. Record scope, owner, evidence, and reopening trigger.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Requires source, timing, codec, manifests, network, authorization, latency, quality, buffering, captions, recording, privacy, observability, and recovery objectives.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Provide stream type, segments, clocks, bitrate, clients, latency, access, DRM or protection, captions, failover, storage, and user-outcome requirements.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Humans approve access, content, latency and quality tradeoffs, recording, privacy, continuity, and release. Humans decide incident communication and stream termination.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may detect stalls, optimize adaptation, caption, summarize, and forecast capacity. It must not expose streams, alter access, or trade safety or meaning for engagement without approval.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Architect, Media Engineer, Network Engineer, Security Auditor, Privacy Reviewer, Accessibility specialist, Implementer, Tester, Verifier, Governance Reviewer, Observer, Documentation Writer, Memory Curator, and Red Team. Existing role contracts remain controlling.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Define source, timing, clients, protocol, access, quality, continuity, and lifecycle.
2. Design ingest, packaging, manifests, delivery, adaptation, captions, failover, and recording.
3. Test latency, stalls, loss, drift, authorization, accessibility, scaling, and recovery.
4. Monitor, communicate, correct, and retire streams and contracts.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show stream identity, source time, playback time, access, quality, buffer, captions, evidence, and consequence. Human approval is required for recording, access, and termination; nonstreaming status remains available.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Model source, clock, encode, segment, manifest, cache, edge, authorization, client, caption, and archive boundaries. Support adaptation, backpressure, failover, replay, and deletion.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show stream, audience, latency, stalls, drift, quality, access, incidents, evidence, and controls to throttle, pause, fail over, restrict, record, or terminate.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Streaming charter, source and rights records, protocol and manifest design, latency and quality budgets, access and privacy assessment, accessibility plan, load evidence, runbook, and retirement package.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify stream, source, timestamp, segment, client, network, build, method, result, and limits. Separate delivery, quality, accessibility, security, privacy, and user outcome evidence.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Verify authorization, manifests, timing, stalls, loss, reconnect, adaptation, captions, failover, load, recording, deletion, and client compatibility.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Unbounded buffering; stale manifests; unauthorized caches; hiding latency; ignoring captions; recording without consent; and treating stream availability as comprehension.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Pause or restrict stream, preserve source and timeline, fail over, provide alternate delivery, revoke access, compensate commitments, and reopen timing, rights, or capacity decisions.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Source, timing, access, quality, evidence, accessibility, recovery, ownership, and lifecycle are explicit. Keep CONTENT-COMPLETE until independent verification and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve source, manifest, segment, access, traces, incidents, decisions, evidence, and next safe action. Hand off to Media, Video, Network, CDN, Search, Security, Accessibility, Testing, and Operations.
