SECTION 214 — Incident Response

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Prepare for, detect, analyze, contain, eradicate, recover from, communicate, and learn from incidents affecting security, safety, privacy, service, people, property, or trust.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

An incident-response model with classification, authority, roles, intake, triage, evidence, containment, communication, recovery, legal and privacy coordination, closure, and lessons-learned paths.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Incident response is a time-constrained authority problem as well as a technical process. Delayed escalation, unclear ownership, unsafe containment, lost evidence, or premature closure can increase harm and prevent learning.

APPLICABILITY

Apply when an actual or suspected event may violate security, privacy, safety, availability, policy, law, contract, user trust, or organizational control.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

Record non-applicability only for events with no material consequence, no protected asset or person, and a documented routine handling path that preserves evidence and escalation triggers.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Maintain contacts, severity model, authority, playbooks, telemetry, evidence storage, communication paths, legal and privacy guidance, continuity, backup, recovery, and support coverage.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Alert or report, affected assets and people, time, source, scope, severity criteria, evidence, dependencies, responders, authorities, communications, recovery objectives, and known constraints.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

The incident commander owns coordination and can stop unsafe work. Accountable owners approve containment, service interruption, disclosure, user communication, legal escalation, recovery, and closure. Humans decide material severity, attribution, harm, and residual risk.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may correlate authorized signals, summarize timelines, draft hypotheses, identify affected scope, suggest playbook steps, and track actions. It must label evidence, inference, uncertainty, and recommendation; protect sensitive data; and preserve the incident record. It must not declare an incident, contain, delete evidence, notify external parties, attribute, or close without explicit authority and human control.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Incident Responder, Incident Commander, Security Operations Analyst, Forensic Analyst, Researcher, Analyst, Coordinator, Architect, Security Auditor, Governance Reviewer, Legal Advisor, Privacy Reviewer, Tester, Verifier, Documentation Writer, and Memory Curator.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

Prepare contacts and exercises; intake and record; validate and classify; preserve evidence; scope impact; contain safely; eradicate or remediate; communicate; recover and monitor; close with owner decision; perform postmortem; track actions and reopening.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show case, severity, asset, people, evidence, uncertainty, authority, action, impact, and next decision. Require human approval for containment, data access, user or external communication, legal notification, recovery, and closure. Keep manual response and emergency stop paths available.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Separate intake, case record, evidence, analysis, action authority, communication, recovery, and postmortem. Design for degraded telemetry, provider loss, conflicting reports, clock differences, immutable timelines, privacy minimization, and safe rollback.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show incident state, commander, severity, scope, affected people and assets, evidence freshness, actions, dependencies, communications, decisions, and residual risk. Provide authorized acknowledge, escalate, contain, release, preserve, notify, recover, reopen, and close controls.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Incident charter and severity record; case timeline; evidence and custody record; impact and communication plan; containment and recovery actions; legal and privacy assessment; remediation; postmortem; decisions, exceptions, and continuity record.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Identify source, asset, person, event, time basis, method, actor, action, result, confidence, impact, and limitation. Preserve original references and distinguish confirmed, suspected, disproved, and unknown conditions.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Exercise intake, classification, paging, command, evidence, containment, communication, legal and privacy escalation, recovery, backup, rollback, user support, and closure. Verify actions are attributable and can be reconstructed from the record.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Unowned incidents, severity by volume alone, silent scope changes, destructive containment, lost evidence, premature closure, uncoordinated communication, and postmortems that blame people instead of improving controls.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Preserve state and evidence, stabilize affected services, protect people, communicate truthfully, and use approved containment, restore, failover, or manual operation. Reassess harm and residual risk after new evidence. Reopen when impact, scope, root cause, or recovery claim changes.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Authority, severity, scope, evidence, actions, communications, recovery, owners, residual risk, lessons, and reopening conditions are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending independent review and owner acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve case identity, timeline, evidence references, decisions, communications, actions, postmortem, remediation, and open gaps. Hand off to Operations, Security, Privacy, Legal, Records, Support, Governance, Testing, and affected owners.
