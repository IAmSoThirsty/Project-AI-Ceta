SECTION 176 — Ontology Engineering

STATUS: CONTENT-COMPLETE

PURPOSE

Define governed design and maintenance of formal concepts, classes, properties, constraints, axioms, mappings, and terminology that establish shared meaning across domains and systems.

REQUIRED OUTCOME

- An ontology scope, competency questions, concepts, relationships, constraints, mappings, owners, and version policy.
- Evidence that definitions fit domain usage, stakeholder needs, data, information, and system behavior.
- Controlled change, translation, conflict, deprecation, and adoption paths.

WHY THIS MATTERS

An ontology can encode one group's assumptions as universal truth. Misclassified concepts and silent version drift propagate through data, search, analytics, models, and decisions.

APPLICABILITY

Applies when shared formal meaning, reasoning, mapping, semantic validation, cross-domain exchange, or ontology-based AI is required. Depth increases with legal, safety, identity, public, or automated decision impact.

NON-APPLICABILITY CONDITIONS

May be non-applicable where a bounded local vocabulary has no cross-system, inferential, decision, or lifecycle effect. Record scope, authority, and reopening trigger; a glossary is not automatically an ontology.

PREREQUISITES AND DEPENDENCIES

Requires domain authorities, information and data models, competency questions, stakeholders, translation needs, governance, and versioning. Depends on Sections 007, 016, 030, 031, 055, 175, and 177.

REQUIRED INPUTS

Purpose; domain; users; competency questions; terms; sources; definitions; relations; constraints; mappings; jurisdictions; languages; inference needs; access; lifecycle; and acceptance authority.

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITIES AND AUTHORITY

Domain owners approve meaning, scope, axioms, contested terms, mappings, deprecations, and exceptions. Ontologists facilitate and document; they do not impose a definition over legitimate domain authority or lived experience.

AI RESPONSIBILITIES AND BOUNDARIES

AI may extract terms, propose hierarchies and mappings, and test consistency. It must preserve source and dissent and must not declare a canonical concept, resolve contested meaning, or infer policy from similarity.

APPLICABLE EXISTING AGENT ROLES

Architect, Researcher, Domain Analyst, Ontology Engineer, Information Architect, Data Governance Reviewer, Critic, Verifier, Tester, Documentation Writer, Memory Curator, Governance Reviewer, and Accessibility Reviewer.

REQUIRED ACTIVITIES

1. Define scope, competency questions, users, jurisdictions, languages, and non-goals.
2. Inventory source vocabularies, conflicts, terms, and authoritative owners.
3. Model concepts, properties, constraints, axioms, mappings, and provenance.
4. Test competency questions, consistency, completeness, ambiguity, and unintended inference.
5. Review accessibility, cultural, legal, privacy, and stakeholder effects.
6. Version, publish, deprecate, migrate, and communicate changes.
7. Record dissent, unresolved meaning, and owner acceptance.

HUMAN-AI INTERACTION REQUIREMENTS

Show source, definition, rationale, scope, confidence, mapping, inference, reviewer, and version. Humans must edit and contest concepts without erasing history. Generated mappings remain proposals and cannot grant access or authority.

ARCHITECTURE AND SYSTEM IMPLICATIONS

Version concepts, relations, axioms, constraints, mappings, translations, namespaces, reasoners, and applications. Separate semantic definition from implementation schema. Preserve provenance, compatibility, migration, deprecation, and inference limits.

OWNER COCKPIT AND USER-CONTROL REQUIREMENTS

Show ontology version, owner, scope, term, definition, source, mappings, usages, inferences, conflicts, and lifecycle. Allow authorized approve, edit, contest, deprecate, map, restrict, supersede, and retire actions.

REQUIRED OUTPUTS AND ARTIFACTS

Ontology charter; competency-question register; concept and relation model; axioms and constraints; source and mapping register; glossary; version and deprecation policy; validation report; decisions, dissent, and handoff.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Each concept and axiom identifies authority, source, scope, rationale, version, reviewer, applicability, and limitation. A lexical similarity, reasoner result, or mapping does not prove domain truth or acceptance.

VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE TESTS

Run competency questions; test consistency, ambiguity, unintended entailment, mapping collisions, version compatibility, access implications, multilingual terms, and deprecation migration. Independently verify representative definitions.

FAILURE STATES AND ANTI-PATTERNS

Universal vocabulary without authority; ontology by majority vote; hidden axioms; semantic overreach; mapping by string match; contested terms erased; version drift; and inferred policy presented as definition.

RECOVERY AND RECONSIDERATION

Quarantine affected inferences on semantic conflict, invalid axiom, mapping error, or authority dispute. Preserve prior version and evidence, restrict downstream use, consult domain owners, migrate or supersede deliberately, and reopen.

COMPLETION CRITERIA

Scope, terms, relations, axioms, sources, owners, mappings, tests, version, conflicts, lifecycle, and authority are explicit. Status remains CONTENT-COMPLETE pending reconciliation and acceptance.

HANDOFF AND CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Preserve ontology IDs, namespaces, versions, definitions, mappings, tests, decisions, dissent, usages, and gaps. Hand off to Information, Data, Knowledge Graph, Semantic Systems, Search, AI, and Governance.
