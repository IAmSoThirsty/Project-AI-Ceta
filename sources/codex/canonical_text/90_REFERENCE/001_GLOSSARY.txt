
GLOSSARY — WORKING DRAFT

Action contract
A durable record translating an intended consequential action into explicit objective, actor, authority, environment, scope, preconditions, risk, approval, state, and evidence requirements.

Agent
An artificial-intelligence process acting through a stable or attributable identity, runtime, role contract, capability set, assignment, and authority boundary.

Agent governance
The rules, authority boundaries, evidence requirements, verification gates, continuity practices, and enforcement mechanisms controlling how an agent may act and how its work is judged.

Agent role
A bounded professional function with defined inputs, responsibilities, prohibited behavior, outputs, and completion conditions.

Assumption
A proposition temporarily used for reasoning or planning without sufficient evidence to call it established.

Authority
The legitimate permission to make a decision or perform an action within a defined scope and consequence boundary. Authority may be bounded, delegated, expired, reduced, revoked, or exercised; it may not be invented.

Blocked
A state in which a prerequisite prevents the affected work from advancing safely or truthfully. Blocked is not the same as failed.

Capability
What an actor or system can technically do. Capability does not itself confer authority.

Codex section
One numbered lifecycle field in Thirsty's Codex, expressed as a governed work contract with applicability, responsibilities, artifacts, evidence, failure, recovery, and completion requirements.

Continuity map
A durable operational handoff record describing current state, completed work, evidence, failures, blockers, risks, unresolved conditions, and next safe action.

Constitutional work package
A pre-execution package containing objective, scope, source state, claims, assumptions, unknowns, obligations, roles, experiments, evidence contracts, acceptance criteria, rollback, and reversal conditions.

Cockpit
The governed human-system operating layer through which an accountable human understands state, exercises authority, directs work, inspects evidence, recovers, and stops or exits the system.

Delegation
A recorded grant of bounded authority from an authorized actor to another actor. Delegation narrows or transfers exercisable authority under conditions; it does not create new authority.

Dissent ledger
A durable record preserving material disagreement, its source, impact, disposition, decision owner, and reopening condition.

Evidence
An artifact, observation, command output, test result, log, configuration, trace, measurement, record, source, signature, hash, or other admissible basis supporting a bounded claim.

Critical uncertainty
A condition that is both materially consequential to a decision and genuinely unresolved, making it suitable for explicit scenario or sensitivity analysis.

Driver
A force, condition, dependency, or structural pressure capable of influencing future states over the relevant horizon.

Foresight
The governed examination of plausible change, uncertainty, alternative futures, consequences, indicators, and reversal conditions to improve present decisions without pretending to predict one inevitable future.

Future stakeholder
A person, community, owner, operator, institution, intelligent system, or other affected party that may inherit material consequences of a present decision.

Horizon
A defined future time boundary, lifecycle stage, or decision event used to bound analysis.

Indicator
An observable condition monitored for evidence that a scenario assumption, risk, opportunity, or decision basis is strengthening, weakening, or changing.

Path dependency
A condition in which earlier choices constrain later options, often increasing the cost or difficulty of change.

Point of no return
A decision or state boundary after which reversal becomes impossible, prohibited, or materially more costly or harmful.

Robust option
An option that remains useful or acceptable across multiple materially different plausible futures.

Scenario
A structured, internally traceable plausible future used to test decisions and consequences. A scenario is not a prediction.

Signal
A dated observation that may indicate emerging change but does not by itself establish a trend or future outcome.


Fail closed
A control posture in which missing authority, evidence, identity, integrity, or another required condition prevents the affected action rather than silently permitting it.

Human authority
The retained authority of the authorized human to define objectives, approve material decisions, accept risk, correct intent, revoke delegation, and make final determinations where human judgment is required.

Human-AI interaction
The governed exchange through which humans and AI systems communicate intent, questions, explanations, recommendations, plans, actions, evidence, corrections, uncertainty, and decisions.

Independent verification
Assessment of a claim by a role or mechanism not relying solely on the claimant's interpretation or the implementation under review.

Objective substitution
The unauthorized replacement, broadening, narrowing, sanitization, or displacement of the human's actual objective.

Operating contract
The standing behavioral and governance requirements that apply before a role-specific assignment begins.

Proof-carrying work product
A post-execution artifact set binding the delivered result to identity, changes, execution records, evidence, tests, hostile review, dissent, unresolved conditions, transition recommendation, rollback, and continuity.

Recovery
The governed process of returning from failure, degradation, error, or invalid state to a known safe state or an explicit human decision point.

Role contract
The source-defined professional boundary governing an assigned agent role.

Scope
The declared boundary of included work, exclusions, preservation requirements, acceptance criteria, stop conditions, and consequence limits.

Source identity
The information required to know what a source is, where it came from, which version or revision is being used, how current it is, and whether its integrity and authority are adequate for a claim.

Task and authority envelope
The assignment record specifying mode, objective, role, allowed tools, permissions, data boundaries, dependencies, resource limits, prohibited actions, acceptance criteria, evidence, stop conditions, and escalation.

Trusted transition
A controlled change in trusted state, such as promotion, quarantine, hold, rejection, or revocation, that cannot outrun authority and evidence.

Unknown
A material fact or state that has not been established. Unknown is information and must not be silently converted to false, zero, safe, or irrelevant.

Verification
The process of bounding a claim, checking identity and environment, examining evidence integrity, reproducing or falsifying where practical, and stating remaining uncertainty.

Vibe coder
A person who uses natural-language and AI-assisted creation to express and build an idea, including a person who may not know every professional discipline required to make the result real.

EXPANDED TERMS — SECTIONS 003–018

Anti-metric
An indicator used to detect when apparent success in a target metric is accompanied by displacement, gaming, exclusion, harm, degraded quality, or loss of the intended outcome.

Business rule
A source-bound statement defining a condition, decision, obligation, permission, prohibition, calculation, classification, or action within an organizational or domain context, including its owner, exceptions, jurisdiction, and effective period.

Business strategy
A coherent set of human-authorized choices about where to participate, whom to serve, how value will be created and sustained, which capabilities and dependencies are required, and what will not be pursued.

Bounded context
An explicit semantic and responsibility boundary inside which a domain model, terminology, rules, ownership, and source authority are internally coherent. Meaning crossing the boundary requires a defined contract or translation.

Capability model
A representation of what outcomes an organization, product, system, actor, or service must be able to produce, separated from the features, technologies, implementations, or providers used to realize those capabilities.

Commitment
An attributable promise or obligation with authorized speaker, exact beneficiary or recipient, scope, conditions, evidence, due state, change authority, and closure or breach handling.

Competitive intelligence
Lawful, ethical, source-grounded analysis of competitors, substitutes, ecosystem actors, alternatives, and relevant signals for a defined decision, with claimed and verified capability kept distinct.

Domain
The real-world or institutional field of meaning, behavior, rules, decisions, actors, information, authority, and lifecycle in which a system or change operates.

Domain event
A source-meaningful occurrence in the domain that has happened and may affect state, obligations, decisions, evidence, or downstream behavior. It is not merely a transport message.

Domain invariant
A condition that must remain true within a domain or bounded context. If a proposed state or action violates it, the transition is invalid regardless of aggregate benefit or confidence.

Entity
A domain concept whose identity and continuity matter across changes in attributes or state.

Innovation hypothesis
A bounded proposition that a proposed change can improve an evidenced outcome under declared conditions, with assumptions, failure hypotheses, experiment, evidence threshold, stop conditions, and reversal path.

Market research
Methodologically bounded inquiry into an external population, market, category, behavior, need, channel, price or funding condition, adoption barrier, or market structure for a defined decision.

Mental model
A person's internal understanding of how a system, task, concept, or process works, including expectations about available actions, consequence, evidence, failure, and recovery.

Mission invariant
A protected value, condition, prohibition, or decision boundary that downstream strategy and implementation may not silently trade away.

Mission strategy
The durable, human-ratified purpose, beneficiaries, protected values, non-goals, decision principles, outcome evidence, drift controls, and amendment path governing downstream choices.

Participant
A person who knowingly takes part in a research activity under the applicable consent, privacy, safety, accessibility, data-use, withdrawal, and grievance conditions.

Portfolio
The governed collection of initiatives, products, programs, projects, experiments, capabilities, maintenance obligations, and retirements competing for shared resources and carrying shared dependencies or risks.

Product management
The continuous decision function governing product discovery, intake, prioritization, roadmap, requirements, release, learning, support, change, deprecation, and retirement.

Product strategy
The evidence-grounded definition of what product should exist, for whom, to produce which outcomes, under which principles, capabilities, non-goals, lifecycle boundaries, and decision conditions.

Program
A coordinated group of related projects, products, workstreams, and changes managed together because the authorized outcome, integration, transition, or benefits cannot be achieved by one project alone.

Project
A temporary, bounded, authorized endeavor that creates or changes defined deliverables and outcomes under explicit scope, resources, dependencies, risk, evidence, acceptance, and closure conditions.

Source of truth
The authoritative system, record, artifact, institution, or owner from which a particular fact, state, definition, rule, or decision must be read. A source of truth is scoped; no source is automatically authoritative for every claim.

Stakeholder
A person, group, institution, owner, operator, user, affected non-user, partner, regulator, community, or other legitimate party whose authority, rights, interests, obligations, knowledge, benefit, or exposure materially affects the work.

State transition
A governed change from one defined domain or system state to another, including trigger, actor, authority, preconditions, guards, evidence, effects, failure handling, and resulting state.

User job
The outcome a user, owner, operator, or other actor is trying to achieve in a particular context, including the evidence needed before acting and the consequence of success, failure, or delay.

User journey
The end-to-end path from trigger and entry through information, decisions, actions, evidence, consequence, failure, recovery, exit, and continuity for a user or owner job.

User research
Governed inquiry using actual participants, observations, artifacts, and contextual evidence to understand human goals, behavior, language, mental models, accessibility, trust, failure, and recovery for a defined decision.

Value object
A domain concept defined primarily by its attributes and meaning rather than a persistent independent identity. Equality and replacement rules must be domain-defined.

EXPANDED TERMS — SECTIONS 017–018

Control effectiveness
The evidence-supported degree to which an implemented control achieves its objective in the declared environment, version, scope, and period. Documentation or configuration presence alone does not establish effectiveness.

Disqualifying condition
A mandatory constraint violation or other condition that makes an option ineligible within the declared scope, regardless of its aggregate score or benefits.

Feasibility
The evidence-bounded ability to realize, operate, govern, support, recover, maintain, and retire an option under declared authority, constraints, resources, dependencies, environment, scale, and lifecycle conditions.

Feasibility condition
A requirement, dependency, approval, resource, control, evidence threshold, environment, or operating limitation that must remain true for a feasibility determination to hold.

Inherent risk
Risk assessed before considering the effect of current controls.

Risk
Uncertainty that may affect an objective, value, right, person, system, commitment, or environment. A useful risk statement identifies a source or cause, an uncertain event, and a consequence within a bounded context.

Risk acceptance
An attributable, authorized, evidence-informed decision to tolerate a specifically bounded residual risk for a defined scope, period, and set of conditions. Continued work or silence is not acceptance.

Risk appetite
The human-authorized type and amount of risk an accountable entity is prepared to pursue or retain in support of objectives, subject to mandatory legal, safety, rights, privacy, security, and other constraints.

Risk tolerance
A defined permissible variation or threshold around an objective or risk appetite, including the point at which escalation, treatment, degradation, or halt is required.

Residual risk
Risk remaining after considering the verified effect of current or proposed controls. Current residual risk and projected residual risk after planned treatment must be kept distinct.

Safe halt
A governed state that prevents new prohibited or consequential actions while preserving authorized diagnosis, evidence, communication, and recovery pathways.

Treatment-created risk
New or increased risk introduced by a proposed risk treatment, such as added complexity, surveillance, lock-in, operator burden, control conflict, or recovery fragility.

EXPANDED TERMS — SECTION 019

Acceptance criterion
An observable condition, threshold, state, journey outcome, or evidence requirement used to determine whether a requirement has been satisfied within its declared scope and environment.

Derived requirement
A requirement produced through documented reasoning from a higher-level objective, need, constraint, risk treatment, architecture obligation, or governing source. Its derivation and authority must remain traceable.

Functional requirement
A bounded statement of behavior, service, response, transformation, or interaction the system or designated actor must perform under declared conditions.

Nonfunctional requirement
A requirement concerning a quality, constraint, operating condition, or cross-cutting property such as security, privacy, safety, accessibility, reliability, performance, maintainability, recoverability, or governance. The label does not make it optional.

Requirement
A versioned, attributable, bounded, and verifiable obligation or constraint that states what outcome, behavior, quality, control, evidence, or lifecycle condition must be satisfied within declared applicability.

Requirement baseline
An attributable, approved, immutable or change-controlled version of the in-scope requirement set, including scope, variants, open findings, source versions, approvals, and effective date.

Requirement criticality
The consequence-based importance of satisfying a requirement, distinct from implementation priority or scheduling order.

Requirement source
The authoritative artifact, law, policy, contract, decision, stakeholder statement, user evidence, domain rule, feasibility condition, risk treatment, or other traceable origin supporting a requirement.

Requirements traceability
The durable, bidirectional relationships connecting sources, needs, requirements, architecture, implementation, tests, evidence, acceptance, operation, change, and retirement.

Requirements validation
Evaluation of whether the requirement set expresses the authorized objective and legitimate stakeholder, user, domain, lifecycle, and governing needs for the declared context.

Requirements verification
Evaluation of whether requirement statements and their management records meet defined quality, consistency, traceability, structure, and evidence rules before implementation satisfaction is assessed.

EXPANDED TERMS — SECTION 020

Concept of operations
A controlled description of how a system is intended to be used, supervised, supported, degraded, recovered, changed, and retired by identified actors in declared environments and lifecycle conditions.

Configuration item
A system element, artifact, model, interface, data set, policy, environment, procedure, or other controlled object whose identity, version, status, relationships, and change history must be managed.

Enabling system
A system, service, facility, process, organization, tool, supplier, or capability that supports development, integration, verification, validation, transition, operation, maintenance, or retirement of the system of interest without necessarily being part of the delivered system.

Interface control
The governed definition and change management of a boundary between producers and consumers, including exchanged data or resources, protocol, timing, units, identity, authority, errors, compatibility, security, evidence, and ownership.

Measure of effectiveness
A measure of whether the system produces the authorized mission, owner, user, or operational outcome in the intended context.

Measure of performance
A measure of how well a system or system element performs a defined function under declared conditions.

System baseline
An attributable, versioned, approved, and change-controlled representation of the system at a defined lifecycle point, including applicable requirements, elements, interfaces, configuration, evidence, open findings, and authority.

System element
A bounded part of a system with defined purpose, responsibilities, inputs, outputs, dependencies, authority, interfaces, failure behavior, recovery behavior, ownership, and verification method.

System of interest
The system selected as the primary subject of analysis, design, implementation, integration, verification, validation, operation, maintenance, or retirement, with an explicit boundary separating it from external and enabling systems.

Systems Engineering
The interdisciplinary lifecycle function that converts an approved requirements baseline into a coherent whole-system definition, allocation, interface model, technical baseline, integration path, verification and validation strategy, operational transition, support model, and retirement path.

Technical performance measure
A tracked technical characteristic, margin, or threshold used to assess whether the evolving system is likely to satisfy a critical requirement or system objective.

Validation
Evidence-based determination that the integrated system solves the right problem and supports the intended outcomes and complete journeys in the declared operational context.

Verification
Evidence-based determination that a specified requirement, interface, element, or system claim has been satisfied within its declared scope, configuration, environment, and method.

EXPANDED TERMS — SECTIONS 021–022

Architecture decision record
A durable record of a material architecture decision containing context, decision owner, authority state, options, criteria, evidence, assumptions, tradeoffs, selected or recommended outcome, dissent, conditions, consequences, review trigger, and supersession history.

Architecture principle
A human-authorized rule or strong preference guiding architecture decisions across a declared scope. A principle must identify its authority, rationale, applicability, exceptions, and review condition.

Architecture roadmap
A dependency-aware sequence of current, transition, and target states with decisions, increments, evidence gates, migration, rollback, support, and retirement obligations.

Capability
An outcome-producing ability that an enterprise, system, organization, product, service, human, or agent must be able to sustain. A capability is distinct from the technology, team, feature, or provider used to realize it.

Current-state architecture
A source-bound representation of the architecture that actually exists for a declared version, environment, and evidence date. It must not include planned or inferred elements as though they were implemented.

Enterprise Architecture
The governed alignment of mission, strategy, operating model, capabilities, value streams, organizations, information, applications, technology, controls, investment, transition, and lifecycle across an enterprise boundary.

Exception
An authorized, bounded, time- or condition-limited departure from a principle, standard, requirement, or baseline, with rationale, risk, owner, evidence, expiry, monitoring, and closure or renewal path.

Reference architecture
A reusable architecture pattern defining expected layers, responsibilities, interfaces, constraints, and prohibited shortcuts for a class of solutions without claiming that a specific implementation exists.

Solution Architecture
The governed design of a bounded solution that maps requirements and context to components, interfaces, data, state, authority, security, deployment, operations, evidence, recovery, maintenance, and retirement.

Target-state architecture
An approved or advisory representation of a desired future architecture, explicitly distinguished from current and transition states and bound to assumptions, evidence, authority, and acceptance conditions.

Transition architecture
An intermediate architecture required to move safely from one controlled state to another while preserving continuity, compatibility, authority, evidence, recovery, and rollback.

Value stream
The end-to-end sequence through which an initiating need is transformed into an outcome for a stakeholder or affected party, including decisions, capabilities, processes, information, systems, evidence, support, and recovery.

EXPANDED TERMS — SECTIONS 023–024

Anti-corruption layer
A controlled translation boundary that prevents one domain context's model, language, assumptions, or authority from leaking directly into another context.

Architecture invariant
A condition that must remain true across architecture states, implementations, interfaces, and changes. An invariant is treated as a mandatory constraint rather than a preference that can be outweighed by aggregate scoring.

Bounded context
A declared boundary within which a domain model, language, rules, state, authority, and ownership have consistent meaning. Meaning crossing the boundary requires an explicit contract or translation.

Context map
A governed representation of bounded contexts and their relationships, including upstream and downstream responsibility, translation, shared models, published language, dependencies, authority, and lifecycle state.

Domain Architecture
The governed organization of domain boundaries, language, models, rules, state, decisions, data ownership, services, events, authority, integrations, and change across bounded contexts.

Domain service
A domain-owned function that performs behavior belonging to a bounded context when that behavior does not naturally belong to one entity or value object. Its authority and interfaces remain explicit.

Failure domain
A bounded set of system elements, state, dependencies, or operations that may fail together or share a common cause, together with the expected blast radius, containment, detection, and recovery behavior.

Published language
A versioned, owned contract through which one domain context exposes defined meaning to other contexts without exposing or imposing its entire internal model.

Semantic contract
A versioned agreement defining the meaning, source, authority, timing, validity, error, correction, and lifecycle semantics of information or behavior exchanged across a boundary.

State ownership
The explicit assignment of authoritative responsibility for creating, changing, validating, reconciling, evidencing, and retiring a defined state.

Systems Architecture
The governed structural architecture of a system of interest, defining its elements, responsibilities, interfaces, dependencies, state, data, authority, trust, evidence, failure, recovery, deployment, operation, maintenance, and retirement.

EXPANDED TERMS — SECTIONS 025–026

Business Architecture
The governed structure connecting mission and strategy to value propositions, outcomes, value streams, capabilities, organizations, roles, decisions, policies, information, products, services, resources, measures, investments, controls, transitions, and lifecycle.

Business capability
An enduring outcome-producing ability required by an enterprise or bounded organization, independent of the temporary organization, project, process, technology, provider, or product used to realize it.

Capability Architecture
The governed definition of required capabilities, including their outcomes, boundaries, ownership, realization, dependencies, levels, gaps, measures, evidence, resilience, increments, investments, transitions, and lifecycle state.

Capability catalog
A versioned register of capabilities and their definitions, owners, outcomes, scope, realization, dependencies, measures, evidence, risks, controls, and lifecycle states.

Capability increment
A bounded, dependency-aware change intended to establish or improve a defined capability outcome under explicit authority, acceptance criteria, evidence gates, transition, support, and rollback conditions.

Capability map
A governed structural view of capabilities and their relationships, decomposition, ownership, dependencies, and lifecycle state. A map does not by itself prove capability existence or effectiveness.

Capability realization
The combination of people, roles, competencies, processes, functions, information, data, applications, technologies, facilities, partners, suppliers, AI, controls, and governance through which a capability is delivered.

Capability state
The truthful status of a capability within a declared scope and environment, such as required, proposed, existing, available, authorized, demonstrated, operational, effective, degraded, unsupported, superseded, or retired.

Operating model
The governed arrangement through which value streams, capabilities, organizations, roles, decision rights, processes, information, technology, locations, partners, resources, and controls combine to deliver outcomes.

Value proposition
A source-grounded statement of the outcome or benefit intended for a defined stakeholder or affected party, together with obligations, assumptions, harms to avoid, acceptance boundaries, and evidence methods.
