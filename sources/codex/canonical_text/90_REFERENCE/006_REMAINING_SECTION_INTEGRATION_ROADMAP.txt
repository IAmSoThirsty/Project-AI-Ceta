STATUS BOUNDARY

CONTENT-COMPLETE means the authoring pass is complete for the declared section scope. It does not mean SOURCE-RECONCILED, HOSTILE-REVIEWED, OWNER-ACCEPTED, IMPLEMENTED, TESTED, VERIFIED, READY, RATIFIED, or PUBLISHED.

A checkpoint may promote a section only when the exact gate criteria and evidence are recorded. Owner acceptance may be assigned only by the accountable human owner over exact content and artifact identity.

ASSURANCE PRINCIPLES

- Preserve the human objective, terminology, values, authority, and final decision rights.
- Preserve source identity, provenance, version, scope, time, method, and evidence boundaries.
- Keep fact, source statement, inference, assumption, recommendation, decision, implementation, test, verification, and acceptance distinct.
- Unknown means unknown. Missing evidence must not be repaired with confident prose.
- Preserve dissent, contradictory evidence, exceptions, and unresolved gaps.
- Reopen earlier sections when later evidence reveals a material defect, contradiction, omission, or stale assumption.
- Use the smallest sufficient role set and keep execution, challenge, verification, and approval independent where consequence requires it.
- A denial or block must name the exact blocker, safe work that may continue, the smallest resolution path, authority required, and evidence needed for reconsideration.

CHECKPOINT WORKFLOW

For each checkpoint:

1. Establish exact baseline identity.
- Record archive or commit identity, internal manifest result, working directory, and date.
- Confirm section count, status count, role count, contract count, and safe archive paths.
- Preserve pre-change hashes for source-defined roles, imported contracts, and source artifacts.

2. Read the complete checkpoint range and its dependencies.
- Read upstream sections named by prerequisites and related-section references.
- Read downstream handoff targets where meaning, allocation, evidence, or authority crosses the checkpoint boundary.
- Read applicable shared governance, role contracts, source register, glossary, index, cross-reference register, manifest, continuity map, and prior checkpoint records.

3. Reconcile structure and meaning.
- Check terminology, definitions, duplicated ownership, missing ownership, conflicting requirements, lifecycle state, authority, evidence, failure, recovery, and handoff consistency.
- Check that requirements flow into architecture, implementation, testing, operations, maintenance, and retirement without disappearing.
- Check that human authority, AI boundaries, informed consent, capability-versus-permission, reversibility, dignity, non-deception, traceable delegation, and owner operability remain coherent.

4. Reconcile sources.
- Identify supplied sources controlling each material claim.
- Record source version, authority, scope, currency, conflicts, and unresolved gaps.
- Distinguish source-derived requirements from Codex synthesis, recommendation, example, and open question.
- Use current external sources only when authorized and record them in the source register.

5. Conduct hostile review.
- Challenge omissions, contradictions, false completeness, vague applicability, untestable criteria, hidden authority, unsupported universal claims, security and privacy gaps, inaccessible user paths, missing failure handling, weak recovery, stale evidence, and circular verification.
- Preserve each material finding with severity, confidence, evidence, affected sections, owner, disposition, and reopening condition.

6. Reopen and correct only what evidence requires.
- Record exact sections reopened and why.
- Preserve prior state and correction history.
- Return a section to CONTENT-COMPLETE after the material revision is fully authored.
- Promote to SOURCE-RECONCILED or HOSTILE-REVIEWED only when the complete gate is actually satisfied and evidenced.

REOPENING RULE

Reopening is normal assurance work. A section must be reopened when material evidence shows that its content is incomplete, contradictory, incorrectly scoped, unsupported, unusable, unsafe, inaccessible, or inconsistent with governing source or later lifecycle obligations.

A reopening record must identify:

- section and exact affected claim;
- trigger and evidence;
- consequence if unchanged;
- authority for the correction;
- related sections and artifacts;
- required revision and acceptance method;
- prior and new status;
- evidence required to close the reopening.
